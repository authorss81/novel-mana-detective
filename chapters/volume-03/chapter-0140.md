# Chapter 0140 — The Fifth Line

The laboratory was two rooms behind a hospital's linen office on the third floor of Saint Orra Reach, and there was a corridor between them with a fire door at one end and a window at the other that looked at a wall about four feet away, and there were four stools at the bench and nobody was sitting on three of them.

It was half past nine on Friday the twenty-sixth of April and the hard case was shut and the foil stencil was in its box and the bench drawer was shut and the consent strip in the brown envelope in that drawer had not been read since the sixth of January, and plate 1201 was in its slot four inches from the case, and the log book with the rubber band was where the log book is.

Mara Quill had a plate of toast on a mug that she had not eaten, and she had been in the room about forty minutes, and she had a sheet of paper in front of her with four lines on it and a fifth space under them and a line ruled at the bottom of the fifth space with nothing on the other side of it.

"Sit down. Do not touch anything, and do not tell me that you are not going to, because I have known that since February and I have stopped finding it interesting."

He sat down on the end stool.

"What have I done," he said.

"You have read a page, and you have carried a man's location to a woman on a stair in a care home who is not a route and who is a night sister, and I have found that out in about four seconds, because you have got that thing in your face that you had on Monday night and I have known that face for two years." She turned the sheet round. "I am not going to have it out with you. **I want to do a piece of work and I want you in the room for it and I do not want to be doing it with you, and those two things are going to be true at once for the next about four months and I have decided to stop arguing with it.**"

"What is the piece of work."

"The fifth line," said Mara Quill. "**And I want you to understand what that means, because you have had a page in your coat since February and you have never once asked me for anything, and this is me asking you for something, and I have never done it, and I have thought about it for three days.**"

---

"There are four," she said. "You know there are four. I am not going to read them to you and I am not going to let you read them, because a rule that a person can read out loud in a corridor is not a rule, it is a sentence.

"Four of them were written on a stair and on a bench and at the end of a long night in November and in January, and the third one broke on the day it was needed and we corrected it and did not publish it, and I have never told you why the third one broke and I am not going to now.

"**The fifth one is about a person.**"

"There is not much room."

"**There is about two inches, so write it small, and I am going to say what it has to do before you write it, and I have thought about this for three days and this is the whole of what I have arrived at, and you may tell me it is wrong.

"It has to do four things. It has to say that there is a person, and that he is in this city, and that he was moved by somebody who had no authority to move him, and it has to say what the two of us are doing about it, **and the fourth of those is the one nobody writes down, and the fourth is: nothing.**

"Everybody in this case has written down what they are doing. I have written down what I have measured. You have written down what you have been told. A woman of sixty-one in a laundry has written down a number of items and a destination. **Nobody in this city, in five months, has written down one line about what they are not doing, and that is the only thing about this case that has not been written down, and I have decided that I am going to write it down.**"

He wrote it small. It took him a while, and he did not draft it in his own book first, and she watched him not draft it.

> *There is a person still in this city who was moved out of a building on 28 January by a woman with a bed and a telephone, and nobody arranged it, and he has not been told that anybody is looking. We are not looking for him. If he comes to either of us, we are two people in a room and we are not a building and not an office and not a form. This line is not a rule and it is not a protocol and it is not a document, and it binds nobody but the two of us, and it is the only one of the five that I would write out again.*

She read it twice, which is more than she read the other four, and then she put her pen down and put her hand flat on the paper, and did not say anything for about a minute.

"Right," she said. "**That is the fifth one and it is not signed and it is not going to be signed, and I am going to put it in the back of my own log with the date on it, and the log is not a file and there is no case number on the log, and if a police officer ever asks me for that page then I will tell him it is a page of measurements and he will be correct to think I am lying.**"

"That is a risk you are taking."

"That is a risk I am taking," said Mara Quill, "and I want you to know that I have thought about it for three days, and that I have taken it because on Monday night you told me a thing in a corridor and it is the first time in four years that a person has handed me something they had no right to hand me, **and I am not going to be the one who then refuses to write one line down.**"

---

"The third rule," she said, and took a second sheet out of a drawer, and did not shut the drawer, which he noticed. "You have two rules on the back of a delivery note. I am not going to read them out and you are not going to make me. Here is the third and it is not finished and it will not be finished tonight, and it is the only thing I have put in a document in five months and I have put it in a document because it has to survive both of us.

"**A person is asked whether they agree with a document before they are asked to sign it.** A person asked may say no. A person who says no may say it twice. The no is minuted. The minute goes to the person and not to the building. A copy exists and the copy is the only copy.

"That is it. **It is nine lines and it is not a protocol and it is not a document that anybody has to sign, and the reason it is written in my own words and not on a form from Licensing is that a form from Licensing would have a box for the officer's name on it and a box for the witness's name on it and a box for the date, and it would not have a box for the second no, and the second no is the entire content of this arc.**"

"**The second no is the entire content of this arc,**" said Elias Venn, "and it is not on a form in four offices and it is the reason a woman in Calder Street agreed to a true document about herself in nine minutes with a cold cup of tea and nobody in the room did anything at all."

"Yes," said Mara Quill. "**And I am not going to put her in this. I am not going to put her name in a log, or in a book, or in a piece of paper, or in anything, and if you put her name in anything then this stops being a document and becomes a collection, and a collection is a thing with a person in it.**"

"I have not."

"You have her name in your own book, in your own hand, written on a Monday in a garage, and I know that, and I am not going to ask you to tear it out, and I am not going to pretend it is not there, and that is the third thing I have said to you in three days that I have thought about for longer than a day." She put the second sheet under the first one. "**So here is what we are, and it is a small list, and I have been keeping it in my head since Monday and I would like it said out loud once in a room with a light on, as a woman who has been told to do that by another woman, and who did it about as well as she could.**

"A woman with a bed moved a man out of a building in four minutes off her own book because there was nobody to ask, and nobody arranged him, and there is no form for what she did. A registered witness agrees in printed words, four lines, on a page that costs three credits, to make her account match a record that is already true; nobody frightens her and nobody breaks a rule and the whole of it takes about nine minutes. **And nobody hid anything from anybody, and that is not because the people involved are good, and it is not because they are bad, and it is the single most frightening thing I have learned in nine years.**

"That is the case. That is what is in it. **And we are two people who are going to write it down, and we are not going to be able to do it in a corridor at one in the morning.**"

---

The lift on the third floor of Saint Orra Reach was out for a service and there was a notice on it with a date on it, and the notice said it would be back on the twenty-eighth.

"Right," said Mara Quill, at about eleven, "then we walk, and I am not complaining, because the corridor is a corridor and I have not said one useful thing in it all week."

They did the last part of it at the bench first, because there was one thing left and she wanted it on the record of the room and not on a stair.

"Two things about where this goes," she said. "**It goes to the clinic. Not the police. Not because the police are bad — I have never worked with the police and I have no opinion that is worth having — and not because of anything that happened in a room on Monday. It goes to the clinic because a document that changes what happens to a person belongs with the people who look after people, and the day somebody in this city can point at a piece of paper and say *this is what is now required before a document is put in front of a person*, the person they will point at will be a doctor or a nurse or a woman behind a desk, and not a building.**"

"It is not signed."

"It is not signed and it is not finished and I have not written to the clinic about it, and neither have you, and there is a line at the top of the second draft that says where it is going and neither of us has looked at it since Monday." She squared the two sheets. "**And it is a draft, and it is ours, and I am not going to call it a protocol, because a protocol is a thing with a title and a version and somebody who owns it, and the first of those three things would be a lie and the third of them would be me.**"

"Neither of us is going to work alone on it any more."

"No," said Mara Quill. "**And there is one more thing about the four, and it is not a rule, and I have never said it out loud. The fourth one is yours. I am not going to read it and you are not going to read it, and in about a year you are going to come in here and try to change it, and I am going to let you, and that is the whole of the negotiation and I have known that since November.**"

"Since November."

"**Since the stairs, in November, and you did not notice, and I have watched you not notice for five months, and on Monday night of all times you came into this room and told me about a man's location and did not notice that I have been waiting five months to be asked one question about a document, and you asked me a different one.**" She put her pen down. "Go on, then. Say it."

"You have been waiting to be asked whether the fourth one is still yours."

"Yes," said Mara Quill. "**And now it is, and it has been, and it is a piece of paper with two lines on it in a room behind a linen office and nobody has ever seen it, and in about a year one of us is going to be angry about it and it will not matter, because the fifth one is now the one with a person in it and the fifth one is the one that will still be true.**"

---

"Take stock," she said. "Not of the case. Of the Friday. I have been doing a thing in my head all week and I want to do it out loud because I do not trust myself to do it in my head, and because there are four stools in this room and it is four, and I have known it was four every time since February and neither of us has ever mentioned it once."

"Nobody has mentioned it."

"**Nobody has mentioned it, and that is a rule of this room and neither of us made it.**" She turned round on the stool. "Right. Things that exist at half past ten on Friday the twenty-sixth of April. One: a manual in a carrier bag on a shelf behind a door in Kettle Rows with a page in it that four offices in this city have been following since 2022. Two: a sheet of A4 in my hand and a sheet of A4 in your hand, and neither of them has a name on it, and the two of them together are the beginning of the only document in this case that anybody has drafted rather than found. Three: a copy of a minute in a cardigan pocket in Calder Street, one sheet, four lines, a name and a time, belonging to a woman who is entitled to it. Four: a hardback book under a cloth four streets off a parade with a Tuesday the ninth of April in it, which is the first day in four years in that book that was not a Friday. Five: a piece of paper in a till in a laundry in this city with two hundred and an address on it and a signature, and above the till a hardback job book with a man's name at the top of the first clean page, and in May that shop is going to a unit in Ferrier Street, **and that is the only one of these six that anybody has decided anything about.**"

"And six?"

"**And six is the other thing in that same bag. A single sheet of A4 with a Registry header on it, bought for two credits and a signature on the fourth of March, and it is certified, and every line on it is true, and it has three acts in it and the three of them are a hall and a turn and a release, and a release does not go last, and there is not one word on that page about the man it names. I have never read it and I am not going to, and you have not looked at it since March and you are not going to either, and a page that is true in every line and means nothing is the hardest kind of paper in this city to put in front of anybody, so you have a lawful reason to hold it and no lawful way to publish it, and that is the whole of what we have got and it is the whole of what is in front of us, and it is Friday.**"

"Then say what we do on Monday."

"On Monday," said Mara Quill, "I write to a different counter in Morrow Hill and I buy a second copy of that manual and I read the other half of the printing, because the printing in your bag is two years old and has nine modules in it and the current one has eleven, and **the two that were added are all about fees, and the other printing of that page may say something different, and if it does then a woman in a basement who has never been asked what she leaves out has been cutting records to a specification that has changed under her hand and nobody told her, and that is a thing I can go and ask her about on a Tuesday.**"

"And I?"

"You go and find out whether a licensing committee in June has on its papers anything to do with a page of a manual," said Mara Quill, "**and I am telling you to do it as a thing I am asking you for, and not as a thing you are doing for me, because the day those two things are the same thing is the day this becomes something else, and I have watched you be careful for five months and I would like you to be useful instead.**"

"And when we have both of those."

"**Then we will have a count and a committee and a draft with no names on it and four hundred and nine closed matters in a drawer in a building with no paper behind them, and at that point, Elias, there will be about nine people in this city who have to be in one room, and none of them will be able to do it, because none of them is anybody's employee, and every one of them has a person behind them who cannot be told.**"

"That is not a plan."

"That is not a plan," said Mara Quill. "**That is a list of what will be in the way, and I have been keeping it since Monday, and I have put six things on it and I have put the sixth one last because I wanted to look at you while I said it.**"

"And the arrangement," said Elias Venn. "Say the sentence."

"I said it on Monday night. You did not take it."

"**I said it on Monday night in the corridor outside this room at about one in the morning and I meant it and I have not stopped meaning it, and I am not going to say it a second time in four days because a man asked me to.**" She picked up the two sheets. "**We are not lovers and we are not going to be able to pretend to be colleagues, and there is no word in this city for the thing we are, so we are going to have to keep saying the sentence out loud, and the sentence is that neither of us goes into a room the other one is in. And that is not romance and it is not friendship and it is the only arrangement I have ever been in with anybody that I would put in writing, and I am putting it in writing, and you are going to sign it, and it is Friday.**"

He signed it, and it took him about four seconds, and there was no ceremony and neither of them said a word about what it was.

Then they went out into the corridor and along it to the stair, and the stair at Saint Orra Reach is a wooden one out of a hospital's service end with a handrail on one side only, and at the top of it there was a fire door with a thumbbolt and a wedge of wood under it with a strip of tape on the wedge, and on the strip of tape there was a date and an initial in ballpoint in the year before last, and somebody in that building had written it there so that a person opening the door at two in the morning would know it had been wedged on purpose and would not report it.

Mara Quill put the two sheets in her coat and held the door with her shoulder, and he went up the wooden stair and past her, and neither of them said anything, and the door did not shut behind them.
