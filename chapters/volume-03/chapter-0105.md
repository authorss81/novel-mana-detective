# Chapter 0105 — A Schedule of Beds

He opened them at twenty to eight in the morning on Tuesday in the back room of 3 Tanners Row, with the electric ring off, because Otto Vahl was downstairs and the ring woke him, and he had been opening somebody's post in his own shop for five months and had not once thought of it as a thing he did.

The plastic wallet had been in the inside pocket of a good coat since the fourth of November 2029. The memorandum was one sheet, headed with a floor and a building — **60 Verge Row** — and four numbered lines, and at the foot of it a reference like *60/A/2/1188* and a woman's initials and a date, and the fourth line was a form of words and not a sentence anybody would say.

The reply was one sheet, on the same paper, and it was **unsigned**, and it began in the middle of a line in the way that replies do when somebody has been asked something and has decided to answer the part of it that does not commit them.

> *— is not the department that does that. The department that does that is Licensing, or it is not anybody, and I have asked on the floor and the answer I have been given is that it is not a department, it is a process, and I have worked out what that means and I do not like it and I am not going to write it down.*
>
> *You should not send things to this office. Anything sent to this office is read by the person whose tray it is in, and the person whose tray it is in is me on a Tuesday and a Thursday, and on a Tuesday I am the person who finds things out and I have been that twice and both of them cost me somebody.*
>
> *I am not signing this because I have said the thing I am going to say and I do not want to be a document.*

That was the whole of four months. He read the reply three times, folded both sheets back into the wallet, and put the wallet in the inside pocket of the coat again.

Sixty/A/2/1188. He wrote it out in his own notebook, because a number is not a document and he would be permitted to write one down, and then he looked at the twenty-five and twenty-six and understood what it was: **A, two, one thousand one hundred and eighty-eight.** The second floor was the rehabilitation enquiries counter at 6 Verge Row and the second floor at 60 was claims assessment, and a form was being filled out and given a number by the person on the floor, and **the number had no prefix to tell anybody that it was about a ward and no suffix to tell anybody that it was about a room, and a man in a good coat had been sent a piece of paper with it on it by somebody who wanted him to go and find it out and did not want to be in the room.**

He did not telephone anybody about it. He had been given a form of words and an instruction not to send things to that office, and the instruction had come four months late and unsigned, and he honoured it, and he wrote underneath the number: *she told me not to go, and she told me in a document, and I am not going.*

---

Gareth Prothero came to 2 Anselm Street at about eleven on the same morning because Tuesdays were contract days, and he was forty-four years old and had been a contract officer for eleven years and had three contracts, one of which was this one, and he had a coat over his arm and a folder under it and he was not in a hurry.

He sat in the office with the door open and the hall in front of him and eleven people in the hall, and he looked at the hall the way a man looks at a thing that is both his work and not his work.

"You will be the one with the list," said Perpetua Ayodele, and she went out of the office, and she did it the way a person does a thing that is not a discourtesy.

"I am the one with the list." He put a folder on the desk. "You do the rounds on the beds?"

"No, I do the list. The rounds are the night man's and he is asleep and he has been asleep since about seven."

"Then I am going to do the rounds on the beds myself, and it will take me an hour, and I am going to ask you the questions off the sheet and you are going to tell me if a bed is not what the sheet says it is, and I am not going to be offended, because a bed that is not what the sheet says it is is the whole job." He said it pleasantly and he meant it, and Elias Venn, standing in a doorway with nothing to do, watched a competent man do a competent thing and felt the pull of it.

He was not needed. He was not asked for anything. He was a man in a good coat in a doorway and after about eleven minutes Gareth Prothero said "sorry" and shut the door, and Elias Venn went and sat on the second chair in the hall and watched the light go across the floor.

---

The bed schedule came off the wall at about half past twelve, when Prothero came out with it rolled under his arm, and he rolled it the other way and unrolled it along the top of the counter, and it was four pages of A4 printed on both sides with a header and a rule and no signature, and it was the most legible document Elias Venn had seen in five months.

> ANSELM STREET SERVICE — BED SCHEDULE, CONTRACT 12/B
> **Period 1 April 2029 to 31 March 2030. Operator: Rowan Trust, reg. 4471-C.**
> One line per bed. Bed · band · rate per night · operator code · care need · review date.
> **Care need must be entered as one of: LOW / DEPENDENT / CONTINENCE / ACUTE.**

Forty-one lines. Forty-one beds, numbered one to forty-one, in bands of four apart from the last, with rates that went up by about a credit a night in the third band and by three in the fifth, and a care need in the fifth column for every one of them, and a review date of 31 March 2030 at the foot of every line.

Elias Venn read the fifth column down.

LOW. LOW. LOW. DEPENDENT. LOW. LOW. DEPENDENT. LOW. LOW. ACUTE. LOW. DEPENDENT. LOW. LOW. LOW. LOW. LOW. LOW. DEPENDENT. LOW. LOW. CONTINENCE. LOW. LOW. LOW. DEPENDENT. LOW. LOW. CONTINENCE. LOW. LOW. DEPENDENT. LOW. LOW. LOW. CONTINENCE. LOW. LOW. LOW. LOW. DEPENDENT.

Thirty LOW, seven DEPENDENT, three CONTINENCE, one ACUTE. Forty-one values in forty-one rows, and **not one of them was a person.**

"Two of those are wrong," said Gareth Prothero. "Bed twenty-two is CONTINENCE and has been DEPENDENT since October, and bed thirty is LOW because the man in it is forty-one and walks to the shop and buys his own cigarettes, and I have never once been able to make the form say that, and it is not a complaint, it is a form."

"It is four values."

"It is four values, and there are four, and that is the correct answer." He rolled it up. "If I could put a fifth one in I would put *walks to the shop* in, and in about four years somebody would have written a book about that value, and the answer to why there are only four is that somebody in an office in 2011 counted them and it did not need a fifth one that year, and nobody has looked at it since."

"Who is paid."

"The trust. The trust is paid by the council on a contract, and the trust pays the operator code, and the operator code is the trust." He said it without any weight, because there was nothing to be dramatic about. "**I am paid for forty-one beds.** I have been doing this for eleven years and I have three contracts and this is the one I know, and I have never once been paid for a person, and I have thought about that about once a year and I have a filing system and a budget cycle and I have never once put it in writing, and I would not put it in writing now, because the day I put it in writing somebody would have to answer it and I have a budget meeting in June."

Elias Venn looked at the rolled schedule under the man's arm.

"May I ask one question," he said. "It is a general one and it is not about a bed."

"Go on."

"Is the review open to anybody."

"Completely open," said Gareth Prothero. "It is a consultation. It says so on the notice. I have got a hardback book on that table and a box under it, and either of those, and about nine people came to the last one of these in October 2028 and every one of them worked for me." He looked at his watch. "It is on the ninth of April at two o'clock at the Corrie Street rooms, and I have had that notice on that wall since the fourteenth of January, and the book has been on that table since the fourteenth of January, and I have never had a single person in who did not work for me, and I want to say something about that and then I am going to go, because I know what it looks like from outside and I have looked at it myself." He put the folder under his arm. "It is not a punishment and it is not a hearing. It is a fortnight of a building being decided by somebody who does not sleep in it, and everybody who sleeps in it has been invited three times and has not once come, and I have run out of things to say about that which are true."

He left at ten past one. At the door he stopped.

"You are the man who has been in here four times in nine days," he said. "Are you all right? Because if you are not, there is a woman on a Tuesday afternoon who is very good and there is nothing at all wrong with you that a bed will not fix."

"I am all right."

"Then come on the ninth of April and put your name in the book," said Gareth Prothero, "and if you do not want to put your name in the book, put it in the box, and I have never once in eleven years had anybody use the box, and I have started to think the box was a mistake."

---

Saint Orra Reach had a public corridor on the second floor with a bench in it and a vending machine that had not worked since the spring, and Mara Quill came out of a door at about five past three with her coat already on and stood in the corridor and did not pretend she had not been waiting.

"You have a piece of paper in your hand and it is the same piece of paper you have had since November, which means you have opened it, which means you have read the reply, which means you know that a floor does not do a thing that somebody has told you a floor does not do." She fell into step. "And you have come to me and not telephoned, which is a decision I would like noted, because the telephone is faster and I have been doing this for eleven years and I know exactly what a person with a piece of paper in their hand is about to do with it."

"You do not know what I am about to do with it."

"I am going to be wrong," she said. "Say it."

He said it. They were at the end of the corridor by the vending machine and it was the only place in the building with no window and no staff, and he said it there, standing up, with his hand on the side of the machine:

"I have spent five months finding out that a person who cannot say their own account is right is not being difficult. They are being the only kind of witness there is. **And the city has a form for that, and the form is a piece of paper, and the piece of paper has a box on it, and the box does not have a name in it.**"

Mara Quill did not answer for long enough that he thought she had not heard.

"Say that again," she said, "and do not do it in a corridor, because if I am going to answer it I want to do it standing up and I want to be able to say no to you afterwards."

---

So they did it in the laboratory, and she put her bag down inside the door and did not take her coat off, and the plate was in its slot on the shelf four inches from the hard case and was not in the case, and the hard case was not opened, and he did not look at the shelf because he had promised himself in the bus in October.

"The box has a name in it," she said. "Not a printed name. A name somebody has to be able to write, by hand, at a counter, on a Tuesday, in a building that is not hers, for a service that ends in March." She took her hands out of her coat pockets. "You have described a box in your head for four months and you have never been to see it, and the box is a good one, and the notice above it is not a legal notice and it does not have anybody's name on it and it does not have a police number on it, and I have been in that hall twice in the last year because somebody who sleeps rough asked me for a plaster, and I gave her one."

"That is not what I meant."

"No," said Mara Quill. "It is not. And here is what you meant and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be unkind about it." She put one hand on the bench. "What you meant is that there is nowhere in this city for a person to say *I was there and it was not me* and be written down while saying it. And that is true and I have known it for six years for a reason that is in a drawer under a bench in this room and that I am not going to talk about tonight, and you are going to hear about it eventually, and it will not be because I have been asked nicely by a man in a good coat." She looked at him. "And here is the other half, and I want it in your notebook in my words: **a box with a name in it is not a system. It is a request.** A form is a thing that requires a person to do the correct thing at the correct moment on behalf of somebody else. A notice with a hole in it and the word NAME on it is a person asking another person to stand in a room on a day and be counted. Those are not the same object and the distance between them is the whole of what you have been doing since September."

"I have been doing one thing since September."

"You have been doing two," said Mara Quill. "You have been finding out who is wrong, and you have been finding out how a person is stopped from saying so out loud, and they are not the same and they are not the same project, and the first one is a police project and you are not a police officer, and the second one is a practice project and you have not asked anybody to be in it with you."

She picked her bag up off the floor.

"I am not going to be unkind about it either," she said. "I am going to be exact, and then I am going to go home, and there is a plate on a shelf and it is not coming out this month and I am not going to tell you why, and you are not going to ask me why, and if you ask me why I shall tell you to go away, and that will be the end of a friendship I have valued for six months and I have a right to do that."

She stopped in the doorway.

"One more thing, and it is not about the box, and then I am going." She did not turn round. "You have been in here four times since the third of November and I have opened the door every time, and the first time you were asking about a plate and the second time about a form and the third time you were not asking me anything at all, you stood in my laboratory and told me about a woman in a laboratory who is me, and this time you brought me a piece of paper and a rule. And in about four months you are going to bring me something that is not paper, because that is what happens, and on that day I am going to ask you one question and you are going to have to answer it in advance of the thing, not after."

"What question."

"I am not going to tell you," said Mara Quill. "If I told you, you would prepare an answer to it, and there is not one single thing in this city I want more than a prepared answer to one of my questions."

---

He got the bus back and let himself into 3 Tanners Row at about ten past six and Otto Vahl was on the landing with a cup of his own and a plate of crusts, and he looked at the good coat and said:

"Shop's shut. Bench is yours if you want it and I'm not asking."

"I am all right."

"You are not all right and that is not a question." Otto Vahl moved the cup a foot to the left so that there was room. "You have had a day and you have come out of it with the face of a man who has been told something true. I have watched that face twice since September. Once was the eleventh of January and you came down that stair at ten to six in the morning and I had a cup out, and I did not ask you then either, and I am not going to now."

"You have never asked me."

"No," said Otto Vahl. "I have not, and I have told you why, and you have never once tried to make me, and that is the arrangement and it goes on." He drank his tea. "There is a plate of crusts and there is a man of twenty-five in the shop downstairs who has not been up those stairs since Christmas and will not go up them while I am in my own house, and there is a woman on the first floor at number fourteen who has started leaving her milk in on a Sunday, and there is a whole street of people who have decided about this building in the last five months. You have not noticed any of it because you have been somewhere else. I am not asking you about the somewhere else. I am telling you that the somewhere else has a rent attached to it and I am the landlord and I have not raised it, and I am going to keep not raising it, and I want that to be a thing you have been told rather than a thing you have worked out."

He went to bed at seven, which is what he does.

Elias Venn sat on the bench in the back room with a carrier bag on his knees and a folder of a man's own papers in it, and the electric ring off, and the ten-day clock going, and read the schedule of forty-one beds three more times, and wrote nothing, and slept for four hours.
