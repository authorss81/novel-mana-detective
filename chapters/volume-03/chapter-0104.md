# Chapter 0104 — The Public Book

He got a bus to Morrow Hill on Monday morning with a notebook in his coat and thirty-one credits in his pocket, and he had not had thirty-one credits off that till since the twelfth of January and he was aware of the arithmetic in a way he had not been aware of anything else that week.

The ground floor of Concord Hall had a counter on the left, a counter on the right, a stone floor, and a radiator against the far wall that had been against the far wall long enough to have paint the colour of weak tea above it where the heat had gone up the wall for four decades.

Above the radiator, on a wire rack, in a room where about sixty people were doing things that were not related to each other, there was a row of publications at three credits each, and he had bought one of them in September in a different coat, and the man behind the table remembered the coat.

"You again."

"Two items." He put the money down. "The Witness Training Compendium, and I have got the Compendium already, so the other one is not a book, it is a question."

"Then the book is three credits and the question is free until it isn't." The man gave him a brown one with a picture of a door on it that had nothing to do with anything. "That is the current printing. The one you want is the second shelf down and it is the green one, and the green one is two years old, and the two-year-old one has nine modules and this one has eleven, and the three that were added are all about fees, and if you want to know what a man signed up for in 2028 then you want the green one, and the green one is two credits because it is out of date and nobody is going to print it again."

Elias Venn bought the brown one for three credits and read the contents on the inside cover standing up, in a public room, with about forty other people in it, because it was a public document and he had a right to read a public document and no reason to pretend otherwise.

**Module 1. What a sequence is, and what it is not.**
**Module 2. Anchor, address, turn, release.**
**Module 3. Noticing without helping.**
**Module 4. Why an account is not evidence.**
**Module 5. Speaking in a room.**
**Module 6. Being recorded.**
**Module 7. Withdrawal, and what happens to a file.**
**Module 8. Disagreement, and how to say it out loud.**
**Module 9. Continuity of account.**
**Module 10. Fees, and who pays them.**
**Module 11. Conduct in a licensed building.**

He read number 9 twice and did not read it three times, because he had a rule he had made for himself in November and had not broken yet, and the rule was that a man who has not asked you for anything is not a man you are entitled to go and read about.

Then he read number 7, which was two pages and which he read in its entirety because a man who has written a letter to a police force deserves to know what happens to him, and it said exactly what Petra Lund had said, and it gave a form number, and it gave the four pages, and it said a withdrawn account is superseded and remains in the file and is marked as superseded on both the statement and the record of the interview, and it said nothing whatever about a certificate, and there is a line in the middle of page two that says **a certificate is issued by the Proof Registry and is not affected by any action taken by an investigating force**, and it gave no reason, and it was the seventh line of page two of module 7 of a training manual that anybody may buy for three credits, and it is the only sentence in the whole of the printed apparatus of this city that puts the two buildings in the same sentence.

He wrote that in his own book, in the bus, and underlined it once, and then he crossed the underline out, because underlining a sentence in a man's own notebook is a thing that turns a note into a flag, and he had learned that in September.

---

The Witness Register was in the same room, on a table by the counter on the right, in a rack, hardback, about two hundred pages, with a card in front of it that said:

> **WITNESS REGISTER — LICENSING**
> **A public record. Entries are made under delegation. Persons may read this register. Enquiries about any entry are not accepted.**

That last sentence was the whole of the design of the object, and he stood and looked at it for a while.

The columns were: number, name, course reference, date of registration, course delivered by, funder, and status.

The last column was the one that mattered, and it was the narrowest, and it had been the narrowest in a way that suggested a printer had been told to make room for it in about 2019 and had not been asked to make room for anything wider since.

A woman of about forty behind the counter came out from under the counter when he had been standing there long enough.

"Are you asking for a person or about a person."

"About a person."

"That is the answer that gets you read and it is also the answer that gets you written down." She had a book of her own and it was not the register. "I have to write it either way, because the card says enquiries are not accepted and the only way I can tell the difference between a person reading and a person asking is to write down what they said. That is not me being careful. That is me being the only thing between a public register and a private one."

"Then write it down."

"So I am going to write: *Monday twenty-fifth February, public counter, member of the public, male, about thirty-nine, asks about a person, has not given a name, has given a registration reference.*" She wrote it as she said it, which he appreciated more than he expected to. "Do you want to read it back."

"Yes, please."

He read it back, and it was correct, and it was in a hand that leaned slightly right.

"I have put your description in and not your name because you have not given me one, and if you give me one now I will put that in as well, and it will be in a Concord book in a building I do not control, and I have been here eleven years and the last four of them have been a different department and I would like that on both sides."

"I am not going to give you my name."

"Then that is the end of it and I have not minded once in eleven years." She put her pen down on top of the book. "Read the register or do not. It is a rack. Anybody reads it. It has been a rack since 1998 and the only thing that has changed is that in about 2011 somebody decided it would be easier to look at if it were on a table, and that is a decision about furniture."

---

He read the R's for about forty minutes.

**Sallis, Ruben.** Number 4412. Course reference **WT/2/4** — a two-day course, and there was a footnote at the head of the column that said **2 = two-day residential**, and that the residential ran on a Tuesday and a Wednesday. Date of registration **14 March 2028**. Course delivered by a name he had not got, because the column was headed *course delivered by* and what was in it was a place: **Concourse B, Licensing, ground floor.** Funder: **Rowan Trust, Kettle Rows.** And then, in the narrow column, in the same hand as everything else in the row:

**ACTIVE.**

And at the far right of the row, in a second hand, in ballpoint, in what appeared to be a weekly or fortnightly housekeeping entry of some kind, because there were about nine hundred of them down the page in that hand alone:

**13/2/30 — reissued.**

The letter was dated the eleventh of February. It was logged on the twentieth. The register says ACTIVE and the last thing anybody wrote against his name was on the thirteenth.

He stood at the table and did the arithmetic, and the arithmetic was: a man wrote a letter on a Tuesday, it sat in a tray for nine days, and three days after it came off the post somebody in this room, or in the room behind this room, went to a table in a public building and wrote the word *reissued* in ballpoint against his name.

*Reissued* is a word about a document.

He did not ask about it. The card in front of the rack said enquiries are not accepted, and the woman behind the counter had told him that the register is maintained under a delegation that is not hers, and he believed her, and he was not going to spend the one good thing that had happened to him in nine days by putting a question to a public counter that had already been written down in a book.

But he read the rows above and below his, because a public register is a public register and nobody had said he could not, and about eleven of the twenty rows he looked at had a housekeeping entry against them in the same ballpoint, and the entries were not all the same word. Most were *reissued*. Two were *lapsed*. One was *transferred — see 4408* and 4408 was a number five rows up, and the man in 4408 had a status of ACTIVE and a housekeeping entry against him dated the sixth of March, which was Saturday.

Nobody's file gets a word written in it on a Saturday by a man in an office. Somebody's book does.

He wrote that down too, and then he read the card in front of the rack four more times, and the card said what it said.

He copied nothing. He went and stood by the window with a cup of coffee from a machine at the end of the room, and he wrote four lines in his own notebook, and they were:

> *25/2/30. He is on a list. 14/3/28, a two-day course, paid for by the trust that runs the building, held on this floor in a room called Concourse B, and the fund line has a name on it, so somebody in a charity in Kettle Rows decided in 2028 that a night porter was worth two days.*
> *Status: ACTIVE. Last entry 13/2/30, reissued, in a different hand, on a book of housekeeping entries. The same word against about eleven of the twenty rows I looked at. Two lapsed. One transferred, and the receiving number has an entry dated Saturday 6/3/30, which nobody does on a Saturday. That is a register, not a file.*
> *The letter is the eleventh. It was logged on the twentieth. Somebody wrote reissued on the thirteenth.*
> *I did not ask. The card says enquiries are not accepted and the woman was eleven years in her chair and told me the truth about the furniture before I thought of a question. When this is finished I am going to want to know what reissued means and I am going to have to be a person who is allowed to ask.*

He looked at the fourth line and then he wrote a fifth one underneath it, which he did not expect to write.

> *I put my own name in a book in this building on Monday morning to read the name of a man I have not met. I have been a member of the public since the third of November and it turns out that is not a thing that is free. It costs three credits and it costs your own name and both of them are cheap and both of them are on a page, and the woman who took mine wrote it down while I watched and showed it to me afterwards, and that was the correct thing to do and I want it written down that I noticed.*

---

The bus back to Kettle Rows was at two o'clock and was late, and he read in it.

He read module 7 in full on the bus with a rule case in his lap and a man asleep four rows away, and he wrote nothing down while he read it, and afterwards in the shelter he wrote: *M7/4. A withdrawn account is superseded and remains in the file and is marked as superseded on both the statement and the record of the interview.* Then: *M7/9. A certificate is issued by the Proof Registry and is not affected by any action taken by an investigating force.* Then, in capitals, because he wanted it to be findable: *THAT IS THE ONLY SENTENCE IN THE WHOLE OF THE PRINTED APPARATUS OF THIS CITY THAT PUTS THE TWO BUILDINGS IN THE SAME SENTENCE AND IT IS IN A MANUAL ANYBODY MAY BUY FOR THREE CREDITS.*

And then, on Monday night, in the back room of 3 Tanners Row with the electric ring off, he read module 8.

**Module 8. Disagreement, and how to say it out loud.**

It was nine pages, and it was the best thing in the book, and it was written by somebody who had taught people to disagree for a very long time and had given up on institutions and not on people.

Page one was the reason. It said that a witness who has been trained will, in the majority of cases, agree with a document placed in front of them within about ninety seconds, and that this is not deception and not weakness and not even surprise, and that it is the ordinary result of a person who has been taught that a document in a room is correct and has never been in a room where a document in a room was not correct.

> *A trained witness is not a person who has been taught to lie. A trained witness is a person who has been taught that there is somebody in the room who knows.*

Page three was the mechanism, and it was short.

> *Disagreement is stated in a room, with other people in it, in the first person, about a specific act, without reference to anybody's honesty or motives, and it is stated once.*
> *It is then not repeated.*

Page four was why.

> *Repetition is the mechanism by which a disagreement becomes a refusal, and a refusal is a thing a person can be asked to justify, and a justification is a second account of the same forty seconds, and the second account is where a witness loses.*

Page seven was a worked example, in which a witness of a working in a licensed building had said *that is not what I said* and had then signed, and the trainer's note on the case was four lines long and the four lines were an account of what the witness had said and not an account of what the witness had meant.

Elias Venn read page seven twice.

Then he read page nine, which was entitled *What to do if the document is correct and the witness is wrong*, and it was half a page, and it said:

> *Nothing. There is no procedure. A document being correct and a witness being wrong is not a situation that this training addresses, and a trainer who has a procedure for it has understood something about the work that the work does not contain.*
> *What is required, in that situation, is a different kind of document entirely, and it does not exist at the date of this printing.*

*At the date of this printing.* He turned the booklet over. The printing was dated March of this year.

He put the booklet down on his knee and sat with it for about ten minutes in a back room in Kettle Rows, and then he wrote in his own notebook, and what he wrote was not a note about a module.

> *Since November, and again on the eleventh. She read my second of two readings back to me and said it was the same. It was not the same and I did not say it was the same, and I let it go, because she was measuring and I was seeing, and I have wanted for five months to be the man who is different.*
> *Page 4. It is not repetition, it is a second account of the same forty seconds. That is what I have been doing since November and I have been calling it diligence and there is a word for it in a manual I bought on Monday and I have had it in a bag in a good coat for four months.*
> *I am not going to read module 9. Not this month. A man who has not asked me for anything is not a man I am entitled to go and read about, and I wrote that down on Monday in a bus and I have not broken it and I am going to be able to say on the day that I did not break it, and if I break it I am going to have to write down the day I broke it and that is the only discipline I have got left.*

He got the bus back at ten past one and sat in it for forty minutes with a brown booklet about a door on his knees and read modules 1 to 4, which he already knew, and read the words *why an account is not evidence* at the top of page nine of module 4, and did not read further, and then he looked out of the window for four minutes and then he read the rest of it anyway, because he has never once in his life been able to leave a page alone, and the sentence was: **an account is a record of a person, and a record of a person is a thing that can be wrong in the ordinary way, and a sequence is a record of an order, and the two are kept in different buildings and are not the same instrument and must never be entered on the same line of anything.**

*Which is the second time in two days that I have been told about two buildings,* he thought, *by a person who did not have to.*

On Anselm Street a man of thirty-six was sleeping in the third chair with a newspaper folded under his arm, and the lamp over the door was lit at ten to two in the afternoon, and on a board under a fire notice there was a piece of A4 with his name and a telephone number on it and nothing else, and it was not there any more.
