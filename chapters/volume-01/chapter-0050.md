# Chapter 0050 — The Missing Fourth Act

The woman who came in at half past nine on Saturday morning had a folder under her arm and a sheet of paper face down on the table in front of the chair he was not going to be sitting in for another four minutes, and she shut the door and stood with her back to it.

"Mr Venn. I am the Deputy Chief's secretary and I am going to read you the difference between two things before I read you anything else, because people get it wrong and it does forty years of damage.

"In October you received a departmental censure. A censure is a file matter. It goes on the file, it closes the file, it is signed for, it is read by the officers who hold the file. The word is *housekeeping*, and it is in the manual, and I am not paraphrasing.

"This is a formal reprimand. It is a finding. It has a notice served on you and a hearing behind it and a right of reply, and when it is signed it does not live on a file in this building. It goes on a record, and the record goes to any office that asks for it for as long as the department exists, and I am told there are four, and I have not counted them."

Elias Venn sat down in the other chair.

"What is the finding," he said.

"Four minutes," said the secretary, "and then she reads it, and I am not permitted to summarise it, and I have not."

---

Deputy Chief Aveline Orr came in at five to ten, put a folder on the table with the seal on it, and sat down without opening it.

"You were right. I have read Schedule 1 and I have had a woman from my own standards office agree with you and tell me she could not certify it either. That is not the question before me, it has not been since Friday, and if you spend your right of reply on it I will be recording that you spent your right of reply on it. The finding, and then your eleven minutes."

The finding was one side and it was in her own hand.

> *Formal reprimand. E. Venn, formerly D/Sgt, Sequence Desk.*
> *Fourth examination of a physical object in this matter, on 30 October 2029, between 14:38 and 14:47, by contact, upon the shaft of a walking stick belonging to a named member of the public, in a treatment room, in the presence of the owner and a medical practitioner, during a working in progress.*
> *The prohibition filed 29 September 2029 required a witness. The plan of 29 October 2029 was amended at 11:20 by D/Sgt Rennick to record that the member of the public was not a witness to it. He was not a witness. He was the actor.*
> *No consent naming a sequence was held. No breach note was written before the act. The instrument log entry of the medical practitioner for 28 October 2029 was not made in respect of this object, and her figure for 28 October 2029 was not made on this object.*
> *Consequence: unrestricted access to the first-scene samples at Morrow Hill, samples 441-KR/01 to 04, first scene, 14 Kettle Rows, withdrawn. Access thereafter only on the written request of the officer holding the file. The member of the public is not that officer.*
> *This finding is not a censure. It is a formal reprimand and it is referred.*

Nadia Kest was against the back wall with the plan in a folder on her knees, because she had to be, and she did not open it.

Elias Venn read the finding twice, the way he had read the practice note on the eighth of October, until the words stopped being words and became a fact.

"The second paragraph is wrong," he said. "It says her log entry for the twenty-eighth was not made in respect of this object. She logged 1198 at 11:14 in a kitchen in Tanners Row, owner present, Jules Ardent second witness, and the object in that entry is the sleeve, and the sleeve is what I put my hand on on Tuesday, because it was in the ferrule and the ferrule was on the stick. So the entry was made in respect of this object, two days before, and she was not told on Tuesday what I was going to do with it and I did not ask. If you want the closest thing to blame in that paragraph it is not that her entry was not made. It is that nobody wrote down in advance that a working was going to happen in a room with a man on a couch and the object four inches away."

"That is a different point from the one in the paragraph."

"It is the whole of the paragraph," said Elias Venn, "and I am not going to argue about the finding. Take my eleven minutes."

"I was the fourth person in this matter to put a hand on something and lose the use of something afterwards. The three before me had a consent in advance with my name on the breach beside hers, and a witness, and a figure that came first. This one had nine minutes and a sheet of five lines with four of them done.

"Line five is *who consents, in writing, and what they were told*, and I had that; he consented in writing and said it back in his own words. Line four is *what has been measured before any hand goes on it, and by whom*, and it was hers, and it was done on the Sunday, and it is the only reason I knew what the object was.

"Line three is *who is doing it, and who is watching*, and that is the one we did not do, because at ten past two on the Tuesday I did not know which of forty-one people in that room was going to be on the couch, and by the time I knew it was four minutes past and I was on the floor. Written up, it says a man examined an object in a room with five people in it and the officer's own plan said he was not a witness. The truth is that there were four witnesses in that room and none of us had a paper saying what we were.

"I would do it again. I am not going to pretend to you that I would not, and I am not going to use it as a defence, because it is not one, and I have had Wednesday and Thursday to think about it and I have come to the conclusion that I am allowed to say it and am not allowed to hide behind it. I am asking for the finding and for nothing else, and I am not going to waste eleven minutes of somebody's morning on a speech about a registry."

Deputy Chief Aveline Orr wrote for about forty seconds.

"Noted. It does not alter the finding, and the phrase I am obliged to use is that the finding is made in the circumstances." She closed the folder. "The reprimand is signed and referred this afternoon and it is not held. You have a right to a written statement of reasons, which you have just made, and a right of appeal to a panel in about five weeks, and I am obliged to tell you that nobody has ever won one and that the panel has never once had a person turn up." She looked at the woman against the wall. "And put Inspector Kest's plan in your notebook in my words, because in about four years somebody is going to read it and find that four people wrote their own names down in advance of the only thing in this case anybody could have done on purpose: *the finding stands, and the thing that made the finding possible is also on the page.*"

---

Jules Ardent came into the shop at ten past one on Saturday afternoon, put a cold chisel and a steel drift on the front bench side by side, and stood back.

"They went through the plan at eleven o'clock and my name is on it four times and one of them says *mechanical means, estimated nine seconds*, which is the only line in it I would have written about myself." He looked at the shelf above the back bench, where the certificate now sat in a row with a censure and a Factors letter. "That is the thing that happened on Tuesday, is it."

"By hand, to the shop, because the address is on a connected certified record, and the record is four sheets of paper on a clinic wall with my name at the bottom of the first one."

"You told me on Tuesday night what a censure is, and not what a reprimand is, and I have thought about that since." Elias Venn did not turn round. "It costs me the samples. The first-scene ones, from the seventeenth of September. I cannot go into the sample room at Morrow Hill without the written request of the officer holding that file, and there is one of those and it is not me. That is the only thing in this case I have ever been refused that I wanted, and I asked for it."

Jules Ardent put his hand flat on the bench over the chisel and the drift.

"You were on the floor," he said.

"About four minutes of it, and I did not notice, and I said *don't let go of that* twice with my eyes shut, and you did not let go, and I have not told anybody that you did not let go, and I would like it written down that I have not, because you will be asked about that floor at some point and I want one thing in this city you do not have to explain." He picked the chisel and the drift up. "Four of us, one could not see, one was not allowed to be a witness, one was the owner. I spent all morning working out what it would have been with me in a room on my own, and it would have been the same nine seconds and nobody would have been in trouble."

---

Nadia Kest came at twenty to two in her coat and put four documents on the counter in the order they were made: the plan, in 441-KR; the dispute, Schedule 3; the supplement, half a sheet, one line, the word *dispute*; and the certificate, on a shelf above the back bench in a glass front.

"Not one of those four can get to any of the other three," she said. "That is not a failure, Elias. That is the arrangement, and it is the first one in nine weeks built on purpose by three people who will not be thanked for it. And if you had been an officer on the fifth of October there is no version of Tuesday in which that certificate is issued, and none in which you do not have a witness, and none in which I have to write a paragraph explaining why I could not put a man's account in a schedule. You did not take a case away from this city. You were not holding one. The reason that sounds like an excuse is that the department took it off you in four minutes in October and never gave it back, and I have been on the other side of that for five weeks. If anybody hears that I said it I will deny it."

---

Mara Quill telephoned at three. "Come at four. The room, not the counter. I am writing something down before I do it, because you taught me that in October."

He was there at ten past four, with the hard case on the bench and the padlock on and shut, and another plate on a folded sheet.

"Read the plate," she said. "The log you can have afterwards. That is the part I cannot do twice."

Elias Venn looked at four inches of channel section under a hood and said the thing the plate was, in the flat voice of a man told to say a number out loud so that somebody else does the arithmetic, and he stopped in the middle of it.

"There is no place on it where one of them stops and the other one has not started."

"No," said Mara Quill. "My instrument will hold one order and this surface has two, and there is one person in this city who will not be able to stop looking at a place where two of them cross, and I am not able to put it in a drawer, and that is why I am going to do something today that I have not done in eleven years."

She turned the log round.

> *Plate 1201, lent to E. Venn, member of the public, 3 November 2029, from this building and not from the hard case. Reason: no instrument available to me holds two orders at once, and there is one person who will not be able to stop looking at one surface, and I am not able to put it in a drawer.*
> *Conditions. It does not enter the hard case. It is not measured. It is not photographed. It is signed back into this log by a person, and not posted, and not carried by an office, and not left in a corridor. Due back Thursday 14 November.*
> *M. Quill. Log 1202.*

"Read it again and tell me if there is a word you would take out."

"None. There is a word I would add, and it is *Thursday*," said Elias Venn. "It is not due back until Thursday and the reason is not that I want four days with it. The reason is that on Thursday I am going to have to ask somebody in an office for a sheet of A4 with five lines on it, and I want to be able to say that I did it having had this in my hand, because that is the argument I am going to have to make and it is a bad argument and it is the only one I have got."

Mara Quill wrote *Thursday* on the sheet and initialled it, put the plate into a bag, and gave it to him with both hands, which she did not do on Tuesday and did not comment on. Then she gave him a fourth copy of the five lines, folded twice.

"That is not a protocol. A protocol has a counterparty and an office and a number and a committee, and this is a sheet of paper in my building with our names on it, lodged nowhere, and that is why it works and also why it is worthless in a room full of lawyers. It broke on Tuesday because line three asked for a thing nobody could promise. Line three should say *who is doing it, who is watching, and if it cannot be known in advance then who finds out, and in what order.* Write it in your copy and do not tell anybody I said so, because in about four years somebody will take five lines off a laboratory wall and say it was the practice that let a policeman put his hand on a stranger's walking stick, and it will not be if I write it properly."

---

He went up the stairs to his own flat at half past three in the dark, and the box was on the shelf where it had been since the seventeenth of September 2017, and it was a red box about the size of a shoebox with a lid and no lock, and there was red thread knotted round it four or five times, tied in a police corridor in 2017 when he was thirty-one and the complaint was twelve years old and nobody had read it since.

He untied the knot. It took a while, because it was a good knot and he had tied it well. He put the thread over his shoulder where it hung in a loop against his hip, and he put the box back on the shelf in the same place at the same angle, and he did not lift the lid, and he said nothing out loud in the room, because he had said it to a magistrate once and it had not helped.

Then he shut the shop, put the door up, and walked up Marshall Street in the rain with a plate in a bag in one hand and forty feet of red thread over his shoulder.

---

The clinic was shut. The door was locked, the closure notice was up, and under it, since Friday, a second printed notice about a contained accident involving an unregistered ward tool; and behind the polythene on the board the ninety-odd copied pages, the four sheets and the transfer order in its sleeve were holding, which is what they had done since the eighth of October.

Mara Quill was at the kerb in a coat with her hood down in the rain, because she had come out of the laboratory to sign a plate back in and had not gone home.

Elias Venn stopped about a yard away.

"Two things, and the first is a credit to somebody else. Rennick got the drawing office register read in place on the nineteenth of October by asking an officer to come and stand there, and the officer was him, because a third party's record opens on a case number and I had not got one. Harrow got the tenant's word on the Sunday by walking to Halloway Street on his own time, because it is his door and I had not got a reason worth a landlord losing an evening over. Neither did it for me, both were paid nothing, neither was thanked, and both are why there is anything left to argue with at all."

"The second is the one I have to ask, and I have not written it down anywhere." Rain came down the gutter. "Somebody has spent nine months turning a fitter with a van and a card with four lines on it into a man who can put a working through a living patient on a Tuesday afternoon in a room full of people, and every part of that is a document, and not one piece of it names a teacher. I have got a trading name on a corkboard, a class on an invoice, a docket in a schedule with a premises and a depot, a curriculum with a page about not supplying an order, and eleven letters scratched inside a tin lid. Who is teaching him."

Mara Quill did not answer, and after a moment said, "No. And I have looked for the other one, which is worse, because I have had four days and a plate and a laboratory."

"Second question, and then I will stop. Aegis Mutual's name is in one line in one bench hand in a book that burnt on Thursday morning, and the only reason that line exists anywhere in the world is a photograph taken on a bench by a sergeant who was careful. It is a customer line, a payer line, a risk office that prices off reported faults and a member of a coalition you could stand in front of in a hotel lobby. And in the middle of September there is a rehabilitation account with a card and a premises on Verge Row that books patients at its own address and merges files to reach anybody else, and somewhere in that machinery there is a certificate for a treatment, and no patient in this city remembers receiving the treatment the certificate is for." He had it out, and he had not said it aloud in any room until now, in ninety days, in a notebook he had ruled a line under on the inside cover and left sitting there. "So why does Aegis Mutual hold a certificate for a treatment no patient remembers receiving."

Mara Quill looked at the bag in his hand for a while.

"I have not got an answer to either of those and I am not going to give you a half one at five o'clock on a Saturday to be kind," she said. "I have a plate with two orders on it and five lines and a log number, and that is more than I had in October and it is not enough for either question."

Then she held out her hand, and he gave her the bag, and she signed for it on the laboratory sheet in the rain with a pen that did not work first time, and wrote 16:38 beside her own name, and then set the bag down on the low wall between them with the top of it open, because a plate that is going to be looked at ought not to be looked at through a bag.

"Thursday. You will bring it back, and you will not put it in the shop or in a drawer in the shop, and if you cannot find me you will telephone the laboratory and leave a message, and you will not leave it on a bench."

"I will bring it back on Thursday."

Elias Venn stood on the wet pavement outside a closed clinic in Kettle Rows with five lines in his coat and forty feet of red thread over his shoulder and nothing else in his hands at all, and about a yard away from him on a low wall there was a bag with the top of it open and a plate in it.

And a walking stick with a real threshold ward in it — a man's own working, done in his own kitchen on a Friday afternoon by a man in a very good coat — was in a depot north of the ring road, or on a shelf, or in a drawer, or in a van, and it was the only thing in this case anybody could still use, and nobody in the city knew where it was, including the man who had read its order out loud in a treatment room on Tuesday afternoon.

He unwound the red thread from over his shoulder with both hands. It came off his hip in a long loop and he gathered it in his fist, and he stood there on the pavement and wound it round the bag, and round the clipboard, and round both of them together, and the last of it went over the top of the plate and lay there wet in the rain. It was the thread off a red box he had not opened since the seventeenth of September 2017, and there was enough of it and there was more than he needed.

Mara Quill picked the bag up off the wall and put it inside her coat against her ribs and held it there with one forearm, the way a person carries a thing they have agreed to take back.

He did not tie the knot.

In the glass, under a hood, in a laboratory behind a linen office four miles away, two residue orders lay on four inches of channel section in the wood of a man's own walking stick: one honestly made in his own kitchen on a Friday afternoon by a man in a very good coat, and one carried into this city on a solid object and seated in a channel a fitter fitted to a length of ash on the twenty-fourth of October. Where they crossed there was no place on the surface at which one of them stopped and the other one had not started, and the release that was never performed waited in the middle of the crossing, between the two accounts, and it went on waiting.
