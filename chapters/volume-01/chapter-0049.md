# Chapter 0049 — Post-Issue

Desmond Coyle was taken at twenty-five to ten on Friday morning on the forecourt of a lock-up on Draggery Street, and he did not run and he did not argue, and about four minutes after they had him in the back of a car he asked whether somebody could ring his daughter, and a police constable called Debra Ncube rang her from the front seat with the door open, and stood there for the length of the conversation, and came back and said that the daughter was fine and that she had asked whether her father was all right and that he was not going to be told anything today, and Coyle said *right* and shut his eyes.

He was forty-four years old and he was a fitter of ward tools with a van and a bench in it and no premises registered anywhere, and he had eleven calls in a book with the prices in them, and he was charged with the making of an unregistered ward tool and with being in a room where a working was in progress, and the second one was the one that would cost him, because it is the one with a list.

He said one thing in the car, and only one, and he said it to the constable and not to the officer.

"I did the bracket. I did the bracket properly and I did it free because he is seventy-eight and there is nobody in that house." He looked at his own hands. "The other bit was not mine."

"Was it somebody in a van?"

"The other bit was in the van when I opened it," said Desmond Coyle, "and I did not look at the other bit, and I will tell you why I did not look at it, and you will not believe it, and I have thought about it in a cell all night and I am still not going to be able to say it any better than this: because it was on a card, and it was on the card in the van, and I have been doing this for nine years and everything I have ever been told to do has been on a card, and I have never once been told a name."

---

Yvonne Pratt was taken at ten past two on the Friday afternoon at the staffing agency in Draggery Street, at her own desk, in front of two colleagues, and she did not cry, and she asked for one thing, which was that somebody should ring her mother, and that was done, and afterwards she asked the constable whether the clinic would be all right, and the constable said that was not a question anybody could answer, and she said *I know that, I have worked in six of them.*

Her records went with her in a cardboard box, and in the box there were four sessions' booking sheets for the Kettle Rows autumn clinic, and on the sheet for the thirtieth of October, in the fourth line of the venue list, in the agency's own typeface, the tool check at a quarter to three had a service call reference against it, and the reference was on a slip that was in a different hand from the rest of the sheet, and nobody in the room said out loud whose hand it was, and the slip went into an evidence bag with the sheets.

---

The certificate came to 14 Kettle Rows by hand at twenty to nine on Friday morning, in an A4 envelope with a window, addressed to **Venn Repairs, 14 Kettle Rows, Kettle Rows, Marshall Street**, and it had been served on that address because the address is on a connected certified record, which was four sheets of paper on a clinic board with a doctor's ink under them and a member of the public's name at the foot of the first sheet, and which had been on that board since the eighth of October.

Otto Vahl took the envelope off the mat and put it on the front bench and did not open it and went back to the wireless.

Elias Venn opened it standing up, and it is one side, and he read it standing up, and then he read it a second time and then he sat down on the stool and read it a third time with a pencil in his hand.

> **PROOF REGISTRY — CERTIFICATE C-441-KR-06**
> **Dangerous working. Sequence certified.** File 441-KR/6.
> Premises: Kettle Rows Community Clinic, Marshall Street, treatment room two. Date of working: Tuesday 30 October 2029, 14:38.
>
> **Sequence certified: anchor, address, turn, release. Complete. Self-directed. Performing person: the subject.**
>
> Subject: NEVE, Alfred J., 78, of 3 Tanners Row, Kettle Rows.
> Object: one unregistered ward tool, one length of channel section, recovered 14:47, transferred not seized.
>
> Schedule 1 — independent measurement, uncertified; laboratory of M. Quill; plates not on any register. **Not relied on.**
> Schedule 2 — patient statements. **Not part of this certification and is not published with it.**
> Schedule 3 — dispute. One entry, lodged 2 November 2029 at 11:40 by D/I N. Kest, countersigned D/Sgt P. Rennick. **Not affecting the certified sequence.**
> Schedule 4 — not compiled.

> Witness support: account on file 11 September 2029, Witness Services (Sequence Support). Withdrawn 5 October 2029, Form W-1 (rev. 3), field six. *A withdrawn account is superseded and remains on file. Withdrawal does not by itself impugn either account.*
>
> Issued 1 November 2029, 16:10. File opened 29 October 2029, 09:20, on a request received 27 October.
> Registrar-Counsel: **B. Haile.**

The only thing on the certificate that anybody in this city had fought for, and the only thing he had ever wanted off a piece of paper like this, was on it. A number in a drawer in Concord Hall that had been a woman's threshold measurement in pencil on the twenty-seventh of September had a sentence under it saying *not relied on*, and the woman had not even been asked, because there is no step in the process at which a person is asked.

He put it on the shelf above the bench, in the row, between the censure and the Factors letter, and then took it off again and put it on the counter, because a certificate is a document and documents do not live in a row with a man's private papers, and then he put it back on the shelf, because on Friday morning he was not in a condition to decide where a certificate goes.

---

He found Bettina Haile at a quarter past eleven in a corridor at Morrow Hill, and she was on her way to a hearing and had eleven minutes, and she gave him eleven, and she gave them in a corridor and not in a room and said why first.

"I have signed it and I have read Schedule 1 and I am not going to be asked about Schedule 1 in a room by anybody, so we will do this standing up."

"You did not have to read Schedule 1."

"I read it because it is on the file and it is the only measurement in the matter and it is by an uncertified forensic mage whose plates are in her own name and not on any register in this city, and I have wanted for four years for there to be a rule that says what that means and there is not one." She looked at her watch. "Three minutes. What do you want."

"I want you to put Alfred Neve's statement in the schedule as sequence material."

"No."

"He is the subject. He is the performing person. There is a certificate that says a man of seventy-eight performed a complete working at twenty to three yesterday afternoon and he was on a couch with a nurse at the time and he says in writing that he performed a threshold bracket in his own kitchen on the twenty-fourth and has never done anything else in his life, and you have his statement, and you have put it in a book that is not published."

"His statement is in Schedule 2 and Schedule 2 is patient statements, and Schedule 2 is not part of a sequence certification, and that is the oldest rule in this building and it is older than both of us." Bettina Haile said it fast and it was not unkind. "I can put a trained witness's account into sequence material. There is a witness support line on that certificate and it is not empty and the account on file is a withdrawn account and the withdrawal is regular and Form W-1 says exactly what a withdrawal does, and I applied the form as it is written, in the ordinary course, and if I had done anything else I would have had to write a memorandum and explain it to a committee. If I were to certify a patient's account of his own working as sequence material, then every man in this city who ever performed a dangerous working could certify himself out of it by saying he did not do it. That is not a rule I invented to be unkind to you. That is the reason the class of evidence has never been admitted here or anywhere else, and I did not know that until four years ago and I did not particularly want to know."

"Four minutes," she said. "And now the thing I am going to say in a corridor, and I am going to say it once, and then you are going to go away and I am going to a hearing."

He waited.

"The first three acts of that working are his," said Bettina Haile. "A bracket a fitter put in a socket in his own walking stick on the twenty-fourth, a threshold that holds his own step, and a release he made in his own kitchen with a man standing in his lane. M. Quill's plate says so, and it is the best work in this city, and it is not on a register. If all of that is true then the sequence is real. It is real and it is his, and there is not one false word in the first three acts of that certificate. The only thing in it that is not his is the fourth, and Mr Rennick's photograph of a bench channel in October shows a pen line in the metal, which is a sentence, and a sentence is what this office exists to record." She put her folder under her arm. "I signed it at ten past four yesterday afternoon and the file it comes out of was opened on Monday morning at nine twenty, and I am going to tell you what that is, and then I am going to go: a file is opened when a report is made, and a report was made on the Tuesday, and the sequence is certified from the residue and from the witness support and not from the date on the file, and if you want the date on the file to be different you take it up with the index office and not with me. There is nothing improper about the date. It is only early."

Nadia Kest lodged the dispute at twenty to twelve on the Friday, in her own hand, on the Registry's own form, in the room where such things are lodged, and she read it to herself out loud before she signed it, which she does, and then she made Rennick countersign it, which he did without reading it, and then she made him read it.

"I have been working on this since Wednesday afternoon," she said, "and I want you to know what I have been doing. I have been trying to make a dispute that can be entered. Not a statement, not an allegation, not something for the paper. A dispute."

"What is in it?"

"Four hundred and ten words and it is a dispute, and it says what is disputed, by whom, on what material, and what the material is and where it is and who made it." She turned the sheet round. "Item one: a dispute as to whether the release certified in this certificate was performed or was made elsewhere and delivered, on the material of plate log 1201 in the name of M. Quill, lifted 30 October at 19:30, not on a register, and of the officer's file note of 30 October. Item two: a dispute as to the seating of that release upon a working of the patient's own, on the same material. Item three: a dispute as to the reliance permitted on uncertified independent measurement, and I do not say it is refused, I say it is disputed. Item four: a request that Schedule 2 be compiled, with the patient's statement, and my name against the request."

"Item four is not going to happen."

"No," said Nadia Kest. "Item four is going to be in the file with my name on it for as long as this Registry exists, and in about four years somebody is going to be a Registrar-Counsel and will read item four and will not be frightened by it, because it will be a request and not a claim, and a request can be granted by a person who is not frightened. I have watched a great many things go into files as claims and come out of them as defeats. I put requests in."

"And the cell."

Nadia Kest closed her own hand over the form.

"They published it at ten past four yesterday with the certificate, and the supplement goes in this evening at five o'clock, and the supplement has one line under the heading, and the line is the word *dispute*, and the word has a cross-reference to Schedule 3, and Schedule 3 has a schedule number on it." She tapped the counter once with a knuckle. "That is the whole of what a dispute looks like from outside a building. Not an article, not a headline, not a woman on a doorstep. A word and a number. And in about a year somebody who has never heard of any of this will write to the ward authority asking what it means, and somebody will tell them it means a police officer disagreed, and that will be the first sentence anybody in this city has said in public that any of it was false."

"That is all you get?"

"That is everything I can get, and it took me four days, and I would have taken four months for that." She looked at him, and it was not unkind. "You are going to ask me why I did not put it in the paper on Wednesday, when there was a story on Wednesday, and I am going to answer you now so that you do not have to ask. Because if it goes in a paper on the Wednesday it is answered on the Thursday by a denial from this office, and a denial from this office is a document, and then on Friday I have a denial and a rumour and nothing else, and a rumour with a file number is worth more than a rumour, and I can hold a rumour with a file number for about four years and I cannot hold one without a number for four days."

"You are right," said Elias Venn.

"I know I am right. I would rather be argued with than admired, and you have not argued with me once since the car park, and I have noticed that too." She picked the form up. "And I have not put your name on this, and I want to be exact about why. You are not an officer. If I enter a statement from the officer whose perception is disputed, then this ceases to be a dispute about a measurement and becomes a dispute about a man, and then the office has to decide whether he is a reliable witness, and it will decide that in about eleven minutes, and it will decide it against him, and it will do it correctly. So it is in my name and Rennick's, and it says what the material is and who made it and where it is, and it does not say that a member of the public agrees with it, and that omission is the only reason the entry survives the morning."

He made his own document at three o'clock on the Friday afternoon at the back bench, with the carbon book shut and the door up and the rain coming off the yard door, and it is a page and a half in the small bad hand, and it is the only account of the working of 30 October that exists anywhere.

> *Physical discrepancy — Kettle Rows, one length of channel section, plate of M. Quill, log 1201, lifted 30 Oct 2029 19:30, witnessed D/I N. Kest. Two residue orders on one surface, overlapping; one a licensed threshold ward of the owner's own, one a release not performed by him.*
>
> *What I can state: the order I perceived by contact with the object on 30 October 2029 between 14:38 and 14:47, which is a binding to a stick, an address to a record, a turn completed by the patient, and a release already seated and not his.*
>
> *What I cannot state: which of the two orders on that surface was there first. Whether the release was made in this city. Whether it was made by one person. Who, if anybody, put it in a card, or wrote a depth, or sent a list of six households to a lane.*
>
> *I am not an officer. I hold no licence. I hold no register. This is a document of a member of the public, it is not admissible, and it is not evidence, and it is the only description of that working in existence that anybody has written down.*
>
> *E. Venn, member of the public. 2 Nov 2029, 15:20.*

He signed it and read it and did not tear off the second half, because there is no second half, and then he wrote at the bottom of it, before he thought about it, the sentence a man in his shop had been carrying since the twelfth of October:

*Marguerite Osei. Not written to. Not this month. Still right.*

Then he put it in the notebook and the notebook went on the shelf, and the certificate stayed on the shelf in the row, and the two of them lay about six inches apart on a plank of wood in a repair shop that has no trade account, and one of them had a Registry crest on it and the other one had a man's initials at the bottom of it, and neither of them could get into the other one, and there was no office in the city that would take either of them from him.

The statement went up on the board at Morrow Hill at ten past four on the Friday, and a copy went to the ward authority, and a printed notice went under Dr Okafor's closure notice on the clinic door in Marshall Street, and a man read it out on the steps of the ward building to nine people who had come for a briefing.

> **STATEMENT — SEQUENCE DESK, MORROW HILL, 2 NOVEMBER 2029**
> A dangerous working took place at the Kettle Rows Community Clinic, Marshall Street, on the afternoon of Tuesday 30 October. It has been contained. No dangerous working is in progress at that premises and there is no continuing risk to patients at that address. **The event is described publicly as a contained accident involving an unregistered ward tool.** The clinic will reopen on Monday 4 November at 09:00.
> There is no continuing danger to the public. The matter is closed.

A complete self-directed working, performed by a man of seventy-eight, at a named address, on a named afternoon, certified by a named office, with a schedule number.

A contained accident involving an unregistered ward tool.

Both of them were true, in the sense that a thing can be true and say the whole of nothing, and both of them came out of the same building on the same afternoon, and the second one is the one that went on the door of a clinic in Kettle Rows, and the second one is the one nine people heard on a step.

At twenty-five to five he was in a car outside Morrow Hill with Rennick, who had come down to say three things because there were three things.

"One: the dispensary counter ward on that line flickered twice during the incident and held both times, and Jules Ardent has put that in his bench book in his own hand with the times, and the circuit is being replaced in full and the light is on the new one this morning. That is a real line in a real file and it is the only good news in the whole of this week.

"Two: the file is not yours. It has not been yours since the fifth of October and it is not going to be yours again on the strength of a certificate, and if anybody in this building tells you otherwise they are trying to be kind to you.

"Three." He looked at the steering wheel. "There is a room booked for half past nine tomorrow morning and the Deputy Chief is going to be in it, and I have read the booking, and it is not a censure, and I am not going to tell you what it is because I have got a rule and you know what my rule is. What I will tell you is that the booking came in at twenty-five to five on Wednesday afternoon, which is a day before the certificate was signed and two days before your dispute was entered, and the man who booked it did not know either of those things when he booked it, because he booked it on a piece of paper that arrived at four o'clock."

Elias Venn got out of the car and stood on the pavement outside a police building on a Friday evening in November.

At five o'clock the supplement came out, and it is half a sheet, and it has the crest on it and a heading and one line under the heading, and the line is the word *dispute*, and under the word there is a cross-reference to Schedule 3, and under that there is a schedule number.

That is all it says. Eleven people on Marshall Street will see it, and about forty in Kettle Rows, and a ward authority clerk will file it, and in about a year somebody will write in and ask what it means, and somebody will tell them it means a police officer disagreed.
