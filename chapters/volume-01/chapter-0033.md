# Chapter 0033 — A Favour Asked

The assessor came at eight on Wednesday morning with a schedule on a clipboard, and Elias Venn met him in the shop with the Factors drawer wedged shut and the carbon book inside it, and the morning went the way such mornings go.

The man was not unkind. He wanted the monthly slips for ward material, and Elias showed him the statements through the gap in the drawer without taking the book out, fuse wire and elements back to the spring, nothing that held a working, nothing with a licence class. The assessor wrote *no ward components handled* on his schedule, signed it, and said he would return if the position changed. It took forty minutes and cost Elias a morning's bench and Otto a morning's patience, and the cat slept through all of it on the radio bench in the sun.

"You handled that well," said Otto afterwards. "Twenty years in that drawer and never a channel."

"Long may it stay so," said Elias, and walked to Morrow Hill with the single side of paper folded in his inside pocket, arriving at half past nine with his hands in his pockets and his standing exactly what it was.

Nadia Kest came out of the Sequence Desk at half past nine on Wednesday morning with a file under her arm that was not 441-KR, and saw Elias Venn standing by the car-park wall with his hands in his pockets, and stopped.

"You have no reason to be in this building," she said.

"I know," said Elias. "I am a member of the public standing by a wall. I am not asking to come in."

She looked at him for a long moment, then jerked her head toward the far corner where the vans parked and nobody ate lunch.

"Four minutes," she said. "Because I have a board at ten."

He walked with her to the corner. The rain had stopped and the tarmac shone.

A constable washed a van with a hose. Gulls turned over the hill. The Sequence Desk windows stood open on the second floor, and from one of them came the sound of a board being wiped — the laminate squeak Elias knew from ten days of keeping a board honest, now in somebody else's handwriting for a Holloway Street burglary he would never hear about again.

"I went to Fenwick and Marsh yesterday," he said. "Twelve Cutter Street. Draughtsmen. The register behind the counter holds DRG HW-CS-03, Sheet one of two, Issues A and B. The keeper refused me correctly. Opens on a case number or a maker's authority. Read in place, photographed in place."

Nadia nodded once. "That is the same shape as Bramwell's ledger and the trading-names register. I am not surprised."

"I am asking you for a favour," said Elias. "Not as an officer. As a member of the public. Carry one side of paper to Paul Rennick. It asks him, as the officer on 441-KR, to consider attending the drawing office and having the entry read in place. It names the reference and nothing else. It asks nothing about any file."

Nadia was quiet for a while. A van reversed with its alarm going.

"You want me to become your route into a file I signed you off," she said at last. "I signed the retention review that took you off 441-KR. I wrote on it *other — connected certified record, application pending*. I told you in a car park that the department is better off without you on the file. I said the review was the last thing I would sign on that file with you."

"You did," said Elias. "I wrote it down."

"And now you want me to carry paper to the man who holds the file, so that you can have a register read that you cannot open yourself." She shifted the file under her arm. "Give me one reason why I should, that is not about you getting what you want."

He had prepared for this on the walk over, and the preparation left him now, which was probably correct.

"Because the frame CS-3 describes has not been found," he said. "Because the seat in it is cut to a picture and the picture was made from a description and the description gave a depth that is Mara Quill's figure. Because somebody drew that and somebody cut it and the register is the only thread that runs back to a person. Because if Rennick does not open it, nobody opens it, and the workshop stays a rumour."

"That is four reasons and all of them are about the case." Nadia looked at him levelly. "Here is my reason against, and I want you to hear it whole. Rennick is decent and thorough and six years in the unit. He has read the whole file on a corner of a desk with his coat on. He has put your four sheets into it because they are a document. If I walk a request from you into his room, I put him in a position where refusing you looks like protecting the department and agreeing looks like doing you a favour. You have no standing, Elias. Asking through me does not give you standing. It gives him a problem."

"Yes," said Elias. "That is true. That is why I am asking you to carry paper and not a message. Paper he can refuse. A message he has to answer to you."

They stood in silence. Somewhere above them a window went up.

"You disagree with me," said Nadia at last, "for a valid reason. I disagree with you for a valid reason. Say the rest of it."

He told her about the Issue A line as Draper had confirmed it — *to be confirmed* — and the Issue B line with the figure, and the six days between them, and the certified copy in a drawer Mara Quill could not open. He told her without raising his voice, because the car park was a public place and a favour asked quietly is a favour that can be refused quietly.

"The rest is Niko Senn," said Elias. "He is booked back into Witness Services tomorrow morning at half past eleven. He holds a true order he cannot honestly say he saw. He has a card in his pocket I have refused three times. I am not using him as leverage to get a counter opened, and I am not asking Rennick to approach him. I have written that on the paper so Rennick does not have to ask."

Nadia held out her hand. He gave her the single side, folded once. She read it standing by the wall, all of it, twice.

*To D/Sgt P. Rennick, Sequence Desk, officer on file 441-KR. From E. Venn, member of the public. Request to consider attending Fenwick & Marsh, 12 Cutter Street, and having the register entry for DRG HW-CS-03 read in place. Reference only. No request about any file. Niko Senn not to be approached for standing. — E.V., 17 Oct 2029.*

She folded it again.

"I will carry it," she said. "I will minute in my own book that I carried paper, not a message, at your request, with the time on it. I will not speak for it. I will not follow it up. If he refuses, you will hear it from him and not from me."

They stood a moment more. A gull cried over the hill. From the open window above came a voice calling a file number, and another answering. The department went on being the department, with or without either of them.

"Nadia," said Elias. "The review you signed. The ground on it — *other, connected certified record.* Was it true?"

"It was the truest box on the form," she said. "You had an application pending for a connected record. The department is better off without you on the file while that is true. Not because of what you did. Because of what you published. You have four people in this city who are frightened, and a practice note with your name in it as a worked example, and a plate with your breach on its sleeve. A file cannot carry all of that and stay a file."

He nodded. He had asked, and she had answered, and the answer was the same one she had given him in the car park on Saturday, in four minutes, without softening anything.

"That is all I am asking."

"It is not all you are asking," said Nadia, "and we both know it. It is the whole of what I will do." She put the paper in the file under her arm, on top, where it could be taken out without opening anything. "And Elias. This is not the last thing. Do not mistake it for one because you want it to be small. A favour recorded is a favour that can be counted, and somebody will count it."

"I know."

She walked back toward the building without looking back. He stood by the wall until she had gone in, then walked out of the car park and down toward Kettle Rows.

Marguerite Osei's note was in his inside pocket. He had carried it for seven days. He had not replied, because an answer is correspondence and correspondence has a file on it, and the depot on Tanager Street appeared in his notebook that morning only as a named absence: *will not write to M.O.*

At the shop the telephone was ringing. It was Mara Quill, to say that the laboratory would be open Saturday morning for a measurement and for nothing else, and that Jules Ardent was to come if he could read a channel without touching anything.

"We can," said Elias. "Thank you."

"Do not thank me," said Mara. "Bring the stamped copy and leave it in your pocket until I tell you to take it out."

He wrote *Mara Sat 20 Oct, 10:00, stamped copy stays in pocket* in the notebook, with the time, 12:10, and put the pen down.

He walked back to the car park wall and stood there a while after she had gone in, with the tarmac drying under his feet. The favour sat in her file on top, where it could be taken out without opening anything. It would be minuted in her own book with the time on it. Somebody would count it one day. That was the price, and he had agreed to it before he asked.

The afternoon went to the shop because the shop was the only place that would have him without a form. He mended two kettles, refused a ward cooker again with the same words, and at three took the bus to Saint Orra Reach with a parcel of elements for the hospital workshop, because the hospital paid cash and cash paid the rent. The bus smelled of rain and disinfectant. He sat on the right-hand side so his good ear faced the aisle, the way he had sat since September, and watched the city go past without reading any of it.

Mara Quill's laboratory stood two rooms behind the linen office with its door shut and its log inside. He did not go to it. He left the parcel at the workshop hatch, signed, and came back by way of the market, buying bread and soup and cat food with money from the till book, writing each item down because a man whose trade account is being looked at writes everything down.

The bell rang at five. It was the assessor's clerk, come to say the assessor was satisfied with the morning and would not attend Thursday after all unless the position changed. Elias said he understood, and the clerk went away, and Otto took the wedge out of the drawer with a screwdriver and put it in his pocket without a word.

He sat in the back room for a while with his hands flat on the bench, the way a man in a smock had taught a boy to, and then took them off again. The carrier bag stood under the window. The cat slept on the radio bench in the front. The drawer with twenty years of carbon slips opened clean when he tried it, because Otto had unwedged it.

He left it closed.
