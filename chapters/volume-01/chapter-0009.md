# Chapter 0009 — The Missing Release

The laboratory at Saint Orra Reach had three chairs and Mara Quill had pushed two of them together and put a board across them and on the board she had laid out four sheets of paper and a plate.

"No objects," she said, before he had got the door shut. "Not the plate, not a pencil I have touched, not your phone, and not me. If you want to be in this room you will sit on that chair and you will read numbers off a page, and if your hands go anywhere near any surface in here I will stop the meeting and I will not apologise for it."

"Say the order out loud so I can follow."

"I will." She put her finger on the first sheet. "Which I have not done for a police officer before, and which I am doing because you behaved on Wednesday night and because there is no other way to do this and get a result you will be allowed to use."

The board was titled, in Mara's flat capitals: **NO CONTACT — FIGURES ONLY.**

---

"Sheet one. My first comparison, Wednesday evening, after nine. Plate against swab fourteen from the left edge of the desk. I wrote these numbers before your hand went anywhere and I have not changed them since, and if anybody asks me to change them I will produce the instrument's own log, which is kept in a different building and which I do not have the key to."

She turned the sheet so he could read it.

"Four acts in the residue, and the residue is continuous, and the continuity is the woman's. Her door working, seated in a drilled and filled frame, addressed at a person, turned, and released at the threshold. Four acts, all of them her own, all of them ordinary, all of them laid down by a licensed working that cost ninety credits and eleven minutes to seat. There is a fifth thing on the same plate and I want a word about it before I give you the numbers."

"Go on."

"The fifth thing is release-shaped. It has the timing profile of a release, it has the load profile of a release, and it sits exactly where a release sits when a working has finished and wants to stop being unfinished. And it is not hers." She turned the sheet over and showed him the back, which had a hand-drawn diagram of a jaw. "It is on a different phase from the other four. Everything she did has a settling curve. This has none. It arrived already settled."

Elias felt the room get very quiet and very cold, and heard his own voice come from a long way away.

"Meaning."

"Meaning it was not made in that room," said Mara. "Meaning somebody made it somewhere, on something, and brought it, and seated it into a drilled and filled frame that is exactly the right shape to receive a release. It came in on a solid object and it went off. And when it went off, it went off on a person, and that person was in a doorway, and the person in the doorway was not the woman."

"You are telling me it was a tool."

"I am telling you it was *delivered*." She turned her finger on the paper. "Elias, I want to be extremely careful here, and I want you to be extremely careful too, because this is the entire case and because you are about to do the thing you always do. It could have arrived on a tool. It could have arrived on a token, a plate, a coin, a stamped piece of brass, a card, a bead on a chain. Any solid object small enough to sit in a channel. And I can tell you it was not made in the room. I cannot tell you what it arrived on. And *you* cannot tell me what it arrived on either, and that is not a small gap, it is the gap."

"Then what did I say on Monday?" Elias said. "I said a second release. I said somebody put it on a frame and let it set."

"You said a second release," Mara agreed. "And a release is something a working does. And a release is also the name of the box on the form." She pulled the third sheet round. "And here is where you and I disagree, and I want the disagreement written down, because it is the most useful thing on this board."

---

Sheet three was her comparison of the transfer order's sequence with the plate.

"The order has four acts in the box," Mara said. "The form's own instruction above the box says a standard residence transfer is three acts, bind, move, settle, and it is a housekeeping act, it should not even have a sequence in it. But somebody has filled in four, and the fourth is a release, and the release has the same phase signature as the fifth thing on the desk."

"Same act."

"The same act, with the same settling curve, arriving in a different medium. That is not a coincidence and it is not a coincidence you can put in front of a tribunal either, because the honest word for it is *familiar*." She wrote a word on the corner of the sheet and turned it round.

The word was **template**.

"Two objects, two media, the same release-shaped thing with no settling curve, arriving into a slot that was waiting for it," said Mara. "That is a template. A template is a physical thing that carries a shape from one job to the next. And you do not get a template in this city in a hardware shop, Elias, you get it from a person, and a person who has one got it from a person who had one."

Elias's hands had gone flat on his own knees.

"And the clamp," he said.

"And the clamp," said Mara. "Which is at an address in the outer wards, in a bench room, in a bench I have never seen, being held by a man who drove it there at half eleven on Wednesday night, and of which I have exactly eleven numbers and a note that says the channel measured empty at eight fifty-two on Wednesday evening. Eleven numbers and a note. That is all I have of it. That is all anybody in this city will ever have of it, because I put a hand in it and there is no instrument in the world that can take a hand out."

"You put a hand in it," Elias said.

"I did not put a hand in it." She looked up. "You did, and I have written that down, and I am not going to soften it for you, and I am also not going to let you carry it alone, because I was standing in the room and I told you to go and stand in a corridor and you did not. That is one half of it. The other half is that I had a live plate running and I did not put my hand over the clamp and I did not stop you, because I was looking at a number." She put the pen down. "I want that in the file in those words. Not as an apology. As a fact about how these things happen. Not because a policeman wanted a number. Because a number was in the room."

She let it sit, and then she went on, and she did not soften anything else either.

---

"The numbers disagree with you," she said, "and you are going to have to decide what to do about it, so I am going to give you the disagreement in the order in which it will be asked about at a hearing, which is the order that will do the most damage."

She put four sheets side by side.

"One. I have a plate from a room. I cannot put it in front of a tribunal, because a salt-glass plate is not a certified instrument unless it is on a Concord register, and mine is not, and mine is not going to be, because I do not have a Concord licence and I have decided not to have one. So what I have is the testimony of a forensic mage who is not certified, about a plate, and a police sergeant who lives upstairs. That is what I am. I have been that for six years and I have got very good at it, and I am telling you where the ceiling is before you walk under it."

"Two: the transfer order's box. That is a public document and anybody can read it, and its four acts are a fact about a piece of paper, and its certificate number and its timestamps are facts about a system. But nobody can say where those four acts came from, and a defence will say — correctly — that a form filling is not a working."

"Three: your reading." Mara's voice did not change. "Your reading is real. I do not doubt that it is real and I have watched you be right about things that nobody else could see. And it is not evidence, and it is not a certificate, and if you put it in a file as a finding, then the first competent defence counsel in this city takes you apart, and they will not do it by calling you a liar, they will do it by being polite and by asking you, gently, eleven times, exactly what you mean by *I saw*."

"And four?"

"Four is that you are the man who lives upstairs, and that is in the file in your own handwriting, and every single one of those four things is made worse by the fact that you are the man who lives upstairs, and it will get worse the longer you are on this case, and the Registry knows it, and they have written it into the schedule."

Elias looked at the board for a while.

"The old phrase," he said.

Mara stopped.

"What?"

"The Morrow Street file." He was looking at the transfer order's four acts and not at her. "I wrote a line into that file when I was twenty-four and I have not been able to unknot it since, and the line is *no release was recorded at the threshold*, and I have read that line about nine thousand times, and I read it as a statement about a working that never finished. That a man's working came apart before it could close and a ward went and twenty-three people died, and eleven months later a maintenance fitter called Teodor Rusk was certified as the last responsible act, and his daughter was nine at the time, and he is dead, and I have never once been able to make that sentence sit down."

He put his hand flat on the board, on the paper, on the edge of the sheet, and Mara Quill's voice came across the room in a tone that had no volume in it at all.

"Off it. Now. And do not tell me that you did not mean to."

He took his hand off the board as though the table had bitten him.

"You gave me that rule yourself, four minutes ago, and you gave it to me." She had not moved from the chair. "There are four sheets on that board and a plate in a locked case in another building, and you have now touched one of the four with a hand that is not a glove, three days after a hand of yours went into a channel I had measured as empty, and I am going to have to write down both of those, because I am the one who will be standing in front of a man who is paid to be unkind about exactly this. Do you understand that this is a document now, and not a mood?"

"I understand."

"Say the rest of it," said Mara. "It is still true. I am not going to pretend it is not."

He put his hands in his pockets, which was a thing he did when he wanted them to be somewhere he could account for.

"There is a release in that room. There is one on the plate. And it is the fourth thing, and it is not hers, and it has no settling curve, and it arrived on something. And the phrase in my file is not describing a working that failed to release. It is describing a working that was *released by somebody else*."

"Careful," said Mara.

"I am being careful. I am not going to say who. I am not going to say why. I am going to say the shape is the same, and that the shape is the same, and that is all I am going to say." He heard his own voice go flat. "And there is a reason I am not going to say anything else, which is that there is a red box in my flat with a thread knotted round it, and inside that box is the only document I have ever read that uses the phrase *the fourth act* as a term, and it is a Concord form, and it is a *form*, with a box on it, in the middle, labelled—"

He stopped.

"Labelled what?" said Mara.

"*An act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own.*"

The extractor hood made its small polite noise.

"So it is a category," said Mara.

"It is a category on a form," said Elias. "Somebody in this city fills in a box like that once a year. It is a *line item*. Twelve years ago a man in a Concord office wrote the words down in a report about a collapse that killed twenty-three people and made a maintenance worker's name the last responsible act, and I have been furious at that man for twelve years, and I want you to understand that I am now frightened that he was filling in a box."

---

They worked until four.

What they produced, in the end, was a document with a title and a number and four sheets attached, and the title was the plainest thing Elias Venn had ever put his name to.

**DISAGREEMENT OF THE ASSIGNED DETECTIVE — RECORDED.**
*Sequence Desk file 441-KR. Kettle Rows matter, 14 Kettle Rows, deceased R.*

*1. The assigned detective has, on three occasions between the 18th and the 19th, perceived an order of working in material residue. This perception is not evidence, is not a certified sequence, and identifies no person, no motive, and no intent. It is set down here so that the file contains it, with its limits attached, instead of in a notebook in a flat in Kettle Rows.*

*2. The assigned detective does not accept the classification in the draft certificate at item 3, on the ground that it records a self-directed working with no third-party address, on the ground that the residue measured in this matter contains an act which is not attributable to the deceased and is not attributable to the door, and which the material does not attribute to any person.*

*3. The assigned detective states that he is unable to say what that act is, whether it was performed in the room or delivered into the room, who made it, or why. He states that he is aware of at least one ordinary explanation for his own account of it that he is not currently able to exclude.*

*4. The assigned detective is resident at the address in which the working occurred, at the second floor, and has been for eleven years, and has made a declaration to that effect to the assigned inspector and to the file, and considers himself to be the person in this matter least entitled to be believed.*

He read it twice. Then he read paragraph four again and did not take it out, because it was true, and because Nadia had taught him on the first day that the only useful time to be the man everybody is about to distrust is while it still costs you something.

"Item three," Mara said. "Item three is going to be the whole document."

"Item three is the only honest thing in it."

"Item three is a detective sergeant telling a tribunal that his own perception has an ordinary explanation he cannot exclude. Do you know what a competent defence counsel does with that? They stop. They put the case down and say, *thank you, detective*." She was looking at the page and not at him. "And then they never ask you anything else, and there is a young man called Niko Senn in a clinic who is safe for another month, and Jules Ardent keeps his certificate, and the thing that did this walks."

"I know."

"And I am going to write it anyway," said Mara, "under my own name, without yours. If your name is on my figure and a defence takes the figure out, the figure goes with it."

"That is the right way round," said Elias, and it came out of him before he had decided to say it.

"I know. That is why I have been arguing with you about it for two days and I have been winning, and I would like you to sit down and enjoy it."

"What did you ask for, though."

"I asked you to let me put my four figures off the threshold plate into the file under my own name and without yours. Not the clamp, which is gone and which you destroyed, and not your perception, which is not evidence. Four figures, on my plate, on a Wednesday evening, with my instrument's log in a building I cannot get into." She was gripping the edge of the board. "Elias, that plate is the only thing in this city that says a release-shaped act arrived in a room. If it is never in a file, then in a year a Registry officer will stand up and say that no instrument in Veyra found anything unusual in the room over Tamsin Ro, and he will be able to say it, because it will be true that nobody in an office has written it down."

"Then write it down."

"I am going to. And I am going to have to tell the plate's chain of custody, and I am going to have to list every person who has been near it, and there is one person on that list who lives at the address."

"There is."

"And I am going to list him," said Mara, "because if I do not, then in a year somebody produces that list without him on it, and that is how people disappear from records. He is in it as a person who was in the room, and the plate's sleeve will say so, and your name will be on the sleeve in your own hand, and there will be a date next to your name and a time, and anyone who wants to know what you did will have to ask me."

She did it at the bench while he watched, and she did not ask him to leave.

The sleeve went into a clear pocket. She wrote the address, the date, the lift conditions, the two witnesses, and her own signature. Then she turned it round and put it in front of him with a pen.

"Sleeve only," she said. "The glass is not for you and never will be."

He signed his name beside her initial and wrote the time, and then, because he did not know what else to do with his hands, he put his fingers on the corner of the pocket and stopped, one inch short of the glass.

"You are going to be all right," said Mara.

"That is not a thing I can check."

"No," she agreed. "It is not."

---

At ten past five his phone rang and it was Bettina Haile, Registrar-Counsel of the Proof Registry, who had a voice like a woman reading a menu and who had been, Elias understood within ninety seconds, extremely helpful to him in a way that was itself a kind of care.

"Sergeant. Thank you for the disagreement. It has been circulated, which is more than I expected, and I have to tell you that item four has caused a certain amount of comment."

"I imagine it has."

"Some of my colleagues read item four as an attempt to disqualify yourself from the matter, which would be a strong move and would succeed, and I have advised against it because I have read the file and I do not think you are doing it for that reason." She let a small silence in. "I would like you to know that I read it the other way. I read item four as an honest man's way of making himself the least credible person in the room, which is a strategy I have seen before, and which usually means he is frightened of something."

Elias looked at Mara, who had turned away and was washing a plate that did not need washing.

"I have a question," he said.

"Of course."

"The public wording." He had the draft of the information sheet in front of him, four lines, released to the Civic Press desk at six. "It says *a contained accident involving an unregistered ward tool at a Kettle Rows address*. I would like to know who wrote that, and when, and whether it was written before or after the residue was measured."

There was a pause of about two seconds, which was the first thing Bettina Haile had done that was not effortless.

"It is on the information sheet already?"

"It is on the sheet dated the nineteenth."

"Yes, well, the sheet has a long lead time." Another pause. "It is drafted by our information office. It is a *draft*, Sergeant. It is a summary in ordinary language for residents, and it is not a finding, and I am sure you can see that *contained* is doing a great deal of ordinary-language work in it."

"I can see that. I would like to know whether the certificate and the information sheet say the same thing, because if they do not, then one of them is going to have to be corrected, and I would like to know which one you intend to correct."

"Do you hear the difference in those two sentences?"

"I hear it very clearly," said Elias. "I am asking which document you intend to correct."

"You have refused," said Bettina Haile, "at four forty-one this afternoon, and nobody in this Registry argued with you about it. There was no meeting. There was a schedule conflict. If anybody tells you otherwise they are embroidering." She sounded almost tired. "What I can tell you is the consequence, and then I am going to let you go home. The hearing is on Monday the twenty-fourth. Without a countersigned preliminary certificate, the Registry will proceed on its own attestation, and a Registry attestation, on this record, is weaker in every respect. It is also, I am told, sufficient."

"I have not put him into the record because he is not a witness to the room. He is a man they tried to use as a door."

"Yes," said Bettina Haile. "And that is a very interesting sentence, and it is not one I am going to put in a file, and I am not going to be unkind about why."

She hung up, and Elias sat in a laboratory in Saint Orra Reach and found that he had been holding his breath for the last part of that.

"You were right about her," Mara said, without turning round.

"I have no idea what I was right about."

"You were right that she is not cruel. She is worse than cruel. She is reasonable."

---

It was Nadia, at ten past seven, and she was in a car again.

"The document request for the hearing pack went out at six this afternoon from Morrow Hill to four addresses," she said. "One of them is the Kettle Rows Community Clinic and the request is for the dispensary's stock records, the ward maintenance book, and any items logged against the account's card. It is signed off and it is due to be *answered* tomorrow morning, which means somebody has to physically walk into that building tomorrow morning with a clipboard."

Elias was already standing.

"Tomorrow morning," he said. "They need it tomorrow morning, which means the carrier they left in that stockroom on Wednesday is on a piece of paper that is about to be copied, and whoever put it there knows that, because whoever put it there is the person who wrote the request."

"Elias—"

"Which means they cannot come tomorrow morning, because the clinic has a night porter and a doctor who does not sleep and a witness protocol that I have personally promised to uphold, and the Registry's own timetable has just told them the exact night they have to be in that stockroom instead."

There was a pause on the line that Nadia Kest almost never allowed herself.

"I am going to say the next part once," she said. "You are not going into that building on your own. That is not a lecture and it is not a condition, it is a fact about what I have got. I have two officers, a transit liaison, and a Witness, Operations registrar who is going to be extremely unpleasant to me for the rest of the year. Jules Ardent is at Saint Orra with a solicitor until nine and then he is, by his own written undertaking, in his shop with his door open until seven in the morning. I will be at the east service door from nine. You will be at the north corridor with me in a car, and you will get out when I say you get out, and if you touch anything in that building I will put you in the van myself and you can complain to the Chief about it on Monday."

"That is the agreement."

"That is the agreement you agreed to on Thursday afternoon in front of a mechanic," said Nadia. "Do not make me say it again, and do not make me enjoy saying it a second time."

Elias Venn hung up, and put his hand flat on the window ledge for a moment, and looked at the laboratory window, at the river, at the dark coming in over Saint Orra Reach where the last run of the night was coming in over the viaduct and changing pitch, and thought about a stockroom eleven minutes' walk from his own front door, full of glass, with a ward line above the counter and a piece of brass in it that somebody had built to be used and was going to be taken back out before the records were copied.

He had a brass clamp at an address in the outer wards that he would never touch again, and a plate with his name on the sleeve of it, and a reading he could not use, and a hearing on Monday, and a file that said he lived at the scene.

It was going to be a long night, and he was going to be in it with three other people, and the first thing he had to do before any of it was walk into a paint works and tell a doctor what time he would be at her door.
