# Chapter 0040 — Standing Open

Tuesday evening Elias Venn sat in the back room with the fortnight's costs in front of him and wrote them out the way the volume counts them: not as symptoms but as standing, money and sleep. The censure on the shelf. The Factors letter in the drawer. The charge sheets in Rennick's file. The daybook photograph in 441-KR with its original in Bay 6. The register behind its counter. The four sheets on their wall. He wrote until ten, slept badly, and woke on Wednesday to rain on the yard and the cat on his feet.

Felix Harrow would not reopen Bay 6 on Wednesday morning without the tenant, and the tenant was at the Sequence Desk answering bail, so Elias Venn stood outside the vault door at nine o'clock with Mara Quill and Jules Ardent and looked at a closed door, which was the whole of the point.

The number above it was fresh. The Yale was oiled. From inside came no sound — no bench, no kettle, no lamp. The worklist hung on its nail in there. The daybook sat closed on the bench with page fourteen in it, photographed but not seized, original staying where it was. Twelve channels at eighteen credits. A seat per a drawing. An account line with an insurer's name in it, in a bench hand, on the third of October, next to a frame. Nobody had sealed anything. Nobody had cleared anything. Nobody had catalogued a single stolen source object, because that belongs to Arc E with the volume close.

"The room stays," said Harrow, with the key on its nail upstairs and his rent book in his hand. "Located, not dismantled. That was the agreement. It stands open in the dark when I lock up, and it stands open in the dark when I open."

Rennick, who had come to log the attendance, wrote it: *Bay 6 viewed 24 Oct 09:00, door closed, no entry, no seal, no clearance.* Harrow countersigned. The photograph of page fourteen sat in 441-KR. The original sat in Bay 6. That was the thread, not the resolution, and the batch would leave it so.

They walked up out of the Cut into a Wednesday that smelled of rain and bread. Jules went to Pell Street to sit in a room with his charge sheet and his clearance and his bench book, all three in front of him. Mara went to Saint Orra Reach to file her 20 October log with its two witnesses. Rennick went to Morrow Hill to put the viewing note in 441-KR. Elias walked to Kettle Rows alone, because Wednesday was for counting.

He stopped on the way at the market for bread and oil, writing each down because a man with no accounts writes everything down twice. The stallholders knew him. The fish stall stood shuttered. The tea woman asked after the clinic board. He answered truly — looked at, touched by nobody — and walked on with the city moving Wednesday around him: porters, nurses, children, a bus with rain on its windows.

Elias walked to Kettle Rows alone, because Wednesday was for counting.

The shop's books did not balance. He laid them on the back bench in the morning light — till book, Factors withdrawal letter, censure, charge-room notes, rent book — and did the arithmetic out loud so Otto could hear it and correct him.

"Cash for parts," said Otto. "Repairs logged. Rent paid to the first. Cat fed."

"Cat fed," said Elias, and wrote it: *24 Oct, shop with no accounts, file closed, censure filed, rent paid, cat fed.*

Niko Senn came at eleven, on his own, with his hands in his pockets and his aunt's cake tin under his arm. He had kept the half past eleven appointment on Thursday or not — Elias did not ask, because a man decides that for himself — and he stood in the shop doorway looking at the back room with the carrier bag on the bench under the window.

"Mr Venn," said Niko Senn. "I am not asking about the sixteenth. I am telling you it is still not finished and still has my name on it as well as hers. I wanted you to hear me say it in this shop."

"I hear you," said Elias. "The card stays in your pocket. The date stays in yours. I am not making either into a route."

Niko Senn put the cake tin on the counter, nodded once, and went out into Kettle Rows with his hands in his pockets. Wen Senn's cakes sat on the counter with the lid on. Elias wrote *Niko Senn, 24 Oct, date still his, card still his* in the notebook and closed it.

At noon Dr Okafor rang to say the board had been looked at once more and touched by nobody, and that the ninety-odd pages were holding. They spoke for a while about ordinary things — the heating, the waiting-room chairs, the night porter's book — because ordinary things were what the board was for. She asked after the shop's books, and he told her truly: no accounts, cash henceforth, rent paid to the first. She was quiet for a moment, then said the clinic paid its suppliers late in winter and early in spring, and that everybody in Kettle Rows knew how to do cash.

At one Nadia rang to say the carrying of paper to Rennick was minuted and closed, and that this was not the last thing. They did not speak long. A favour recorded is a favour that can be counted, and both of them knew somebody would count it. She asked whether he had slept, and he said four hours, and she said that was not enough, and he said he knew. That was the whole of it, and it was enough.

At two the post came with nothing in it, which was itself a kind of news.

He spent the afternoon moving the carrier bag of the 406-page production from the bench under the window to the shelf above it, where the folio, the censure, the Factors letter and the notebook now lived in a row. Every document in one place. The register behind its counter. The daybook in its bay. The four sheets on their wall. The photograph in Rennick's file. The receipt stub in his wallet. Marguerite Osei's note in his inside pocket, unanswered, because an answer is correspondence. Aegis Mutual named once, in a ledger line photographed on the twenty-second, carried here as a held fact and not shouted about: a risk office pricing off reported faults, a Lantern Compact member, a customer in a book, not the hand that made anything, not the reason any ward went dark. Why it holds a certificate for a treatment no patient remembers receiving stayed off the page, sharpened and unanswered, for Chapter 50.

At six he fed the cat, at seven he ate soup standing up, at eight he logged the fortnight: *15–24 Oct: register refused, favour carried, sixteenth asked twice, entry read in place, measured by two, Bay 6 located, ledger photographed, reading taken 14:10–18:40, charges read, censure signed, accounts withdrawn, room left standing.*

Then he put the pen down, went to the front bench, and stood for a while with Otto while the ceiling made its noise and stopped.

"A workshop standing open in the dark in the Cut," said Otto. "A ledger with an insurer's name in it. A man charged and a man cleared. A repair shop with no accounts."

"That is the fortnight," said Elias.

He slept upstairs from ten with the window open on the yard, the shop below him with its drawer that opened clean and its books that did not balance, the bay below the city with its bench and its book, and the city above with its wards flickering once and holding.
