# Chapter 0047 — Somebody Else Keeps the Time

There was a clock on the wall of the corridor outside the linen office at Saint Orra Reach, and at four minutes past nine on Wednesday morning Elias Venn looked at it for a while, and could not say what it said.

He knew what a clock was. He knew the shape of the numbers and he had been reading them off shopfronts and platform indicators and a great many public clocks for thirty-six years, and he stood in front of a white face with a black second hand going round it and the numbers were there and the number that mattered was *nine* and he could not put a *morning* on it.

"It says four minutes past nine," said Mara Quill, from the counter, without looking up, and then she put her pen down and said, "Say that again in your own words and then say the second thing."

"The clock says four minutes past nine and I do not know whether it is four minutes past nine or eleven minutes past nine and I have been standing here since about eight."

"You have been standing here since twenty to nine. I have the door log because I write the door log." She came out from behind the counter with her own book in her hand. "And I am going to say the second thing now, and then I am not going to say it again: from the moment you put your hand on that stick to the moment you took it off, you have not been able to tell how long anything has been going on, and it is not going to come back this morning, and I am the only person in this building who can tell you, and I am not going to be doing it four times a day for a fortnight."

"How long have I got."

"You did not tell anybody that." Mara Quill said it flatly, the way she said things that were going in a log. "Forty-two hours and forty-two minutes, if you want it in a form, and I am not going to be the form. It ends when it ends and I have not got a figure and I am not going to invent one for you, and if Rennick asks me for a number I am going to tell him that the man cannot perceive duration and I will not estimate it, because an estimated duration is a number somebody will use in about four years."

"That is not a duration."

"No," she said. "It is a true sentence, which is more than most of this case has, and you may have it for nothing."

---

The plate was on the bench under the hood with a clean sheet over it, and it was not in the hard case, and Mara Quill said why before he asked.

"It does not go in that case. That case has a foil stencil in it and a sleeve on it and a breach note with your name and my name on the same sheet, and a new plate does not go in beside those, and if anybody in about two years opens that case and finds a fourth plate in it they will not ask what the fourth plate was. They will ask who put it there and why the first three were not enough." She lifted the sheet. "Log twelve hundred and one. Lifted last night at half past seven in this room, on the corridor bench outside the linen office because the item came to me at six and I did not want it in this building overnight, and Detective Inspector Kest witnessed it and signed for it, and I photographed it in place first, on the bench, with the time on the camera and the time on the back of a receipt."

"Nineteen thirty is late for a bench in a corridor."

"It is a laboratory that is behind a laundry, Mr Venn, and the only people in this building after eight are two women who fold sheets and a man from the porters' office who does not speak to anybody." She lifted the hood. "This is what is in it, and I am going to tell you what I can see and I am going to tell you what I cannot, and I have written both of those things in the log, in the ordinary way, because that is what the log is."

Four inches of channel section, split ash, a seat in it, and on the plate under the hood the residue lay in two orders and the two orders were on top of each other.

"That is the bracket's," she said. "That is the honest one. I can see the contact order of a licensed threshold ward, seated in ash on Wednesday the twenty-fourth of October in a kitchen in Tanners Row, and it is a real one, and it is a good one, and it is his. You can see where it starts and where it stops and it has a settling curve on it like a thing that happened where it was made."

"And the other one."

"The other one is a release." Mara Quill put her finger on the hood, not the plate, and moved it a quarter of an inch to the left. "And it is sitting *inside* the other one. It starts before the bracket's order has finished and it finishes after the bracket's order has finished and it is laid across the middle of it, and where they overlap you cannot take one of them off the plate without taking a piece of the other with it, because they have been in the same piece of wood for six days and the wood has taken both of them."

"You are telling me there is no seam."

"I am telling you there is a seam and it is not a gap, it is an **overlap**," said Mara Quill. "Every one of these since the second of September has been a fault, a discontinuity, somewhere you could put a plate and a pen and say *here, and not here*. This is the first one I have had where the copy has been laid down so carefully into a real working that the only place on this plate where you can see it is where it crosses the real one." She put the hood back down. "That is what two and a half months of somebody learning from somebody else buys you, and it is not luck, and there is a man in this city who is better at it than I am, and he is not in this room."

"Can you say it in a sentence a man could repeat in a corridor."

"I can," she said. "It is a false release sitting on top of a real working by the same man, in the same wood, for the same reason, and the real working is two-thirds of the evidence and the false one is on top of it, and that is why the certificate is going to be so hard to argue with, and it is why I have not slept much and I am not going to talk about that."

"You are not going to talk about it."

"I am going to tell you one thing about it and then I am going to stop," said Mara Quill, "and the thing is this. If I have to stand in a corridor on Wednesday afternoon and explain to a man of seventy-eight on oxygen why it is that a number he has never heard of, in a drawer in a building four miles away, is the reason there is a hole in his walking stick — and I am the only person in this city who can say that to him in a way that means anything, and I have no standing at all, because I am not a doctor and I am not a policeman and my plates are on no register in this city — then this is what my professional life has arrived at, and I would like somebody to be sitting down."

Elias Venn sat down on the fire bucket, because it is the only other thing in that room to sit on.

"You have to tell him," he said.

"I know I have to tell him. I have known since Saturday. I have had four drafts of it in a drawer by the door and I threw three of them away because they were explanations, and what he needs is not an explanation, it is a sentence he can carry to his neighbour and say *that is what those people are*." She squared the sheet over the plate. "Go and stand somewhere else. I am doing it just before one o'clock in the clinic corridor with a nurse in the room and you are not going to be four feet away when I do it, because the sentence has your name on it as the man who came to his door and it is bad enough that the woman saying it should not be standing next to a policeman when she says it."

"Elias."

"I know," said Mara Quill. "I have known since the fourth of October. Go and stand somewhere else."

Rennick's note came at half past eleven, on the back of a receipt stub, in his own hand, in a car outside a warehouse on Fenwick Street, where the file was and where the draft was and where the compilation note was.

> *Draft C-441-KR-06, compilation note, third paragraph: witness support — account on file 11 Sept 2029, Witness Services (Sequence Support); see schedule 4. That is the whole of the note. Nothing else is in the note. I am not asking you what you think it means. — PR*

Elias Venn read it four times in the yard at 14 Kettle Rows and then put it in the notebook and did not write one word under it.

---

The Kettle Rows Community Clinic was shut to patients from noon on Wednesday the thirty-first of October until Monday the fourth of November, and the reason was a notice on the door in Dr Okafor's hand.

*The dispensary counter ward failed at 07:40 this morning for a period of about a second. The circuit is being replaced in full. We are closed to patients until Monday 09:00. If you are unwell and you cannot wait, the Saint Orra Reach walk-in is open and there is a bus.*

Underneath the notice there was a sheet of polythene over the waiting-room board, and behind the polythene were ninety-odd copied pages of the clinic's own maintenance book and four sheets of paper in a doctor's ink with four drawing pins in the corners, and a registrar's transfer order in a clear sleeve.

Elias Venn stood in the corridor at ten to one and listened to a woman he could not hear explaining to a man of seventy-eight what had been inside his walking stick, and it took him four separate attempts to work out whether she had been at it for one minute or for eleven, and at the fourth attempt he put his back against the wall of a corridor at a clinic that is shut and stopped trying.

It took her twenty-two minutes. Ahmed was in the corridor at the far end with a mop and he counted it on the wall clock, and afterwards he told two people and neither of them wanted it, and he told a third, and that is the only record in the city of how long Mara Quill stood in a corridor.

---

He slept nine hours on Wednesday night, which is the longest since the seventeenth of September, and he woke at ten past seven on Thursday with a notebook and no times in it for Wednesday afternoon and wrote them out afterwards from the door log in his own shop, which is not evidence, and marked them as not evidence.

Niko Senn came at four o'clock on Wednesday afternoon, on his own, with his hands in his coat, having come up Marshall Street on a bus that he had paid for himself.

He had been told at half past two on the Wednesday, in a room at Morrow Hill with a witness, by Detective Inspector Kest, and he had asked for it to be put in writing, and it had been.

"They are going to certify a working. Dated Tuesday," he said. "Not a working that happened. A working. Four acts. And the fourth thing on the list that they use to agree it happened is an account I gave on the eleventh of September, which I withdrew on the fifth of October in field six in my own hand, and the leaflet says a withdrawn account is superseded and remains on file and does not by itself impugn either account."

"Niko—"

"I have read that sentence about nine hundred times," said Niko Senn. "I read it in a clinic corridor and I read it in my aunt's launderette with a cup of tea going cold and I read it in a call box in Alderney Row in the rain. And every time I read it I thought it was protecting me. And it is not, is it. It is protecting *them*. It says you can keep a copy of what I said and throw away the only person who said it, and nobody has to do anything dishonest, and everybody is behaving correctly, and I am on a certificate about a man I have never met in a street I have never walked."

"You did not make it into this," said Elias Venn. "I have to say that first, because I have said something like that twice this month and both times it turned out to be a way of making a person feel better about a thing I had done."

"What did you do?"

"I put the sentence of an order of mine into a box on a telephone to a woman at Concord Hall on the twenty-first of September, and she read it back to a frightened man in a chair on the twenty-ninth, and it went into a curriculum, and it is a worked example in a practice note in print." He put his hands flat on the bench. "I have not told you that and I am telling you now because you have just been told that your withdrawal was used, and you are owed the whole of it, and you have earned the whole of it by getting on a bus."

Niko Senn stood at the counter of a repair shop in Kettle Rows and looked at the cat on the radio bench for a while.

"You are not going to ask me for the card," he said.

"No."

"That is five times now."

"That is five times now, and the reason has not changed since the fourth of October, which is that a card in my pocket would make you a thing I am carrying, and I have been carrying a man of seventy-eight's walking stick around this city for a week and I have not got the strength to carry anybody who did not ask."

Niko Senn took his hands out of his coat, and there was nothing in them, and he showed them.

"I am going to keep it," he said, "and I am going to be a person who kept it, and I would like you to write that down as a thing a man did, not as a thing a policeman failed to get."

"That goes in the book," said Elias Venn, and wrote it, and it went in as his own line and not as a request and not as an entry about anybody else.

Then Niko Senn said the thing that has been standing in the room since Saturday, and he said it looking at the boards and not at the man.

"You are on a file. I am on a certificate. There is a man in the outer wards who is on a list in three rooms and does not know, and there is a woman at number six in Alderney Row who is on nothing at all, and I have spent four years being a route." He turned round. "I am twenty-five in January and I have decided I would rather be a name in a document than a route, and I have told a police inspector that in a room with a witness, and I would like you to notice that nobody argued with me."

Elias Venn wrote the day's cost out at ten to nine at night, at the back bench, with the carbon book shut and the drawer open, in four lines rather than one, because a man who has lost the use of an afternoon ought not to be allowed one tidy sentence about it.

*Wed 31 Oct. Saint Orra Reach from 08:40. Cannot turn a clock face into a duration. Forty-two hours and forty-two minutes and it does not end when I want it to. Mara will not estimate it and will not be doing it four times a day.*

*Log 1201, one plate, two overlapping orders, the false one on top of the real one, and no seam to put a pen on. She is the best instrument in this city and she is frightened and she has four drafts in a drawer and three of them are explanations.*

*Corridor, 12:38 to 13:00, twenty-two minutes by Ahmed's clock. She told him. I was in the corridor and I was not in the room.*

*Niko Senn, 16:00, told by Nadia with a witness and in writing at his asking, and the witness support line on a draft certificate is his withdrawn account, and he got on a bus to tell me about it, and he showed me his hands, and I did not ask.*

*Do not write in this book what I think that means. Write the times. The times are the part I can be trusted with at the moment.*

Then he wrote the time, and Mara Quill telephoned from the laboratory to tell him what it was, because he had asked her to, and he had asked her to on Saturday in a laboratory behind a laundry and she had said she would not do it four times a day.

It was nine ten.
