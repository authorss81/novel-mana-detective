# Chapter 0028 — What Schedule 1 Is Worth

Room Two at Morrow Hill had a different smell on Monday morning than it had had on the twenty-fourth of September, and it took him about four seconds to work out that it was because the room had been aired.

"Sergeant Venn." Bettina Haile had a chair on the wrong side of a table from him, which was a thing she had not done in September, and a bundle on the table between them with a spine and a number on a white label. "Sit down. Do not sit down if you would rather stand, because I have about eleven minutes and I would rather you sat."

---

"You are not on 441-KR."

"No."

"Then you are a member of the public with a party interest, and I have had a clerk check that this is the correct description of you and not a courtesy one, and it is." She squared the bundle with two fingers. "A Registry cannot withhold a produced document from a party. That is not a favour and it is not a discretion and it is not going to be described to you as either of those things, because the moment it is a favour there is a version of this conversation in which I was kind to you, and I have been reading a file about your refusal for three weeks and I would like it to stay accurate."

"What is it?"

"Four hundred and six pages." She said it without any weight at all. "Tab 3 is the schedule. Tabs 4 to 59 are the attestation and the hearing pack and the exhibits. Tabs 60 to 139 are the witness-support production. Tabs 140 onwards are the application and its exhibits and the account's material. It is on the table because I have nowhere to put four hundred pages in this building that is not a floor."

Elias Venn did not touch it.

"Who applied to strike it out."

"Account 44-9, through a firm, on Friday, at ten past ten in the morning, and the firm is Lindqvist and Roake and the partner who signed it is called Ivo Lindqvist and he has been a solicitor for nineteen years and I have never had a file about him in this building and I am not going to start one." She turned one page of her own file round. "That is the only thing I am going to tell you about the application and I want you to hear why I am saying no to the rest of it. If I tell you who is behind the solicitor, you will build a file around the telling, and you have built a file around a telling before this month and it took a young man out of a clinic. So: no."

"Then I have no idea who applied."

"You have a firm's name and an account's name and a date and a time, and the account is a legal person with an address on Verge Row and a return clause, and a legal person with a return clause wants a box on its own paperwork to look like bookkeeping." She closed the file. "Sergeant. I am going to say a thing and then we are going to stop, because in September I said a thing to you in a lobby and you have quoted it back to me twice since, which is fair, and I am not going to say it again."

"You have."

"I am saying a different one." She took her hands off the table, which was, he understood, the whole of her contribution to the exchange. "A schedule is appealable. The appeal goes to a panel and not to me. If you want to be heard on it, you will be heard by somebody else, in a room with a different set of names on the wall, on a date that has not been set, and you will be entitled to be represented and you will not be entitled to be indignant, and there is no version of this in which I can give you the twenty minutes you want in this room, because there has never been one."

He had not asked for the twenty minutes. He had not got as far as asking.

"Take the bundle," said Bettina Haile. "I am having the whole of it photocopied for you, not only the schedule, because you are a party and a party is entitled to the paper and not to a promise that a clerk will read it out, and I would also like the schedule on the file as a discrete document and not as a page in a spine. She will give it to you at the counter in about ten minutes. Every sheet of your copy carries the stamp *copy*, and your copy is the one you may take out of this building, and the original does not leave it."

---

He read four hundred and six pages between about ten past ten in the morning and four in the afternoon, in a tea room off Fallow Street with a table that had a hard surface and a chair that was not comfortable, having bought a coffee he did not want because the man at the counter had said it was included in the cover charge, and he had not telephoned anybody.

Because a bundle is a job and the job is reading, and the last time he had tried to work four hundred pages in an afternoon he had spent two hours of it being pleased with himself about a folder, and he was not on the file any more and did not have a folder.

The copy of tab 3 came in an envelope with the stamp on every sheet, and a slip from the index clerk in front of it saying that tab 3 of this production was a different document from tab 3 of the bundle produced on Friday, and that the two were not to be read together. It was Schedule 1 — his own four items, in his own small bad hand, four sheets, and on the front of it a printed cover sheet that said:

*SCHEDULE 1. Disagreement of the assigned detective.*
*Bound to the preliminary attestation of 24 September 2029 at the direction of the Registrar-Counsel. Appealable.*
*5 October 2029.*

And at the foot of the cover sheet, in a smaller type, a line he read four times:

*No word of a produced document is to be removed at the request of the officer who wrote it. Removal is a matter for the panel.*

He put that sheet down and picked it up again, because Bettina Haile had written a protection into a production schedule on the day she received an application to have the schedule struck out, and had written it into the system rather than into a letter, and had told him it was not a favour, and it was not a favour, and it was also the only document in four hundred and six pages that had been put there by anybody in that building because of him.

---

Tab 61 was the reason for the day and it took him four minutes to find and about twenty to understand.

**CARRIER SPECIFICATION CS-3.** Issued 30 September 2029. Sheet 1 of 2. Drawing office reference, material callout, a channel section drawn to a larger scale than the rest of the sheet, and in the channel a seat, and in the seat a depth.

The channel was right. He could not read a channel and he could see that it was right, because the same channel section appeared on the carrier that had come off the stockroom wall, and the width, the wall and the lip of the drawing were the width, the wall and the lip of the thing that had been screwed to a brick two floors below his own apartment, and Jules Ardent had said so in a solicitor's rooms ten days ago on the strength of a bag, and nobody had been able to check the bag since the twenty-sixth because it was inside a maker's enquiry.

The seat was not something he could check.

The note in the seat was in Schedule 1's prose.

Not a quotation of it — the actual sentences, word for word, in a smaller hand, as a *note in a seat*, the way a man writes a note in a drawing because he cannot write a dimension:

*Seat to receive an act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own. Depth per description relied on. Held against the assigned account; not to be widened without reference to that account.*

and underneath, in a box of its own, in the same way a key is written into a drawing's title block:

**DEPTH: Fig. 3, Q.**

He sat in a tea room off Fallow Street with a coffee he had not drunk and looked at a woman's figure for about a minute and a half without moving.

Mara Quill had certified four figures on the threshold plate on the twenty-seventh of September at about two o'clock in the afternoon, in a room on the second floor of a building on a shopping street, in her own name, in pencil, with every log number. She had put them in a certified office. She had handed them over. She had refused, on the minute, word for word, to certify that the act was copied, and to certify a motive, and to put any of it on a register.

A certified copy of those four figures is now in a drawer in Concord Hall that Mara Quill cannot open.

And somebody in this city has written **Fig. 3, Q** in a box on a manufacturing drawing, dated the thirtieth of September, three days after it was certified, as the depth of a seat in a piece of metal, and that drawing says *per description relied on*, and *not to be widened without reference to that account*.

He had said, in a laboratory, on the twenty-seventh, that a refusal is a reason to want the thing refused. He had said it as a warning to himself about a stencil.

He had not once thought about a number.

He had read ninety pages when the woman at the counter came over with a cloth and wiped the table round his coffee and said, without any particular meaning in it at all, "You're going to want to move that in a minute, love, the fella at four o'clock wants it."

"Sorry."

"Don't be sorry, be useful, order the second one when you're ready." She took the cloth away and then stopped and said, "You've had your hand flat on that table since about eleven."

He looked down at it. It was flat on the table, palm down, the way a man in a smock had taught a boy to put his hand on a table four floors below him on Wednesday, and it had been there for most of the morning, and the whole of the afternoon.

He put it in his lap and said thank you and did not think about the fact that he had to put it there.

---

Tab 88 was worse and it was only two pages.

**PRACTICE NOTE W-11. Issued 2 October 2029.**
*Order-perception methods in citizen accounts: what may be recorded, and by whom.*

It was an annual-series notice. W-7 was on the wall at the back of Concord Hall on Tuesday morning with its paragraph about withdrawal, and this was the same series, the same issue, the same shelf, and it listed, in a table with four columns, the methods by which a person's account of the order of an event may be stabilised for certification.

The last row of the table was a worked example.

*Method: description of order as perceived by an investigating officer, entered on the support file and held against the account. Worked example: **Schedule 1, Disagreement of the assigned detective, file 441-KR, 24 September 2029** (four items; see item 1). Practitioner guidance: the officer's description is not a finding and does not attribute an act. Where an officer states that he is unable to exclude an ordinary explanation for his own account, the account requires support rather than clarification.*

*Elias Venn read that sentence about nine times.*

*Where an officer states that he is unable to exclude an ordinary explanation for his own account, the account requires support rather than clarification.*

Item three. The sentence Mara Quill had said in a laboratory on the twenty-first of September was going to be the whole document — *a detective sergeant telling a tribunal that his own perception has an ordinary explanation he cannot exclude* — had been copied out of his own schedule, into a practice note, on the second of October, under a heading about practitioner guidance, and used as the reason why a frightened man called Niko Senn needed an order put into him.

His own honesty had been the published justification. He had written a thing into four items in about four minutes on a Friday afternoon in order to stop himself being believed, and a public authority had read it, and had agreed with him, and had then built a service out of agreeing with him.

---

Tab 140 was the application, and tab 141 was a schedule of four-act boxes on the account's own documents.

That was the part he had to sit with for a while, because it was the part that made sense.

Lindqvist and Roake, for rehabilitation account 44-9, had filed a schedule. It listed, on the account's own paperwork — intake forms, treatment schedules, a discharge letter, and two transfer orders — every box on those documents that carried a four-act sequence, with the date, the form number, the office, and a single column headed *account position*, in which every line read the same thing:

*Box completed by a third party under a continuing-treatment schedule. Account position: no knowledge and no objection. Account position is bookkeeping only and is not an admission.*

Six lines. Six boxes. And a legal person that could not book a patient into the Kettle Rows Community Clinic and reached people by merging files was asking a panel to strike the only document in this case that had been produced by a police officer, so that all that would be left of the four-act boxes in this city was a schedule in which an account that cannot book a patient into a building says it has no knowledge of any of them.

An account with a return clause wants a box to look like bookkeeping. Bettina Haile had said that at ten o'clock in the morning and he had written it down and had not understood it until twenty past two in the afternoon.

---

Tab 356 was four lines and he read it twice.

*A procurement schedule produced in the application. Line item: channel section, 300 mm, 60 lengths. Supplier class: SUPPLY. Licence class code and premises held on file.*

Nothing else. A class, a code and a premises on a second page, and the premises was the second floor of a building on Carrow Lane above a printer's, and the name against it was a name he had written in a notebook ten days ago in a queue in the ground floor of a building on the far side of the hill.

He did not write it down. He did not need to; it was in a document, in his own stamped copy of a production, three hundred and fifty-six pages from the back, and the original of it was going to go into a panel in a room he was not yet allowed into.

---

He telephoned Jules Ardent at twenty-five to five from the call box on the corner of Fallow Street, and it was the only telephone call he made all day, and he made it because a channel drawing is a mechanical document and Jules Ardent is a man who can read one.

"I'm not going to say the file number and I'm not going to say whose it is. I want one sentence read. On a drawing, where a dimension is given as *per* something else — is that a dimension?"

"No. That's a referral to another document. The drawing doesn't say how deep the seat is, it says go and find out, and the reason a drawing does that instead of putting a number on it is that whoever drew it hasn't got a number. He's got a description, and the description is somewhere he can point at, and the drawing is a picture of the thing he's been told to make." A pause on the line, and then a second sound, which was a man sitting down. "Elias. Is there a drawing."

"There's a drawing, and I'm not going to tell you what's on it, and that's not distrust, it's that I've read four hundred pages in a day and I would like one of them to be mine for about a week."

"That's fair, and I'd have said no too." A pause. "Then somebody's going to be asking me about a drawing on Friday or the week after, and I'd rather hear it from you than from a notice, and I'd rather you told me the truth about which notice, and I am not asking you to tell me the truth about that tonight, because you sound like a man who has not put anything down in a week."

---

He read the last two hundred pages on the bus and at the counter of a chip shop, and he got back to Kettle Rows at about ten past nine on Monday night with a stamped copy of tab 3 in one pocket and four hundred and six stamped sheets in a carrier bag, and the carrier bag went on the bench under the window in the back room of his own shop, on top of a folio, next to a cat that was asleep on it.

There was a telephone on the wall of the shop and it worked, and it was a quarter past ten, and Saint Orra Reach is eleven minutes away by taxi on a Monday night, and he stood in the middle of the floor with his hand on the receiver for about as long as it takes a kettle.

He did not lift it.

The reason was not kindness and he was clear with himself about that at the time, and it went into the notebook afterwards in one line: *I did not telephone her because I had nothing to ask her that I was entitled to ask, and because the sentence I wanted to say to her is that somebody has written my name on the depth of a thing, and that sentence asks her to be glad, and it is a sentence that would make her glad, and there is not one further thing in the world I want to hand to that woman this month.*

Elias Venn stood in the middle of the floor of a room in his own building with a man of twenty-four withdrawn out of a file and a woman whose number was a dimension and a municipal clerk who had not been asked for anything yet, and he understood the shape of what he had done on Friday the twenty-first of September.

He had written four items in about four minutes in order to stop himself being believed.

It was the most useful page anybody had produced for the people who had done it.

And there was one plate in this city that could take the measurement a drawing was refusing to give, and it was in a laboratory two rooms behind a hospital linen office in the name of a woman who had held a certified copy of her own figure in a drawer she could not open since Thursday, and he was not allowed to touch it. And there was one room in this city with a bench in it that could hold a plate that was not on a register and a die that was on one at the same time, and both of them had refused that room in writing in the same week, and neither of them had ever said so out loud to anybody else, including each other.
