# Chapter 0019 — The Unknotted Gap

On Saturday morning he read a footer.

There were ninety-odd pages of a dispensary's own ward maintenance book pinned to the waiting-room board of the Kettle Rows Community Clinic in a block on the left, forty-one months of it, copied and dated, with a felt-tip line across the top of the block that read *September 2017 to date, produced without request, Dr S. Okafor*, and on the right of the board, in a clear plastic sleeve, one sheet of transfer paper with a registrar's signature and a certificate number and a stamp that said ISSUED 16:51 WEDNESDAY.

Elias Venn stood in front of it for about four minutes at twenty past ten on the Saturday morning and read the sequence box again, four printed lines, and then read the printed instruction above the box, and then read the four lines a third time, and then he went down the sheet to the bottom right corner, which he had not looked at on Friday morning because on Friday morning he had been counting.

In the footer of the form, below the signature block, in eight-point type, it said:

*Form R-2 (rev. 4) · Sequence box: **SEQ 4(c)** · Witness, Operations · Proof Registry*

He wrote it in his own notebook, in the small bad hand, with the date and the time, and then he stood there for another minute, and then he did the thing he had learned to do in a laboratory in 2019 and did not enjoy, which was to go back over it and find out what was wrong with it.

Nothing was wrong with it. That was the first thing. He had gone looking for a reason it could not be right and there was not one. It was a printed footer on a public form, and the form was on a public board in a clinic that had put forty-one months of its own failures on the same board three days ago for entirely different reasons.

The second thing was this. A form's code is in the footer because the form is printed in ten thousand copies and the printer has to know which one to send you, and so the code is not a secret and is not a privilege and is not a classification. It is a shelf number. It is the one part of a document that exists in order to be looked up.

The Morrow Hill forms counter is on the ground floor, past the certificate office, in a corridor with a radiator in it, and it sells three things to anybody who asks: the concordance of sequence-box codes, which is a bound book about two inches thick; the printed practice notes, which are not public; and the stock forms themselves, in sealed folders, for people who have countersigned a request.

The concordance cost four credits and the woman at the counter gave it to him in a plastic sleeve and told him it was the 2029 printing, which was the current one, and that if he wanted the 2017 printing they were sold out and it was not coming back.

Elias Venn sat on the bench by the radiator with the book in his hands and turned to the section headed *Fourth-order and corrective entries*, and the section ran to about two pages, and he read it twice, and it was exactly as boring as it needed to be.

The preface on page four said the categories had not been renumbered since the 2006 consolidation, that a category number meant the same thing in every printing back to 1998, and that where a category had been withdrawn it would still be listed with a line through it and a date. He read that paragraph four times because it was doing a very large piece of work very quietly, and what it was doing was this: it meant that a 4 (c) in a form printed in 2017 and a 4 (c) in the form on a clinic counter in Kettle Rows this morning are the same object, in the same words, written by the same office of the same authority.

*4 (a) An act of turn entered before an address.*
*4 (b) An act of address entered after a turn.*
*4 (c) **An act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own.** Field is completed on the standard form in every case in which a working has a turn and no release of its own. Entry does not assert that a release was performed. Entry asserts that the record is to be treated as though a release was performed, for the purposes of this record only.*
*4 (d) A sequence with no recorded release, closed at the discretion of the certifying office. **Withdrawn from standard forms, 2011. Retained for indexing of pre-2011 records only.***

He put his finger on 4 (d) and kept it there for a while.

4 (d) was his sentence. *No release was recorded at the threshold* was a plain sentence a man had written in a report, and the sentence had a number, and the number had been on the shelf list since before the collapse, and it had been crossed off the shelf list in 2011 and kept on the shelf for one reason only, which was so that somebody could find the old records that used it.

He had spent twelve years carrying a sentence that turned out to be an indexing instruction.

Then he went back one line and read 4 (c) a third time, and then he did the arithmetic, which was not arithmetic, it was the ordinary act of comparing a thing he had in his hand with a thing he had in his head, and it took about four seconds.

*An act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own.*

Chapter 9. Friday afternoon, in a laboratory, with his hand flat on a board of paper and a woman's voice in his ear telling him to take it off. He had said the words out loud in a room with a recorder on it, and they were the words he had read off the middle of a Concord form, and the form had been the old file, and the file had been in a red box in a red thread in his own flat for twelve years, and he had said the words and then Bettina Haile had telephoned and Nadia Kest had telephoned and the Registry's information office had already written a line for a dead woman on a Wednesday afternoon before anybody had measured a room.

And the box on the transfer order in the clinic's waiting room is 4 (c).

And the word *the fourth act* is not a phrase anybody in this city uses. It is a section number in a shelf list. It is *four (c)*, and it has been *four (c)* since before he was a police officer, and a man in a Concord office in 2017 filled in a box on a report about a collapse that killed twenty-three people, and gave that man a name, and then wrote *the fourth act* in that report in a way that meant *four (c)*, and Elias Venn had read that word for twelve years as a name for something a person had done to another person.

He sat on a bench by a radiator in a corridor on the ground floor of Concord Hall with a two-inch book in a plastic sleeve and understood that the thing that had been standing between him and the truth for twelve years was not a conspiracy and not a person and not a secret. It was a shelf number, and about four hundred people in this city fill one in every year, and a man at a counter in a room above a shop that replaces watch straps had just told him that the same shelf number was on a piece of paper twenty feet from a dead woman's cold room four streets from a night market, and neither of those two facts was going to be on anybody's television.

And then he sat there for another quarter of an hour and did the other half of it, which was the half that mattered, and which was this: to know that his file had *four (c)* in it, he had to open the file.

The only document in the world that said what was in the box on the Morrow Street report was the Morrow Street report, and it was in a Tribunal archive on the far side of Morrow Hill, and it was a certified record, and a person who wanted to read a certified record had to put a name and a case number against the request, and a case number on a Morrow Street request filed by the assigned detective on the Kettle Rows matter is a piece of paper that says, in the plainest available words, *this detective wanted the old case and this is why the old case is relevant to his new one.*

He walked home at eleven and did not go out again.

The box was on the shelf in the second-floor flat above the shop, on the left, at the height of his own shoulder, where it had been since he moved in, and it was a red document box of the kind that comes in a pack of ten from an office supplier, and somebody — he did not remember who, and it was not himself — had tied a length of red sewing thread round the lid of it in a knot that had a name in it that he had never looked up.

He stood in front of it.

He had two metres of red cotton thread in his fingers' memory and a shelf, and it would have taken about ninety seconds, and there is no version of this case in which the police officer in the Kettle Rows matter would still be the police officer in the Kettle Rows matter on Tuesday.

What he had instead of the file was a memory of a page, and a memory of a page is a bad thing to build on, and he went and looked at what he actually had, which was as follows.

The report had been about nine pages, and the box had been on page four, and the box had had a tick in it, and the tick had been a real tick rather than a printed one, which had bothered him at the time and had bothered him for twelve years since, because a printed box is filled by a hand and a printed tick is a decision somebody made about the form before anybody put anything in it. Under the box there had been a date, in 2017, and beside the date there had been a reference, four characters and a number, and he had written it down in a notebook on the Thursday night because he had thought it was a case number, and it was not a case number, and it had the shape of the section number of a list.

He had written the four characters down somewhere. He did not know where. He had a notebook in the shop office that went back to about 2013 and there were about nine of them in a drawer, and finding the right page would take him an evening, and if he found it, it would be his own handwriting, written by a man of twenty-four, on a document of his own, and it would not be a record, and it would not be a copy, and a defence would call it a reconstruction and they would be right.

A labelled disagreement was stronger than an unlogged certainty. He had learned that on the eleventh of September and it turned out to apply to himself.

He put his hand flat on the lid, once, and did not touch the thread, and then he went and sat down in the chair by the window with a sheet of A4 and a pen.

*29 September. Comparison note. Not a finding. Not a request.*

*1. On the evening of Friday 21 September I read, from a transfer of place of residence under continuing treatment, patient N. Senn, at a dispensary counter in Kettle Rows, standing back, hands visible, without touching it, a sequence box containing four printed acts. I did not then read the form's footer. I did not read the footer until today, 29 September, at 10:20, from the clinic's own board copy.*

*2. That form is Form R-2 rev. 4, sequence box **SEQ 4(c)**.*

*3. SEQ 4(c) is a category in the public concordance of sequence-box codes, current printing, page 61: "an act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own." The concordance further states that entry of 4(c) does not assert that a release was performed.*

*4. I have for twelve years had a file in which a Concord report of 2017 concerning the Morrow Street ward collapse uses the words "the fourth act" as a term. I have until today understood that phrase as describing an act performed by a person. It is the section number of a category in the same public list.*

*5. I have not opened the file. I am not able to state from any document in my possession that the box completed in that report was 4(c). I am stating the resemblance from my own recollection of a document I have not opened, and my own recollection is not a record and is not evidence and would be worth nothing to anybody.*

*6. To establish item 5 I require a copy of the Morrow Street report. Any such request requires my name and a case number. A case number on a Morrow Street request filed by the assigned detective on file 441-KR creates a documented interest by the assigned detective in a connected matter, and under departmental practice that interest is disclosed to the Registry and to any panel hearing the connected matter.*

*7. Therefore the resemblance stands and cannot be joined, and the reason it cannot be joined is on this page.*

He got up and went and stood at the window, and then he came back and added an eighth item, because there was one document in this case that he had never once looked at, and it was hanging twenty feet from a dead woman's cold room in a clear plastic sleeve on a waiting-room wall.

*8. A 4(c) entry is completed by a certifying office, and every issued certificate carries the name of the office that issued it. There is such a certificate in this file: C-441-KR-04, issued 16:10 on Wednesday 19 September and attached to the transfer order in the patient's hand, which is in a clinic's own file and is also posted on a public board in a waiting room. I have read the box on that document four times. I have never read the issuing-office line.*

*9. I am not requesting the certificate either. I am recording that I have not read it.*

He read it, and he did not soften item seven, and he signed it, and he dated it, and at the bottom he wrote, *This is a note. It is not a request for access and must not be processed as one. If it is processed as one, somebody in this building has done something worse than what I have done.*

Then he went down to the office at half past one on a Saturday afternoon and put it on Nadia Kest's desk and watched her read it, which took her about four minutes and which she did not interrupt.

"Item six," she said.

"Item six."

"You have just written, in your own hand, a document that explains to any future prosecutor exactly how a police officer could have got at a sealed record, and then told them not to do it."

"Yes."

"That is the first thing I have ever seen you write that I would have written." She put it in a folder and initialled the corner and wrote the time. "It goes in as a note. I am not filing it as a request and I am not forwarding it, and if anybody asks me why not, I am going to say the word on the last line and let them decide."

Then she got her coat, and they went out to the car, and she did not start it for about a minute.

"Before Monday," she said. "Two things, and I am telling you both while it is quiet and neither of them is bad news for me."

"Go on."

"Item five." She had her hands on the wheel and was not looking at him. "You have written that your statement about your own file rests on your memory of a document you have not opened, and that is correct, and it is the first time in twelve years you have said that about that box out loud to another person, and I want you to know that I have known that for about eleven years and that I have never once said it to you, because you were twenty-four and you needed a reason to keep opening files, and because it was not my business and because I did not want you to stop."

Elias did not say anything.

"And the second thing," said Nadia Kest. "On Saturday morning, the twenty-second, in a room above a licensing office on Vine Street, between a magistrate and a fan heater, I wrote four hundred words for the Deputy Chief about the Morrow Street sequence position, and I sent it up the chain at about ten to one, and I did not have any difficulty with it at all."

"And?"

"And I left one line out," she said, and put the key in the ignition. "I have been carrying that since Saturday morning and I did not tell you on Saturday and I did not tell you on Monday and I am telling you now because we are in a car and I cannot keep it in a folder any more. I left out the words *no release was recorded at the threshold*, and I have known since about eleven o'clock on Saturday morning exactly why, and I would like you to be extremely angry with me about it, because I would like to be extremely angry with me about it, and I have not been able to be that on my own since Saturday."
