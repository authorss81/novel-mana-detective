# Chapter 0035 — Read In Place

The night before, Elias Venn had laid out his clothes the way he had laid them out before the preliminary attestation on the twenty-fourth of September: coat brushed, notebook in the inside pocket, receipt stub in the wallet, stamped copy in the folio and not in the pocket, because Rennick had said *bring nothing* and Otto had written it down. He slept badly and woke at six, worked the bench till eight, and walked to Cutter Street in a morning that smelled of wet stone.

Paul Rennick stood outside Fenwick & Marsh at ten to ten on Friday morning with his coat over his arm and a camera in a case, and looked at Elias Venn standing three paces from the door with his hands in his pockets.

"Morning," said Rennick. "Nadia carried paper. I read it. I am here as the officer on 441-KR to have an entry read in place. You are here as the member of the public who asked. Stand back, keep your hands where the keeper can see them, and do not touch the book."

"Understood," said Elias.

Imogen Draper opened the door at ten, read Rennick's number off his card, wrote it on her own form, signed it, and had Rennick sign under her. Then she took the bound foolscap register from the shelf behind the counter and laid it on the lectern with both hands.

"Reference DRG HW-CS-03," said Rennick. "Sheet one of two. Issues A and B."

She turned the pages with a bone folder. The queue stood back. Elias stood with his back to the window and his hands flat on the counter edge where she could see them.

The register was a bound foolscap ledger with the pages numbered in print and the entries in three hands — the oldest in iron gall, the middle in ballpoint, the newest in fibre tip. Elias saw the dates go past: August, September, September. The whole of the city's drawn metal sat in that book in reference order, and his reference sat among them with nothing to distinguish it except the fact that he had asked for it.

"Entry," said Imogen Draper, and read it out in the flat voice of a woman reading a thing that is not hers to interpret.

*DRG HW-CS-03. Carrier channel. Sheet 1 of 2. Issue A, 24 September 2029, drawn T.B. Issue B, 30 September 2029, drawn T.B. 26 September, checked F.M. Material: galvanised channel section, 300 mm stock; foil 0.4, 90 x 140. Seat note per description relied on. Issue A depth to be confirmed. Issue B depth Fig. 3, Q.*

She stopped.

"That is the whole of the entry," she said. "The register says who drew a sheet and when. It does not say why."

Rennick nodded. "Photograph in place, please. I will move the camera, not the things."

He took two photographs of the open entry with the reference card in frame, the register flat on the lectern, nothing lifted, nothing shifted. Then Draper closed the book with the folder, put it back on the shelf behind the counter with both hands, and wrote the time on the form: 10:22.

Elias copied nothing while the book was open. When it was back on the shelf, with Draper's permission, he copied the reference, sheet, issues, initials and dates from Rennick's camera screen into his notebook, with the time, 10:26, and the words *copied from photograph at counter, with permission.*

Rennick wrote his file note on the counter in front of them both: *Attended Fenwick & Marsh 19 Oct 10:00 as officer on 441-KR. Entry read in place, photographed in place. Member of the public asked, officer decided. Niko Senn not to be approached for standing.*

He signed it, Draper countersigned the attendance, and the note went into Rennick's pocket. The original register stayed behind the counter where it had always been.

Outside, Rennick put the camera away.

The street had filled while they were inside — a courier with a trolley, two builders arguing about a lintel, a woman selling chestnuts from a brazier though it was only October. Rennick stood in it with his coat over his arm like a man who belongs in any street, and Elias understood again why Nadia trusted him with a file: he did not hurry, did not perform, did not make a register into a stage.

"Three things," he said, because that was his number and he kept to it. "One: the drawing itself is not produced. A drawing that cannot be found is not a drawing destroyed, and I am not going looking for it on your asking. Two: the initials are T.B. That is a hand and a date, not a reason. Three: I am putting the photograph in the file because it is a document, and the file that has a photograph about itself in a corridor and not in the file is a file somebody has decided not to know something."

"Thank you," said Elias.

"Do not thank me," said Rennick. "Nadia carried paper. I decided. That is the whole of it."

He got into his car and drove toward Morrow Hill with the photograph in his pocket and the file note with it. Elias walked back to Kettle Rows with the notebook in his inside pocket and the stamped copy still in the folio in the back room where it had stayed all week.

He stopped on the way at the market and bought oil, bread, and soup, writing each down in the notebook under the date because a man whose trade account is being looked at writes everything down. The market smelled of fish and rain. Kirsten Brannigan's stall stood shuttered with a notice about the lease, and he looked at it for a moment and walked on. The door schedule in her drawer stayed in its drawer. The transfer order on the corridor bench stayed under its tin. Every document in one place. That was the rule that had survived three batches, and it would survive this one.

The initials were Tomas Beck. He did not know that yet as a man; he knew it as two letters. A hand that had drawn a seat with no dimension on it on the twenty-sixth of September, checked by somebody with the initials F.M., Issue A with a depth to be confirmed, Issue B with a depth that was a woman's figure certified on the twenty-seventh.

At the shop he wrote the entry out full under the date, 19 October 2029, with the times, and added: *Register stays 12 Cutter St. Photo with Rennick. Stamped copy stays in folio. Drawing not found ≠ destroyed.*

The afternoon went to the shop's books, because a man who has had a register read for him owes the day to ordinary arithmetic. He balanced the till, logged the week's repairs, filed the Factors statements in order, and at three took the bus to the market for parts, sitting on the right-hand side with his good ear to the aisle. The city moved past — porters, nurses, children out of school — and none of it asked him for anything, which was the first rest he had had since Monday.

Otto Vahl had the post sorted when he came back. Among the bills was a letter from the Sequence Desk with a window and a frank, addressed to *Mr E. Venn, Venn Repairs*, and Elias opened it standing at the front bench.

It was a notice that his breach of 9 October had been received and logged, that a departmental file had been opened, and that he would be heard in due course. It was not a censure and not a reprimand. It was the paper that comes before one.

He put it on the shelf above the bench with the notebook, on top of the folio, where he could see it. The cat looked at it and went back to sleep.

At four o'clock Jules Ardent rang the shop bell. Elias told him through the doorway, because the shop was open and anybody could hear, that the register had been read in place, that the initials were T.B., that Issue A said *to be confirmed* and Issue B said *Fig. 3, Q*, and that the register said nothing about why.

Jules stood for a long moment with his hands in his pockets.

"Then somebody got a number between the twenty-fourth and the thirtieth," he said. "Somebody got Fig. 3, Q, off a certified copy and gave it to a draughtsman. That is not a workshop. That is a sentence travelling."

They stood in the doorway while the street went past. A bus sighed. The dry cleaner vented. Jules looked at the shop with its bench, its drawer, its cat, and said the thing he had come to say.

"Elias. Tomorrow Mara measures. I will bring the bench book. I will read at arm's length. I will not touch anything that is not mine." He paused. "And I want you to tell me before any notice, if the measurement says what I think it will say. Not after. Before."

"I will," said Elias. "In the corridor, before the room, with the book between us. As agreed on Monday."

"Yes," said Elias. "Tomorrow Mara measures. Ten o'clock. Bring your bench book and nothing else."

"I will," said Jules, and went back down Kettle Rows toward Pell Street with the notice still on his door and the bench room still shut behind it.

The evening went to preparation, because Saturday morning in a laboratory is earned on Friday night. Elias cleaned the stamped copy's sleeve, checked the folio, laid out his coat, and wrote the day out full with every time: *19 Oct 10:00–10:22 read in place, 10:26 copied from photograph, initials T.B., Issue A to be confirmed, Issue B Fig. 3, Q. Register stays. Photo with Rennick. Drawing not found ≠ destroyed. Niko Senn not approached.*

Niko Senn's name sat on the page with the line under it, and below that Elias added the week's whole ledger of restraint: *Alderney Row asked, Fallow Street asked, dates do not meet. Card stays in his pocket. Appointment kept or not by his decision. Not followed to Concord Hall.*

He closed the notebook, put it on the shelf with the departmental notice about the breach on top of it, and slept from ten till five with the window open. The register stayed behind its counter. The photograph sat in 441-KR. The drawing itself was still not found, which was not the same as destroyed, and the distinction would matter in Chapter 47 the way distinctions always mattered in this case: quietly, on paper, when somebody needed it.
