# Chapter 0056 — What a Protection Order Is Not

Dr Okafor read the four folded slips on the counter of the dispensary at nine minutes past eight on Tuesday morning, standing up, in the corridor, with three people waiting behind her, and she read them the way she read a list, and then she put her hand flat on them and asked the next person in the queue to wait, and said, "No. In here. Both of you. Give us four minutes and then the woman at the front can come in, and tell her I have not forgotten her."

Then she took them into treatment room two, which was shut and dark and had a couch in it and a screen and no window, and she shut the door, and she did not sit down.

"How many."

"Four. Since the nineteenth of October. Eleven credits each."

"Handwriting."

"Two hands. Hers and mine." Elias Venn put the slips on the couch, squared, the way he put everything down. "Hilda Achterberg wrote out the fees and the asks out of a book of her own. The dates and the procedure numbers are the other woman."

"Rhoda Nesbit."

"Yes."

"Registered?"

"No. Six years."

"What does she charge for the one on the top?"

"Eleven." He looked at it. "It is procedure 10. Recovery of a name or date to a person who has lost it. The tariff is twenty-six credits. I read that in a library on Sunday and I have written it out, and I can show you the page, and I have not taken it out of this room."

Dr Okafor did not take it.

"Show me the page."

He showed her the page. She read tariff note 4 and then read it again, and then she read note 8, and then she put both hands on the counter by the couch and said a word that was not a swear word and was not funny.

"Number 4," she said. "**No claim may be made under this schedule.** Mr Venn, you understand what that means. It means when a woman of seventy-four loses her sister's name and goes to a room above a dry cleaner and pays eleven credits and the name comes back and then goes by Wednesday, there is no line anywhere in the city for that. There is no box for it. There is no code. If she goes to a doctor she gets *grief* or she gets *the grid*, and if she goes to a proper practice it is sixty credits and there is a woman on a list who can be rung, and if she goes to a room above a dry cleaner it is eleven credits and it is a favour, and the whole thing is built on the fact that nobody is making a claim about it, so there is no claim, so there is no record, so there is nothing to fund, so there is nothing to check."

"Yes."

"And somebody is making claims about it."

"Somebody is putting claim numbers on her counterfoils, in an office, going back to the fourth of May 2027, and the tariff that forbids it went into print in November and the person writing the numbers has not stopped."

"Then it is not a favour any more," said Sana Okafor. "Whatever it was in 2025, and whatever it was when she gave me the last free one, whatever it was for the parade woman, whatever it was for the man she went to because he could not travel — it stopped being a favour the minute somebody started getting paid for it on paper, and the person who is getting paid is not her and the person she is paying is me, in the sense that I am the place where the cost of this lands when it lands, and it will land here, Mr Venn, it will land in this corridor on a Tuesday afternoon in a person who has come back four times in three weeks, and I want you to hear me say that I know that."

He heard her say that.

"I have heard it," he said. "And I have got four slips with no names on them and a woman in Corrie Street who has been doing this for six years and who can describe the third box on the sheet she works from, and I have got no power in this city, and there is not one door I can walk through now that I could walk through in September. So I have come to the one person in Kettle Rows who has a licence, a building, a board, and a hundred and forty names on a list, and I have come to you and not to anybody else, and I want a protection order over eleven rooms and I want it this week."

"You want a protection order."

"I want a request from this clinic for a temporary protection order in respect of eleven unnamed premises, and I want it to name no person, and I want it to have a date on it and an end date on it."

Sana Okafor put both hands on the counter by the couch and looked at the ceiling for about four seconds.

"Do you know what you are asking me to do to a woman who has been coming to my patients' building for nothing?"

"I have a fair idea."

"You have not got the first half of it." She came down off the counter and stood in front of him. "A protection order is not a poster. A protection order is an order of a competent authority in a city with a ward grid, and it says *these premises are not to be used for the performance of a restricted procedure*, and it is served by somebody, and the first time it is breached there is a file, and the second time there is a charge, and the third time there is a case. And a file about a restricted procedure in a front room above a dry cleaner is a file about eleven-credit Fridays in a house where two of my patients are on the second landing and one of them is seventy-four. **The moment there is a file, that woman stops telling me what happened to her on a Friday.** And she will not tell me because she will be protecting the person who sat with her, and she will do that without being asked, and she will be right to, and I will lose her."

"I know that."

"You know it in the way a man knows a fact. I know it because I have had a woman come into that waiting room in 2027 who was a domestic in a house in Halloway Street and whose employer found out she had been to a practitioner, and it did not go well for her, and she stopped coming to me as well, and I did not find out for a year, and I have never once forgiven the practitioner, and she was not even a bad one. She was a woman doing the work that nobody would do for eleven credits. **The bad thing was never the work. The bad thing was always the file.**"

"I am not going to argue with that."

"Good. Because I have got conditions and if I do not say them out loud you will assume I have some and I have not."

---

She took a sheet of the clinic's own notepaper, which is the same paper the ninety-odd copied pages of the maintenance book are on, and she wrote on it standing up, and she read it back to him before she put her name under anything.

"One. If the work continues it continues **with a person in the room.** Not me, not you, not a person with a form. A person whose only job is to be a person in the room. I will supply that. I have two girls on the clinic rota who are doing a placement and who want the hours, and neither of them is qualified for what is being done upstairs and both of them know that, and that is the correct answer to a question nobody has asked out loud yet."

"Two."

"Two. A written note of what was asked for, **in the patient's own words, on the day**, and a copy on the board in the waiting room." She wrote it. "Not the woman's words. The patient's. And not the fee, because the fee is her business and the clinic does not charge for a woman's memory and does not have a line in a book for it."

"And three."

"Three. The board." She stopped writing and put the pen down and looked at him. "The board is mine. It has got three things on it and it has had three things on it since October and everybody in this building knows what they are and two of them are mine. I am putting a fourth thing on it. In a week, in my waiting room, in public, in the same place, under polythene, with my own three lines and my name under yours."

"You do not have to put your name under mine."

"I have to put my name on it, and if my name is on it then it is a document of this clinic and a person who comes in here has a right to say *this is the doctor's*, and that is exactly why it will work and exactly why it is dangerous, and you know both of those and I know both of those." She picked the pen up. "And the fourth thing is the reason. **It is not going up with your name on it, or mine, or hers.** A document that names Rhoda Nesbit is a document that gets served on Rhoda Nesbit. A document that says *a method is in circulation in this district that is printed on a single sheet and is not in the manual it cites* is a document that can be handed to a constable, or to a licensing inspector, or to the man in the council who came to see my patient's book in March, and it does not give any of them a name on the first page. It gives them a sheet. And the sheet is the thing they have to go and look at, and looking at it is work, and work is the only thing that has ever made anybody in this city do the right thing."

Elias Venn stood in treatment room two of the Kettle Rows Community Clinic with four folded slips on a couch and a piece of notepaper in a doctor's hand and found that the thing he had come for was going to be decided by a woman who had never once in four months asked him for anything and had just given him the whole of her reasons for free.

"Where do I put the request."

"You do not." Sana Okafor folded the sheet and put it in her coat pocket. "If you write a request it has a number and a number on a piece of paper in a district like this is a name with a coat on it. **The clinic makes it. Eleven unnamed premises, one street each, four of them in this district. I write it and I sign it and I put my registration number on it, because I am the only person in this building with a registration number that means anything and I am not going to lend it to a member of the public who has been formally reprimanded a fortnight ago.**"

"That is fair."

"It is not fair, it is correct, and you are welcome." She went to the door and opened it and the woman who had been waiting at the front came four steps forward and said, "Is it all right, doctor," and Sana Okafor said, "It is going to be all right for about four months, and I am going to tell you a sentence now and I want you to remember that I said it to you in this corridor and not in an office." The woman said she was listening. "There is a woman who comes on Fridays and it is eleven credits and it is very good, and she is not on a list, and I am not going to stop her this month. Do not tell anybody I said that."

---

There was a man at the dispensary counter at twenty to eleven that she had not sent for.

He was about fifty and he had a folded piece of paper in his hand, the way people hold a piece of paper they have been holding for three days, and he was pleasant, and Sana Okafor listened to him for about ninety seconds and then said the word *afterwards* twice in a voice that made two people in the corridor look up, and the man stopped talking and said, "Yes. Afterwards. Sorry. It's just — is she all right, that's the question. She's my wife's mother and she lives with us and she's had it three times and she told me about it and she said you don't do it on Fridays because you'd be cross, and I said I'd fetch her, and I didn't fetch her, and it's been a fortnight."

Elias Venn was twelve feet away with his back against a wall and he had a notebook in his pocket and he did not take it out.

"And when you didn't fetch her," said Sana Okafor, "what did she say?"

"She said her friend's daughter had come and taken her."

"Your wife's mother is seventy-one years old and she lives with you. Who is the friend's daughter."

"I don't know. She said Rebecca."

"There are four Rebecas in this waiting room this morning and two of them are on this corridor and one of them is four years old." Dr Okafor did not raise her voice at all. "When you take her on Friday, do not ask her who she went with, and do not tell her you have asked, and do not let anybody in the house make a joke about it, and if she asks you what the money was for then say *for getting a name back* and nothing else, and do not say it like a joke. Can you do those four things?"

"Yes."

"Then do them and do not come back and ask me, because I will not be able to tell you anything and you will not be able to hear it."

The man went, and put the folded paper on the counter as he went, and it was a prescription with a date on it from the Friday before, and Sana Okafor looked at it and did not pick it up.

"Four," said Elias Venn, when she came past.

"Four what."

"Possibility four." He said it quietly, out of the side of his mouth, and she stopped, and he said it again. "The ninth, the sixteenth and the twenty-third of October, and the fourth of those words she did not write down until the second of November. A woman in a book in Corrie Street came three times and did not come a fourth time, and she wrote four words about it, and she gave me four possibilities and said she hated three of them. She did not write down the fifth one and she made me promise."

Sana Okafor stood in a corridor of her own clinic with a prescription she had not picked up in her hand.

"Then somebody has told an old woman in this district that she should not have been coming to a woman of fifty-eight above a dry cleaner, which costs nothing, and which is the cheapest thing anybody has ever done to her," she said. "Do you know what I would have said, in 2027, if somebody had come to me and told me a domestic in Halloway Street was going to a practitioner? I would have said *good, somebody is helping her.* That is what I would have said, Mr Venn. **I would have said it and I would have been wrong and I would have said it pleasantly.**" She put the prescription in the bin. "Go and do your six lines."

---

The order went in on the Wednesday as a request from the director of the Kettle Rows Community Clinic, and it came back on the Thursday morning of the twenty-second, and it is in the drawer in a cabinet in a municipal building and it is worth setting out exactly, because nobody in this case has ever yet been handed a piece of paper that was not better than it had to be.

> *TEMPORARY PROTECTION ORDER — UNREGISTERED PROCEDURE, RESTRICTED CLASS*
> *Applicant: Director, Kettle Rows Community Clinic.*
> *Premises: eleven, unnamed individually, in the district of Kettle Rows and one in the outer wards.*
> *Grounds: performance of a procedure at Schedule D, numbers 2 to 14, at a fee below the registered fee, in premises that are not those of a registered practice, and without the record required at tariff note 8.*
> *Direction: the premises listed shall not be used for the performance of a restricted procedure until such time as the premises are inspected, or the person performing there produces a registration.*
> *Duration: from the date of service to **the fourteenth of January 2030.***

Elias Venn read that at the back bench on the Thursday morning with the paraffin heater on and the cat in the drawer, and he read the last line about nine times, and then he got a pencil and wrote out the arithmetic on the back of a receipt, because he had not written an answer down in a shop for four months and it is the only arithmetic in the case that is not somebody else's.

*22 November to 14 January is fifty-three days. Seven weeks and four days.*

*It is not a protection of the work. It is a postponement with a date on it.*

*And on the fourteenth of January somebody in a municipal building is going to write a word on a form, and if the word is *extended* then the clinic has asked for this and got it, and if the word is *discharged* then in fifty-three days a woman in a flat is back on her own with no order in front of her and nothing at all in the city.*

*There is no version of this document that fixes anything. It is a winter's worth of paper with a date on the end of it and I asked for it and I would ask again.*

He put the receipt in the notebook and then took it out again and put it on the shelf next to the certificate, and the two of them sat on a shelf about six inches apart, and one of them was made by an office that had certified that a man of seventy-eight performed a complete dangerous working, and the other one was made by a doctor who had certified nothing and had put a name and a registration number on it and had written *unregistered procedure, restricted class* across the top in her own hand where a lawyer would see it first.

Then he went up the stairs to his own flat and opened the cupboard where the notebook was not, and found in the inside pocket of the coat he had not worn since the third of November a piece of paper folded twice, and he did not open it, because it was Marguerite Osei's note, and it had been in there since the tenth of October, and he had told three people in four months that he was not going to write to her, and he was not going to, and he put the coat on the back of the chair where it had been on the ninth of October instead of in the pocket where it had lived for seven weeks, and that was the only decision he made that day that cost him nothing.
