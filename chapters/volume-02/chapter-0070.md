# Chapter 0070 — Which of the Three

The panel met in a first-floor room at the establishment on Bell Street with the blinds down and the door shut and three officers at a table and a chair against the wall for him, and a folder on the table with his name on it, and a clock two minutes fast, and it was ten to ten on Wednesday the twelfth of December.

The chair was Chief Inspector Dermot Lusk, forty-four years in, and he listened with his chin on the end of a pen.

"Your statement of reasons is in the folder, and it is good, and we have all read it, and I am going to tell you what is in it in about fifteen seconds so that you know what I think you have done." He turned the first page over. "It is about a prohibition of the twenty-ninth of September, a witness, a breach note, and a plan of the twenty-ninth of October at eleven-twenty. **It is about the paperwork.**"

"Yes, sir."

"And it says nothing whatever about the floor of a treatment room at ten to three on the thirtieth of October with a named member of the public on it and a practitioner behind him."

"No, sir."

"Why?"

Elias Venn had been given one sentence about this in a car park on the Verge Row side on the twenty-first of November and he was not going to say it twice and she had made him make it sound like his own, and he had, and it was in a book, and it was this.

"Because I have no standing to report a dangerous working," he said. "I am not an officer. The file is not mine; it belongs to a sergeant. I have no access to the first-scene samples and I cannot ask for any, because the request would have to be made by the officer holding the file, and he is not me and he is not in this room. And if I stand in a room like this one and tell three officers what I perceived on the thirtieth of October, then in about eleven minutes one of you will have to decide whether a man with a formal reprimand is a witness, and I have read the file, and the answer is no, and if the answer is no then the three of you have to turn it into a matter for somebody else, and by the time you have done that the certificate on my shelf has been in a drawer for six weeks and a man of seventy-eight has a rubber ferrule on his stick."

Nobody wrote anything down for about four seconds.

"That," said Chief Inspector Lusk, "is the best answer anybody has given this panel in about two years and it is the answer I expected you to have and I want you to know that I expected it and that it does me no good at all, and I am going to explain why in a moment."

---

"The finding is upheld," said the chair, flatly and without any pleasure, and then he read the finding out because that is what the form requires. "Fourth examination of a physical object. The thirtieth of October, 14.38 to 14.47, by contact. On the shaft of a walking stick belonging to a named member of the public, in a treatment room, in the presence of the owner and a medical practitioner, **during a working in progress.** The prohibition of the twenty-ninth of September required a witness and the plan of the twenty-ninth of October at 11.20 records that you were not one. No consent naming a sequence. No breach note written before the act. It is well made out and I have had nothing in it to argue with since November."

"Understood."

"The consequence is varied." He turned a page. "**Unrestricted access to samples 441-KR/01 to 04 is not restored. In its place: an application in writing for access to a named certified record, supported by the written concurrence of the officer holding file 441-KR.** Which is a sergeant, and is not you."

Elias Venn looked at the page for a while.

"Sir, can I ask a question about the word *named*."

"You can."

"A named certified record. With a concurrence from a sergeant who holds the file."

"That is the variation."

"Then I have a door," said Elias Venn, "and I have a number in my head, and I have a sentence from a claims director on the Verge Row side on Friday, and the sentence is that a member of the public is not a party to a claim. So the door is a door I can walk to and cannot walk through, and the reason is not the department, it is the department's own method, and the method is the thing I have been trying to put in front of three officers for four months."

Chief Inspector Lusk put the pen down.

"Say the last part again, slowly, and do not put the number on the table, and if you have put the number on the table then we will stop and I will tell the secretary."

"It has not been written down anywhere," said Elias Venn. "I have had it in my head for six days and I decided on Thursday in a corridor not to write it, and I would like it minuted that I decided that, and why, and by whose advice, and I am not going to say whose."

"Minuted," said the chair, and the officer on his left wrote it, and he said afterwards, "You do not understand how much that has just cost you in this room. Four weeks ago I would have had to stop you twice."

---

"You have got until eleven o'clock," said the chair, at about twenty past ten, "and I would like to ask you a question which is not a question about the paperwork, and you may decline it, and if you decline it I will not think less of you and I will probably think more."

"Ask it."

"Mr Venn, in November you put a single sheet of A4 on a public board in a clinic in Marshall Street. There was no name on it. I have read about it in a file I am not supposed to have, and I am not going to ask you about that, and I am going to ask you this: what is the number of people who could describe that sheet from memory."

Elias Venn thought about it.

"About eleven, I should think," he said. "It went up at eleven on a Tuesday morning with four drawing pins of my own in a waiting room with nineteen people in it. There is a doctor, and a woman who came to read her own sheet and stood with her hands behind her back, and a young man on a bus, and I would guess three or four more out of the nineteen looked at the edges."

"That is what I wanted to know." The chair put his hands flat. "**If you do that again, and I am not saying you will and I am not saying you should not, then about eleven people will be able to describe that sheet in about four years, and one of them will be on a panel, and I will be the one sitting here, and I will not be able to get a second hearing out of you.** That is what I wanted to say, and it is the only advice I am going to give you, and I have broken about four rules giving it."

Then he sat back and said, "Right. Whatever you have come in here to say, say it."

---

"I have got a page and a half in a book," said Elias Venn, "in my own hand, in a shop, and nobody has ever read it except a sergeant's first three pages on two occasions when she was in the shop and I was not out. It is the only description of the working of the thirtieth of October that anybody has written down, and there is nowhere in this city to file it, because I am not an officer and the file is not mine. It says where the object came from and when and how long and who took the figure, and what I can state, and the four things I cannot. I am going to read it out, and I am aware that I am reading it into a room for the first time, and that after this it is in a room and not in a shop."

He read it out. It took four minutes and it was nine-tenths of the things he already knew and the whole of the things he could not prove, and the officer on the right stopped writing halfway through and wrote the rest of it from memory, which he did not mention, and which was the single kindest thing that happened to him in December.

"And there is one thing in it that is not in it," said Elias Venn, "and it is a small thing and it is the only thing I have that anybody in this room can put in a pocket.

"Alfred Neve is seventy-eight. On the twenty-fourth of October at ten past two in the afternoon a licensed brass threshold bracket, with a collar number stamped into it, was fitted by a fitter into a socket cut into the shaft of his own walking stick, in his own kitchen, nine years earlier. That is a real four-act working, bound to a man's own object, addressed to his own front step, held, and shut off, and every act in it is his.

"And on the thirtieth of October, at 14.44, a man came into that treatment room, and inside the riveted ferrule there was bonded over the honest working a sleeve of four inches of channel section carrying a release that was not the patient's, addressed to a record and not to a body, and made for one stick at a time, **with no maker's pair on it at all, because there was nothing to grade it against.**"

He put his hands on the table.

"There is no category for that in this form. I am not asking you to invent one, because I have spent four months on a public wall and I know what happens when an office invents one at a member of the public's request. I am telling three officers the shape of a thing and then I am sitting down."

---

"What is it," said the officer on the left.

"A fabricated release entered over a real working, and the real working's acts are the patient's, and the certificate issued on the first of November says in three parts out of four that a man of seventy-eight performed a complete dangerous working."

"You are describing a fourth act."

"I am not," said Elias Venn. "I have not got a word for what I am describing and I have looked, and there is a category on a public form for a release entered where a working has none of its own, and the patient's working has three of its own and the fourth is the one that is not his, and that is not the same as a working with no release in it, and if I call it the other thing then I am the only person anybody can name who has called it that, and I still could not tell you who does it."

The chair wrote for a while and then put the pen down. "That is going in the minutes as *described, not characterised*, and somebody will read it in about two years and want to know why you would not use a word, and you will have to answer that then and not now."

"Understood."

"One more thing," said the chair, "and then I have to say the finding again and you have to stand up while I do it."

"What is it."

"You have been in five offices in eleven days and you have come in here with nothing." He put the file down. "**Say the thing that is in front of you. Not the paperwork. You have been doing the paperwork beautifully for twenty minutes. Say the other one.**"

So Elias Venn said the other one, in order, in the flat voice he had used on a stool in a room above a supermarket in Kettle Rows on the twenty-sixth of November, so that somebody else could do the arithmetic.

"In this city a rehabilitation claim is made by an account and not by a patient, and a certificate for a treatment goes to the payer and not to the patient, and a patient is handed a copy of the claim and not of the certificate. I have that in my own hand in a book and I am not going to read the book out in this room.

"There is a certificate. It was issued in the ordinary way in October by a district office of a public registry, by a registrar-counsel who has never examined a patient. It records a bed, a service code and a fee. It does not record a person. It is a true document and every part of it is true.

"The bed it records is a bed in a ward that was shut for eight days with the water off, and its six residents were in a day room off a corridor, which is not a ward and has no number. The treatment was real, and it was done in an hour, in a first-floor room over a shop, by a practitioner who is fifty-eight and has been doing it for six years, and the fee on the claim is the published fee and the fee she charged is a different number, and the difference is the whole of the reason a claim could exist.

"And there is a woman of eighty-one who was one of the six, and who went out for that hour on that Tuesday, and who paid sixty credits, and who does not remember what was done to her, and who is not named anywhere and has never asked for anything.

"Four offices hold one half of that each. A ward book. A claim file. A payer's assessment. A record of issue. **Not one of the four can telephone any of the other three, and no rule in this city says they may.** And the distance from a bed to a paid claim is four hours and a rubber stamp.

"So the claim was signed by a person, and the certificate was issued by an office, and the treatment was received by nobody who remembers it, and every document in this city that touches any of it is genuine, and I have been at this since the seventeenth of September, and **I cannot tell you which of those three people is the wrong one, and I have been in all four rooms, and I was sent away from two of them by people who were correct.**"

The clock on the wall said five to eleven and it was two minutes fast.

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds.

"Mr Venn," said Chief Inspector Lusk, and he took his chin off the end of the pen, "I have sat in about four hundred of these and nobody has ever stood up in one and told me that they did not know. I have been told *not my area*. I have been told *I have no recollection*. Once, memorably, I have been told *is that the man who did the thing with the dustbins.* This is the first one, and I am not going to be clever about it.

"**I do not know.** And the department is going to tell you that this is not a matter for a district and not a matter for a claims office and not a matter for a registry, and every one of those will be true, and this room will go on doing its Wednesday. But it is now minuted in your words, and it is in a building, and I have never known a thing to be worthless in about four years, and I cannot tell you that I will do anything about it, and I am not going to pretend that is the same as promising."

He read the finding again and upheld it, and the variation was as he had said it, and the minute was signed by three people at five past eleven.

---

Nadia Kest was in the corridor outside with a cup of coffee from the machine and her coat over her arm.

"Nine minutes forty of it."

"Of eleven."

"You used nine minutes forty of eleven and you spent the last of it telling Dermot Lusk that he does not know, and I have watched eleven panels in rooms like that one and I have never seen anybody spend one like that." She drank the coffee. "I am not going to say the thing I said in a car park."

"I have not asked you to."

"I know you have not, which is why I am going to say that I have changed my mind about about a third of it and I am not going to say which third, and that you will find out at the wrong moment." She put the cup on the sill. "What is Sunday?"

"Sunday?"

"You have had a face since Friday and it is not the face of a man who has lost a finding. What is on Sunday?"

Elias Venn stood in a corridor outside a first-floor room on Bell Street with a minute in a folder and a date in a notebook four days away that he had written down on the twenty-sixth of November because a man who will not write the date down is a man who will decide later that it did not happen.

"Nothing," he said. "Sunday is nothing."

"Right," said Nadia Kest, and picked up her coat, and went past him, and stopped, and said one thing over her shoulder that was not about the case and that he did not write down: "Then sleep on it."

He did not tell her. That was the price of it, and it was four days to Sunday the sixteenth, and the conversation had not happened, and he was not going to be the man who tells an inspector in a corridor that he has already had an argument he has not had yet, and the reason he was not going to was not pride and not the case, it was that if he said it out loud then it would be a thing between two people instead of a thing in a book, and he did not know which of those two was worse and he had four days to work it out.

---

He got to Wexford Row at twenty past three and the visitors' room was empty and the jigsaw was on the table with the tin beside it, shut.

Petronella Osei came in with a card. "You are on the list. Sister Warhurst said if you came she would see you, and she has gone home, and she is not to be on the book until the review on the nineteenth, which is a Wednesday, and she is not the kind of person who is furious quietly."

"Is she all right?"

"She is a ward sister of twenty-six years and a member of the public asked her to look at a ward book and she said yes, and now there is a review and it is going to be on a form, and she has four photographs in a drawer that she took herself and nobody is going to be told about them." Petronella put the card down. "And that is not the worst of what happened this week. Go and sit in the day room for a bit before you go home."

He went and sat in it.

Doris Sowerby was at the table by the window with the blanket over her knees and the tin open in front of her, and there were four pieces in it.

"Four," she said. "Two out of a coat sleeve in the corridor and one out of a saucer on a trolley in the canteen, which is disgusting. And the fourth was on this table under a newspaper where somebody had been sitting all afternoon, and it has been there since Friday, and it is the fourth from the left on the blue sky, and it has got a straight edge on the long side, and it is not a boat and it is not a shadow." She put the last piece in and pushed the tin away from her. "It is a doctor. Two of them. In a room with a window. And I have been putting that jigsaw together since the eighth of October and there is nobody in this building who can tell me what is in the picture, and the sister has got the box in her bedroom and she is at the review on Wednesday and I am not going to burden her."

Elias Venn sat down on the other side of the table.

"Sister Warhurst has been taken off the ward book," he said. "Until the nineteenth. It is on a form."

Doris Sowerby looked at him.

"Because she let a stranger look, love. That's all it is. I have been on that wing twice in my life and both times there has been somebody in an office asking about a bed, and the sister is not a fool and she is not a saint, and she let a stranger look at a book with people's names in it, and she did it anyway, and she is the only person in this building who has ever done one single thing for me that was not about my hip." She straightened the tin. "And I asked her about it at lunchtime and she told me and she was crying, and I have not seen a nurse cry before in my life, and I am eighty-one and I am going to be able to say that I saw it."

Then she looked at him properly for the first time, and the day room door was open and the man with the cup was writing something and the television was on with the sound off.

"Now then. Who are you, and don't tell me you're anybody's husband, because you have not got that face. You've got the face of the man who was on the chair in the corridor on Wednesday, and the physiotherapist's told everybody about it and it's all round this building by Friday, so you'll be told."

"I am not a doctor and I am not a police officer," said Elias Venn. "I have spent four months trying to find out who printed a third box on a sheet of paper. I have been in five offices and sent away from two of them by people who were correct, and on Monday a woman in this building told me the whole thing takes four hours and a rubber stamp, and I have got nothing anybody can use, and I am the only person in the city who knows it, and I have not got one single person I can give it to."

"Good heavens," said Doris Sowerby. "Sit down."

"I am sitting down."

"You are not, you are perching." She got up on the arm of the chair, which is not a thing anybody should do at eighty-one, and went to the urn at the end of the table and lifted the lid and looked in and put the lid back down. "The tea has gone. But there's the urn, and there's a cup in the cupboard behind you with somebody's writing on the bottom of it, and it is not going to be clean and I am eighty-one and I am perfectly able to make a cup of tea, and you can have it with milk or without and I do not recommend either."

He got the cup. It had somebody's name on the bottom of it in biro and it was not clean, and it was the first thing anybody in the city had offered him in eleven days that was not a document, and he sat down at the table by the window with it in both hands, and the man of forty turned his magazine over, and on the table there was a tin with a lid on it and inside it a picture of two people in a room with a window, done to the last piece, and somebody had found the box.
