## Volume 01, batch 0001 — Arc A, "The Missing Release" (Chapters 1–10)

Cards: `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md`. The volume is five arcs of ten chapters; this is the first arc and it owns escalation beats 1–4 and opens beat 5.

1. **Ch 1 — the door that opened twice.** Monday 17 September, 01:52. Elias comes home to his own building and finds Tamsin Ro's rented room open two floors above him. The physical deadbolt is unfastened and the door ward has failed in a way that makes the lock and the magical threshold disagree. His first unplanned reading on the jamb shows a working that begins *inside* the room, binds to the desk, addresses a door, turns, and then closes with a release Tamsin never performed. He stops the first responders from moving the door, tells them not to touch the frame, and calls Nadia instead of trusting the shape he recognises from a drawer in his own flat. He recognises the shape; he does not open the file. A man stands in the rain across the street counting the windows of the building and photographs nothing but is seen. **Cost:** four minutes and his balance.
2. **Ch 2 — two residue orders in the threshold.** Monday morning. The physical scene says fight-then-stop; the residue says the working began after the door opened; the witnesses give incompatible times that cannot both be residue-compatible. Concord Ward Works inspector **Wren Abasi** arrives wanting the room sealed, asks in writing whether the assigned detective lives at the address, and gets a signed, dated, self-incriminating answer. Elias notices that his own phrase from the Morrow Street file is a quote and admits he holds a document he has not produced. Mara Quill arrives with a salt-glass plate and an independent witness log and lifts the threshold residue in front of Elias and Abasi, initialling the corner in her own name, before the room can be sealed. He considers a second reading on the unstable threshold and refuses it, standing at the top of the stairs instead, because a plate with a policeman's handprint in its history is a plate a defence removes in four months. **Cost:** the chance to solve it fast, and the first thing he ever wrote down about himself that he did not show anybody. **End:** Mara sees a thin seam in the residue matching neither the victim nor the door, and takes the plate to her laboratory.
3. **Ch 3 — the Margin named and logged.** Monday 17 September, afternoon, Sequence Desk temporary evidence room. Nadia makes Elias enter a residency declaration in the case file before anything else happens. He describes his faculty honestly — not a vision of people but a short perception of the order in which residue changed — and has Nadia cut and reseal the swab on camera, and then takes a limited, logged, timed reading of swab 14 from the desk edge. It shows the desk is the anchor and the address is a person, and it produces the batch's first margin note: *a release follows the turn; a second trace begins before it.* He decides not to force a living-trace reading and waits for Mara and the witness to set the next boundary. **Cost:** the left side of his head, permanently this time, and a block of the afternoon he rebuilds from his own phone's call log — the one record of that afternoon that does not depend on his memory. **End:** the only living trace left belongs to the intended target, who is refusing to speak to police.
4. **Ch 4 — the witness who was there.** Monday 17 September, evening, Saint Orra Reach, family-observation room, with Mara and **Niko Senn**, 24, overnight laundry worker, who came voluntarily and will not speak to police. His account puts the mug before the door's click, which does not fit the room. Mara stops Elias from asking a leading question. Niko describes a paid "grounding" course three or four weeks earlier — a hand flat on a table, wait for a tap, breathe out, say her name — and a brass tool in a smock man's hand with a maker's mark stamped on the barrel. Elias stops the interview, offers Niko the choice to speak unrecorded, and records that a witness-binding was offered by the Registry and refused by the witness in his own words. Elias does not read his living trace. **End:** the number is JA-4417.
5. **Ch 5 — the small repair.** Tuesday 18 September, one whole day, three shops and a hardware archive. The repair number runs through three shops' transfer ledgers — the Kettle Rows Tool Registry, a two-bench tool works off Cutter Street, and an engraver on Ash Street who keeps his own copy of everything he cuts — and then Jules Ardent's bench book. Bess Yonkin gives him the ledger page and refuses the dies. Jules admits he built six clamps, that JA-4417 went out on the seventeenth to a man with a rehabilitation clinic card, and that a clamp holds a circuit open — so if something has been cut to match his jaw, they used his *drawing*, not his tool. Elias follows the paper, feels clean satisfaction, and then recognises that the satisfaction came from using a man as a source. He gets the clinic's intake filing copy legally and slowly at a counter, in a corridor, in front of a receptionist, and signs the visitor book in her ink with his own name on it, because in nine months nobody had written anything down in his own building. Jules agrees to hand the clamp over at four the next morning, documented rather than seized. **End:** a licensed rehabilitation clinic on Verge Row whose ninety-one intake entries contain no account, no patient matching the card, and one printed signature.
6. **Ch 6 — what Elias touched.** Wednesday 19 September, from four in the morning into the evening, Mara's residue laboratory. The clamp is on loan and reverts to rehabilitation account 44-9 at midnight, so the comparison is a deadline. The custody line needs a named police officer; Nadia is two wards up holding night-market porter **Rafi Ilori**, who saw somebody go up the stair and will not stop talking. Elias is alone on the bench, which is the one thing Mara told him not to be. A Registry preservation order lets an inspector treat the dispensary's stock as ordinary maintenance by morning, so the clinic's stockroom is about to disappear into paperwork. He touches the channel unlogged at 20:52, two minutes after she dated a note saying that channel was empty, and gets a third reading: the same release, failing at the same joint, in brass, with a living man's name in it. Mara refuses to use the contaminated reading and states, in order, that the channel is finished and the reason is a person. He sits on a step outside the linen office for two hours, keeps nothing down, and by half past eleven the clamp is gone from the bench in a courier's box. **End:** Niko Senn is booked into the Verge Row clinic through the account as having received a treatment he does not remember, and a transfer notice is initialled at the counter.
7. **Ch 7 — the mechanic's hands.** Thursday 20 September, Nadia's POV, from Rafi Ilori's overnight account to a transit maintenance shed to the back table of Elias's own shop. Rafi adds that the man was checking the buses, not lost. Jules will not say where Niko is until Nadia explains what a notice on a clinic door would do. He then gives it up: he has moved Niko to the Kettle Rows Community Clinic, the one address the account cannot book a patient into, and he explains that the account reaches people by merging files, not by walking through doors. He returns a canvas roll of tools, admits six years of unregistered repairs under a false clinic account, and asks her to record it. Nadia retains Elias on the case on one condition, made in front of Jules: he does not work alone. Elias apologises for using Jules as evidence first. **End:** the frame design was commissioned through a clinic account with no named patient, in a sealed envelope with a maker's stamp pair, and Niko's transfer order is issued at 06:00 Friday.
8. **Ch 8 — the clinic that will not close.** Friday 21 September, morning, Kettle Rows Community Clinic. Sana Okafor will not let him search, will not let him near her patients, and makes him say out loud what he wants from her before she gives it. He stays in the corridor and hears what a Kettle Rows tenant says about the man who lives under the woman he killed. The Wednesday night "maintenance" man came at 21:10 in a Ward Works vest and signed her book, and he was tall-ish and neat. A registrar brings the transfer order; Elias reads the four-act sequence box standing back with his hands visible and does not touch it, and realises Niko Senn is not in the draft at all. Dr Okafor files a clinical hold that stops the transfer without a raid, and puts a copy of the order on the waiting-room board. She tells him plainly that a case he keeps is a boy who does not exist outside a building. **End:** the Proof Registry wants a countersignature before the hearing, and Mara wants him in a laboratory at one o'clock to look at three pieces of paper and not one object.
9. **Ch 9 — the missing release.** Friday 21 September, afternoon into evening, Saint Orra Reach laboratory, figures only. Mara shows that the fifth thing on the plate is release-shaped with **no settling curve**, which means it was not made in that room; the same phase signature appears in the four-act box on Niko's transfer order; the two together make a **template**. She and Elias disagree in public about what he saw on Monday — he read a release performed, she read something delivered — and neither of them can yet tell the difference, which is the ceiling of what First Margin knows. The four acts turn out to be a category on a Concord form: *an act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own*, which is the phrase in his own twelve-year-old Morrow Street file, which he is not permitted to open. He puts a hand flat on the board in the middle of the argument and Mara stops him and writes the breach down, because the same hand went into a channel on Wednesday. He then writes a **Disagreement of the Assigned Detective** with four numbered items, refuses to countersign at 16:41, and signs the plate's sleeve — never the glass — beside Mara's initial. He tells Registrar-Counsel Bettina Haile that he wants to know which document the Registry intends to correct. **End:** the hearing-pack document request is due to be answered tomorrow morning, so the crew cannot come in daylight, and the Registry's own timetable has just told them the exact night to be in that stockroom instead — which is tonight.
10. **Ch 10 — the room behind the dispensary.** Friday 21 September night into Saturday 22 September, the clinic corridor, from half past nine. An hour and a half of almost nothing: a night porter tapping a wall that has had a tick in it for three weeks, Mara logging a sealed room in ten-minute intervals, a sheet of paper nobody has moved, and a counter ward light going out for half a second four times, the last one longer.
Ahmed the night porter points out the sheet of paper the maintenance man left on the corridor bench on Wednesday night, which is the anchor: a second transfer order, never sleeved, stamped issued at 16:51, lying face up under a tin of surgical staples since 21:10 on Wednesday. Mara sets her plate flat against the closed steel door from the corridor and logs the same defect through it — two breaks in the channel, smaller than the clamp's, in better metal. Then Elias takes the fourth reading: the anchor is that sheet, the address is one person in a bed upstairs, and the release is already seated in a channel with no curve on it. He says the order out loud, which is the only thing he can do, and Jules turns it into two brass saddle clips on a wall.
He cuts the latch plate, gets his shoulder in, and levers the nine inches off the brick himself with a length of copper from the cold room while the operator stays on the step ladder and twice tells him he will want to think about it. The operator leaves through the yard and over the paint-works wall after photographing Elias on the paint bins. The working stops because nine inches of galvanised steel came off the brick in his own hands, not because Elias beat anybody and not because he interrupted anything — and the carrier stays in the room, the operator's hands holding nothing but a caliper and a phone. Nadia logs an unregistered ward object, cause undetermined, and signs the halves and the foil stencil over to Mara as a transfer. Mara lifts the second salt-glass plate on a folding table on the pavement outside the paint works at 01:40 on Saturday and measures the same failure at a smaller scale with a cleaner hand, and a third and cleanest maker's pair, graded like a production run. The saddle clips are not on the plate; Jules takes them down in the small hours and reuses them on a new length of conduit.
Nine minutes of ward protection gone — the wards come back on their own at 00:03, every circuit except the dispensary's, which is still hanging off a wall with no conduit on it until Jules re-hangs one at four. Elias sits on the kerb from 02:20 for forty minutes while three residents, one at a time, tell him what the dark was like. **End:** at four on Saturday he goes back in and photographs the open Wednesday page of the dispensary's own maintenance book together with the sheet of paper in a single frame taken from the stockroom doorway, having picked nothing up and carried nothing out of the room, and the crew did not make the frame at all — somebody with better metal was told where to put it. The stencil is now evidence in a laboratory two wards from a hospital, the Registry reissued for 06:00 Monday, and a man on somebody's wall has a photograph of Elias's face.
**Batch close:** two salt-glass plates in Mara Quill's name — the Chapter 2 threshold plate carrying the coarse seam, and the Chapter 10 plate carrying the graded third maker's pair, a second finer seam, both conduit halves and the abandoned foil stencil — a refused countersignature, a recorded disagreement, a delayed and then reissued transfer order, an open hearing on Monday 24 September, a clamp back in the outer wards with only eleven figures surviving it, and the question of who is teaching the network to make frames it cannot currently make.

## Volume 01, batch 0002 — Arc B, "The Ward Under the Clinic" (Chapters 11–20)

Cards: `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0002.md`. Arc B owns escalation beats 5, 6 and 7. It contains **no reading, no margin panel and no nosebleed**; what it takes is Elias's access.

11. **Ch 11 — until Tuesday.** Saturday 22 September, from twenty to seven. He cannot account for Friday except through other people's records of it. At 07:16 a number he has never had sends him a photograph of himself standing on paint bins with blood on his face, and a screenshot of a 400-member residents' group that got it at 04:52 and had written forty-one replies under it by seven, among them *he let her in* and, at 06:58, a stranger's observation that the ward light over the dispensary counter came back at four and the light on Kettle Rows street did not. He writes what Friday night actually cost the building into the Sequence Desk day book in four timed lines, including a fifth about the second-landing stair light whose ballast he replaced himself in the spring and which nobody has ever signed off. Ahmed walks him down the corridor so that he is not the first one through with a camera, tells him he has decided the seventy-one-year-old night clerk is not a villain and wants that written down, and tells him the fire officer's honest answer is Tuesday ten o'clock. Jules has used the two brass saddle clips on a new length of conduit and has written down every tick in the building with three copies in the city, one of which is not his. Mara puts eggs in front of him at ten without discussing it. **Niko Senn** has been marked *discharge* by account 44-9 at six that morning, which fixes him and loses him, and a lad in the corridor has told him there is another one, in Halloway, the woman who had the flicker, and Niko did not ask a single question about her. He hands the photograph to Nadia as a recorded item at twenty to one and says he has not traced the sender and is not asking anybody to.
12. **Ch 12 — the slip in the tray.** Nadia's Saturday. In a room above a licensing office on Vine Street at eight, **Magistrate Hadwin Slade** grants her a *conditional* entry: the endorsement at 23:41, the entry at 23:54, thirteen minutes, no application filed, no finding either way, and his own name on the note. He says he will hear her Carrow Lane application on Monday and will object if she brings it at nine. She then discovers the Deputy Chief's routing slip has acquired a typed annex asking three things — the form category, the box number, and the office that completed the box — and writes four hundred words: items one and two refusable because a form category is a printer's convention, item four the true excuse, item five the true sentence, which refers the file to the Tribunal archive for a full sequence position at the Deputy Chief's authority, due **Friday the fifth of October**. Orr's office accepts and the archive will be opened by a clerk, **without him and without her**, on paper. She initialled the annex *received and referred* and did not send it, and she admits to herself in a car that item four is not an excuse. She discloses the countersigned slip, annex and reply to Elias at four o'clock **in front of a witness**, because a man told on Saturday and read on Sunday has had a whole weekend to be angry about it.
13. **Ch 13 — the whole book.** Sunday 23 September. **Halvard Reisz** comes up his own stairs with a district re-assessment: the block's score has gone from 61 in June to 49, the band from *elevated* to *standard*, and the ward maintenance contract for the block was re-tendered on the June score in August and has been running at forty per cent under since midnight — while the one thing he complained about, a landing light late by a second and a half, has got worse. Elias cannot tell him, because his answer would rest on a faculty that is not evidence, and Reisz goes back down his stairs. At the clinic the consultation form demands a number in public; Sana Okafor writes **a hundred**, and Elias does not correct her, because a hundred is the number that stops a van turning into that yard and he has no standing whatever in the number of people in her waiting room. She refuses him one page of the maintenance book and gives him all forty-one months of it, copied and pinned to her own waiting-room board, so that no page can be lifted out later; the price is the clinic's own failures, published in its own ink, in front of people who can read them for two days. The Wednesday page is photographed again from the stockroom doorway with Ahmed as a second witness, and on it are a name and a vest number: *D. MEARS. W-4471. Check the line.* Both check out — a live vest, a real man, sixty-one, twenty-two years in the trade, resigned in June. A Registry hearing pack arrives by hand for the clinic to answer, and item four of its production schedule asks for the maintenance book complete, **including all pages bearing entries by persons not employed by the clinic** — so somebody already knows the book is there and knows which pages they want.
14. **Ch 14 — Monday the twenty-fourth.** Morrow Hill, Room Two, a Registry *preliminary attestation* and not a hearing of a dangerous working. Bettina Haile reads two and a half well-made pages in which every sentence is true and every sentence is a reason not to look at anything else. Elias gets the Registry's own systems clerk to confirm, on the record, from the screen, that certificate C-441-KR-04 was issued at 16:10 on Wednesday 19 September, that the transfer order came out of the outbox at 16:51, and that the file was opened at 09:20 on Tuesday the 18th. **Nobody disputes the times.** Wren Abasi explains that certificates are generated against the request and not against the print, and that there is a note about it in the manual, and that the note in the manual is a stronger document than a witness in a room. Elias asks for two things. Haile grants the first — the four-item Disagreement attached to the attestation in full as **Schedule 1** — and denies the second, on the correct ground that recording a named man in a document he is not a party to and cannot contest would convert a witness into a subject, and tells him the young man is recorded better in a clinical hold a physician filed that this Registry cannot overwrite. The attestation issues on the Registry's own authority at 10:20, unsigned by him, closing nothing. Nadia affirms on the record, with her name, that she retained an assigned detective she knew lived at the scene. In the lobby Haile warns him that a schedule is appealable, and that the forty-one minutes are in the file, and that she signed the certificate with her eyes open.
15. **Ch 15 — Halloway Street.** Tuesday 25 September. A new consequence of the reissue: account 44-9 marked **Niko Senn** *discharge* at 06:00 on Monday for non-attendance, the file closed, and he is now nobody's patient and unfindable. In a cold room four streets from the market, **Mavis Brannigan**, 57, fish-market stallholder, is on the floor with a chest freezer open and the roof unit running and four degrees of cold. Her daughter **Kirsten Brannigan**, 29, tells them unasked that she bought a *ward continuity unit* in August for 340 credits on the account, found through a card in the letting file, and that the man came at night and gave her a sheet with a diagram and a tick box and told her the tick box was for the warranty survey. A hardback appointment diary holds fifty-three entries since the second week of July, nine of them ending *resolved*, eleven reports in six weeks. On the office desk, ordinary lease paper nobody reads: a **schedule of ward fittings with a four-act sequence box** where the printed instruction describes three. Elias counts four from arm's length with his hands visible and says out loud that all he has brought to this desk is the ability to count four. Mara Quill tells Kirsten Brannigan, before anyone else can, that **no instrument in this city has found anything unusual in the room over the deceased** — that it is true, that it will be true in a year, and that the only thing standing between that sentence and this case is a piece of paper with her figures on it. She takes the lift at 11:35 with the daughter present, in the daughter's name, describing every step, and the daughter signs the sleeve. The Registry's information office circulates a draft line at 14:00, ten hours after the body was found: *a refrigeration fault at a commercial premises in Halloway Street.*
16. **Ch 16 — five documents.** Wednesday 26 September, and no crime anywhere in it. **Desmond Bramwell**, 71, of Bramwell & Sons, will not produce the die ledger without a case number, and volunteers two things nobody asked for: the acceptance-pair profile was cut twice in August, once to a licensed maker on a Kettle Rows address and once to a left-hand post box under a trading name, and the second die carried no maker's initials. A Cutter Street merchant produces three cash invoices to a sole trader trading as **Halloran Supply** — forty sheets of four-tenth foil, sixty lengths of galvanised conduit, one gross of channel section — collected by a courier. At the **Tanager Street works depot** the municipal ward fault log is a plastic box: 106 slips in the quarter, 81 resolved, 77 same-day, 68 with an attendance code, four codes, no names, the approved list in an office, and a woman from the municipal contracts office telephoned on Wednesday asking for the distribution of codes by post code and whether the mapping is unchanged since the April revision. Nadia gets the ward continuity schedules out in public: **44-9 is in the book of premises that are not covered**, and the district block's premium has fallen eleven points while its reported faults have fallen sixty per cent, which is a district made cheap on purpose. At 17:52 on Halloway Street the door schedule is read standing back, photographed in place, original in the client's drawer — and its five printed lines end *Confirm the patient is in the room.*
17. **Ch 17 — what she will not certify.** Mara Quill's Thursday. The Concord letter has already told her that no instrument in the city has found anything unusual in the room over the deceased, and the assessor **Priyanka Lyle** is not cruel: she asks Mara to see where the office is about to stand. Mara certifies the **measurements** — four figures off the threshold plate, the phase signature, the absence of a settling curve, the two break measurements, every log number — and refuses to certify that the act was copied, that anybody had a motive, or to put anything on a Concord register. **Roy Prentice** then asks for the foil stencil, reasonably, on the ground that it is the only physical object linking the first two frames to one production method; the only registered facility in reach is the third floor of the building they are sitting in. She refuses, gives her reason — the moment the object is in a register every figure she has produced becomes a figure taken out of a registered object — and asks for the refusal to be minuted word for word, *because a refusal that nobody wrote down did not happen.* She also hands over the note of Wednesday the 19th, which is the most damaging document in the file and is in her handwriting, and says she was not going to soften it and is not. On a bench in a corridor she tells Elias that she would put the plates on a register this afternoon if he were not the assigned detective; that he is angrier about the note than she is; and then she says her brother's name — **Iven Quill**, six years and eleven days — and attaches no lead to it. She refuses him the laboratory and room two, and ends on the thing that matters: a refusal and a request are the same piece of paper with two names on it, so her refusal is now a reason to want the stencil.
18. **Ch 18 — the supplier.** Friday 28 September, Concord Hall, ground floor, the public register of licensed ward practitioners, a queue and a woman at the counter. **Orin Sable**, registered 4 March 2019, class **supply**, premises 2nd floor Carrow Lane above a printer's, renewed this March, with one endorsement for supply of ward components within a licensed premise and one **restriction** from carrying out any dangerous working, any working affecting a person, and any attendance at a scene where a working is in progress — the restriction being in the original registration and therefore not an investigation. A man of about forty in a very good coat stands behind him in the queue and then goes out at a speed that is not fast. Elias does not follow him, and understands why, and writes four words and nothing else: **SUPPLIER OF FRAMES**. He cannot put the nine inches on an invoice for conduit; what he has is a profile, a trading name, a class of licence and a fortnight. He tells Nadia to write the limitation in his own hand, and that a man in a good coat now knows his name, and that the man in the good coat walking out unhurried is the *only* reason he did not follow. Then the cost of Wednesday: Bess Yonkin telephoned the desk, which she has never done in nineteen years, because **Jules Ardent walked in at 09:45 with his die block in a bag and asked her to seize it**, standing at her own counter and reading the seizure form aloud twice. A maker's enquiry opened. A standard clinical practice notice went on the door of 9 Pell Street at about two o'clock. The uncollected second set is now inside that enquiry and cannot be moved by anybody, including him. In a solicitor's rooms above a tobacconist's the solicitor establishes that a seized die and an unregistered plate can be compared in about twenty minutes, in the one room in the city that can hold both — the room Mara refused on Thursday. Jules offers to open his bench room. **Elias says no**, and records that both refusals are in the chain and that they are now both worse off. At 16:40 a Concord assessor in a Ward Works coat stands four feet from the clinic's board with a finger on a page of the maintenance book, reading, alone, with no questions, because everything she wants is on a wall in the clinic's own ink.
19. **Ch 19 — the unknotted gap.** Saturday 29 September. He reads a footer: the registrar's transfer order on the clinic's board is **Form R-2 (rev. 4), sequence box SEQ 4(c)**, and he did not read the footer on Friday because he was counting. The concordance of sequence-box codes costs four credits at the Morrow Hill forms counter, and the preface says the categories have not been renumbered since 2006, so a 4(c) in 2017 and a 4(c) this morning are the same category in the same words from the same office. **4 (c):** *an act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own* — and then the part nobody reads: entry does not assert that a release was performed, it asserts the record is to be treated as though one was, **for the purposes of this record only**. And **4 (d)** — *a sequence with no recorded release, closed at the discretion of the certifying office* — withdrawn from standard forms in 2011 and retained for indexing pre-2011 records only, which is his sentence: *no release was recorded at the threshold.* Twelve years of carrying a sentence that was an indexing instruction. But the only document that says what is in the box on the Morrow Street report is the report, in an archive, behind a form whose field eleven cannot be left blank. He writes a nine-item note — item 6 the prohibition, item 9 that he is not requesting the certificate either — signs it, and puts *this is a note, it is not a request, do not process it as one* along the bottom. Nadia files it as a note in front of him, and then tells him, in a car, that she wrote the four hundred words on Saturday morning and **deliberately left out the line**, and that the correct response to that is to be extremely angry with her, because she would rather be argued with than admired.
20. **Ch 20 — a line she would not write.** Sunday 30 September. In the car she gives him the real reason: the moment that phrase is in a departmental position the old matter is live, and a live matter makes 441-KR a matter with a connected record, and a detective with a connected record is a man whose *refusal of a countersignature* is now a fact to be attached to a young man's address in every report anybody writes. Then the form. An application for access to a certified record is one side and costs nothing and about four hundred people fill one in a year, and field eleven is headed *other matters to which the applicant is or has been connected, in which the record is or may be relevant*, and it must be completed, and a request without it comes back to whoever returned it. So he fills it in, in his own hand, in the order in which it will be used against him, and gives the full disposition of 441-KR and states that the answer to his own question may be no. It is lodged by hand at about five, logged, with the time on it, and it is not yet served. The review board sat on Thursday the 27th and was **adjourned to Thursday the 4th of October** because the clinic's ninety-odd pages of production were served at four o'clock in the afternoon for a board that sat at ten that morning — which is the Concord's correct answer to Dr Okafor's correct act. He asks for the record to be served after the board. Then, in the clinic corridor, **Niko Senn** — out of room two, waiting for the 17:15 bus, nine days of fear going out of him in ninety seconds — puts a card in his hand: three weeks in a wallet, the smock man's name written on the back in the room. **Elias refuses it**, because in four months it would have his prints and a faint and would make Niko Senn a man who gave a card to the police; and when the boy starts to say the name anyway, he refuses that too, and says why, and it is not noble: the day he has a name he has an expectation, and he has done that to a mechanic and to a woman with a plate in a hard case and he is not doing it to a twenty-four-year-old in a corridor. The card stays in the pocket. At the gate the boy shouts that if he ever sees the man in the smock he will tell Elias first, and goes up Marshall Street to a bus.
**Batch close:** a supplier's name off a public register and a limitation in the assigned detective's own hand; a preliminary attestation that exists with his four-item disagreement bound to the back of it; a second death carrying the same fourth act in a different medium; a district made cheap on purpose and a ward contract cut forty per cent; a stencil refused onto a register on the page; a mechanic's die block surrendered without a word to anybody; a shelf number that has been a man's name for twelve years; and an application lodged in a car park on a Sunday that takes the case away from him, and will be served after Thursday the fourth of October.
