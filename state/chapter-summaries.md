## Volume 01, batch 0001 — Arc A, "The Missing Release" (Chapters 1–10)

Cards: `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md`. The volume is five arcs of ten chapters; this is the first arc and it owns escalation beats 1–4 and opens beat 5.

1. **Ch 1 — the door that opened twice.** Monday, 00:52. Elias comes home to his own building and finds Tamsin Ro's rented room open two floors above him. The physical deadbolt is unfastened and the door ward has failed in a way that makes the lock and the magical threshold disagree. His first unplanned reading on the jamb shows a working that begins *inside* the room, binds to the desk, addresses a door, turns, and then closes with a release Tamsin never performed. He stops the first responders from moving the door, photographs the room before touching the frame, and calls Nadia instead of trusting the shape he recognises from a drawer in his own flat. He recognises the shape; he does not open the file. A man stands in the rain across the street counting the windows of the building and photographs nothing but is seen. **Cost:** four minutes and his balance.
2. **Ch 2 — two residue orders in the threshold.** Monday morning. The physical scene says fight-then-stop; the residue says the working began after the door opened; the witnesses give incompatible times that cannot both be residue-compatible. Concord Ward Works inspector **Wren Abasi** arrives wanting the room sealed, asks in writing whether the assigned detective lives at the address, and gets a signed, dated, self-incriminating answer. Elias notices that his own phrase from the Morrow Street file is a quote and admits he holds a document he has not produced. Mara Quill arrives with a salt-glass plate and an independent witness log and lifts the threshold residue in front of Elias and Abasi, initialling the corner in her own name, before the room can be sealed. He chooses **not** to take a second reading while the room is unstable. **Cost:** the chance to solve it fast. **End:** Mara sees a thin seam in the residue matching neither the victim nor the door, and takes the plate to her laboratory.
3. **Ch 3 — the Margin named and logged.** Monday late morning, Sequence Desk temporary evidence room. Nadia makes Elias enter a residency declaration in the case file before anything else happens. He describes his faculty honestly — not a vision of people but a short perception of the order in which residue changed — and takes a limited, logged, timed reading through plastic on sealed swab 14 from the desk edge. It shows the desk is the anchor and the address is a person, and it produces the batch's first margin note: *a release follows the turn; a second trace begins before it.* He decides not to force a living-trace reading and waits for Mara and the witness to set the next boundary. **Cost:** the left ear, and a block of the afternoon he rebuilds from a phone log. **End:** the only living trace left belongs to the intended target, who is refusing to speak to police.
4. **Ch 4 — the witness who was there.** Monday afternoon, Saint Orra Reach, family-observation room, with Mara and **Niko Senn**, 24, overnight laundry worker, who came voluntarily and will not speak to police. His account puts the mug before the door's click, which does not fit the room. Mara stops Elias from asking a leading question. Niko describes a paid "grounding" course three or four weeks earlier — a hand flat on a table, wait for a tap, breathe out, say her name — and a brass tool in a smock man's hand with a maker's mark stamped on the barrel. Elias stops the interview, offers Niko the choice to speak unrecorded, and records that a witness-binding was offered by the Registry and refused by the witness in his own words. Elias does not read his living trace. **End:** the number is JA-4417.
5. **Ch 5 — the small repair.** Tuesday afternoon, three shops and a hardware archive. The repair number runs through the Kettle Rows Tool Registry, Bramwell & Sons' stamp record, and Jules Ardent's bench book. Bess Yonkin gives him the ledger page and refuses the dies. Jules admits he built six clamps, that JA-4417 went out on the seventeenth of last month to a man with a rehabilitation clinic card, and that a clamp holds a circuit open — so if something has been cut to match his jaw, they used his *drawing*, not his tool. Elias follows the paper, feels clean satisfaction, and then recognises that the satisfaction came from using a man as a source. He gets the clinic's intake filing copy legally and slowly at a counter, in a corridor, in front of a receptionist, and signs the visitor book in her ink with his own name on it, because in nine days nobody had written anything down in his own building. Jules agrees to hand the clamp over at four the next morning, documented rather than seized. **End:** a licensed rehabilitation clinic on Verge Row whose ninety-one intake entries contain no account, no patient matching the card, and one printed signature.
6. **Ch 6 — what Elias touched.** Wednesday evening, Mara's residue laboratory. The clamp is on loan and reverts to rehabilitation account 44-9 at midnight, so the comparison is a deadline. The custody line needs a named police officer; Nadia is two wards up holding night-market porter **Rafi Ilori**, who saw somebody go up the stair and will not stop talking. Elias is alone on the bench, which is the one thing Mara told him not to be. A Registry preservation order lets an inspector treat the dispensary's stock as ordinary maintenance by morning, so the clinic's stockroom is about to disappear into paperwork. He touches the channel unlogged at 20:53 and gets a third reading: the same release, failing at the same joint, in brass, with a living man's name in it. Mara refuses to use the contaminated reading and states, in order, that the channel is finished and the reason is a person. He shakes for two hours, keeps nothing down, and by midnight the clamp is gone from the bench. **End:** Niko Senn is booked into the Verge Row clinic through the account as having received a treatment he does not remember, and a transfer notice is initialled at the counter.
7. **Ch 7 — the mechanic's hands.** Thursday, Nadia's POV, from Rafi Ilori's overnight account to a transit maintenance shed to the back table of Elias's own shop. Rafi adds that the man was checking the buses, not lost. Jules will not say where Niko is until Nadia explains what a notice on a clinic door would do. He then gives it up: he has moved Niko to the Kettle Rows Community Clinic, the one address the account cannot book a patient into, and he explains that the account reaches people by merging files, not by walking through doors. He returns a canvas roll of tools, admits six years of unregistered repairs under a false clinic account, and asks her to record it. Nadia retains Elias on the case on one condition, made in front of Jules: he does not work alone. Elias apologises for using Jules as evidence first. **End:** the frame design was commissioned through a clinic account with no named patient, in a sealed envelope with a maker's stamp pair, and Niko's transfer order is issued at 06:00 Friday.
8. **Ch 8 — the clinic that will not close.** Friday morning, Kettle Rows Community Clinic. Sana Okafor will not let him search, will not let him near her patients, and makes him say out loud what he wants from her before she gives it. He stays in the corridor and hears what a Kettle Rows tenant says about the man who lives under the woman he killed. The Wednesday night "maintenance" man came at 21:10 in a Ward Works vest and signed her book, and he was tall-ish and neat. A registrar brings the transfer order; Elias reads the four-act sequence box standing back with his hands visible and does not touch it, and realises Niko Senn is not in the draft at all. Dr Okafor files a clinical hold that stops the transfer without a raid, and puts a copy of the order on the waiting-room board. She tells him plainly that a case he keeps is a boy who does not exist outside a building. **End:** the Proof Registry wants a countersignature before the hearing, and Mara wants him in a laboratory at one o'clock to look at three pieces of paper and not one object.
9. **Ch 9 — the missing release.** Friday afternoon, Saint Orra Reach laboratory, figures only. Mara shows that the fifth thing on the desk plate is release-shaped with **no settling curve**, which means it was not made in that room; the same phase signature appears in the four-act box on Niko's transfer order; the two together make a **template**. She and Elias disagree in public about what he saw on Monday — he read a release performed, she read something delivered — and neither of them can yet tell the difference, which is the ceiling of what First Margin knows. The four acts turn out to be a category on a Concord form: *an act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own*, which is the phrase in his own twelve-year-old Morrow Street file, which he is not permitted to open. He writes a **Disagreement of the Assigned Detective** with four numbered items, refuses to countersign at 16:41, and signs the plate's sleeve — never the glass — beside Mara's initial. He tells Registrar-Counsel Bettina Haile that he wants to know which document the Registry intends to correct. **End:** the hearing-pack document request tells the crew when the clinic's records are copied, so they will come tonight.
10. **Ch 10 — the room behind the dispensary.** Friday night, the clinic corridor, from half past nine. An hour and a half of almost nothing: a night porter tapping a wall that has had a tick in it for three weeks, Mara logging a sealed room in ten-minute intervals, and a counter ward light going out for half a second four times, the fourth one longer. Then Nadia gets a telephone warrant from an out-of-hours magistrate at 22:41, having told the magistrate she will come to her office at eight to explain it. Jules maps the ward line and names the two brass saddle clips as the only binding. Mara, from a folding stool, gets a fourth reading through the closed steel door: the anchor is the transfer order itself, the address is one person in a bed upstairs, and the release is already seated in a channel with no curve on it. Elias says the order out loud, which is the only thing he can do, and Jules turns it into a place on a wall. He goes in on the latch plate, the operator pulls the tube off the wall, knocks it across the stockroom, and goes out through the yard and over the paint-works wall after photographing Elias on the paint bins. The working stops because nine inches of galvanised steel left the brick, not because Elias beat anybody — and the carrier stays in the room, the operator's hands holding nothing but a caliper and a phone. Nadia logs an unregistered ward object, cause undetermined, and signs the halves and the foil stencil over to Mara as a transfer. Mara lifts the second salt-glass plate on a folding table on Marshall Street at 01:40 and measures the same failure at a smaller scale with a cleaner hand, and a third and cleanest maker's pair, graded like a production run. Nine minutes of ward protection gone. Elias sits on the kerb until nearly three while three residents, one at a time, tell him what the dark was like. **End:** nobody checked the access log, the stencil is now in a laboratory the crew know about, the Registry reissued for 06:00 Monday, and the crew did not make the frame at all.

**Batch close:** two salt-glass plates in Mara Quill's name — the Chapter 2 threshold plate carrying the coarse seam, and the Chapter 10 plate carrying the graded third maker's pair, a second finer seam, both conduit halves and the abandoned foil stencil — a refused countersignature, a recorded disagreement, a delayed and then reissued transfer order, an open hearing on the 14th, a clamp back in the outer wards with only eleven figures surviving it, and the question of who is teaching the network to make frames it cannot currently make.
