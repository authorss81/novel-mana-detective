## Volume 01, batch 0003 — Arc C, "The Witness Who Was Rehearsed" (Chapters 21–30)

> Retained for continuity. 21. **Ch 21 — the flag on the board.** Monday 1 October, 07:45, the Sequence Desk. The case board is wiped for a **Holloway Street burglary** and re-used, and the eraser has been the good kind. **Nadia Kest** says the flag on his application went up at **eleven on Sunday night**, earlier than she said, and that the Registry's index office rang at half past twelve. He is the assigned detective until somebody in writing says otherwise: **four days and four hours.** She is the one who made the deal that his application is served after the board. At **Alderney Row** in the outer wards, **Wen Senn**, 66, nineteen years on a launderette counter, no telephone, tells him about the man who came on Sunday night at about nine, was very polite, and gave her nephew a card: a **Witness Services leaflet**. **Niko Senn**, 24, one night at his aunt's, has been twice to the free service at the back of Concord Hall — **Monday 24 September and Saturday 29 September** — and has been told by a woman called **Kaye** that nothing she did could put anything in his head, and believes her. Elias asks for nothing; he tells him the board is Thursday at ten, that he may bring his aunt, that he is not to be helped into an order he did not see, and he does not ask the other question. **Niko Senn says the name and then stops**, and says: *you don't get to do that.* He leaves **the leaflet and nothing else**, and in the corridor at twenty to twelve on the same street he finally reads item 8 of his own note of 29 September and stands in front of a clear sleeve on a public board and reads the issuing-office line he wrote that he had never read: **Witness Services (Sequence Support).** He writes the date, the time and the name on the back of his own left hand and does not scrub it off.
22. **Ch 22 — Witness Services.** Tuesday 2 October, the Morrow Hill forms counter at opening, then the back of the ground floor of Concord Hall. The **Witness Training Compendium, Part Three: Sequence Support and Recall**, third printing, **three credits**, eleven modules, **Module 4: Grounding and Anchor**, nineteen pages. The manual is careful about the word it does not use. Two boxes: *A supplied order is not an account and may not be recorded as one*, and a second box requiring the practitioner to **record the premises, the date and the reason where a session is conducted anywhere but a room the practitioner has prepared** — the commonest reason being that the person cannot travel. Page eleven, and the four lines in the box on the leaflet are on that page. The office has a free rack of leaflets, a board of thirty certified Module 4 practitioners, and a woman at a counter who tells him the file on **Niko Senn was opened on 11 September**, six days before a woman was killed two floors above his own shop, that the referral is a **card number** she will not read him, and that **a card referral is countable, and somebody is always told who sent a person**. She refuses him the address and is right to. On the wall opposite, **practice note W-7**: *A withdrawn account is superseded and remains on file. Withdrawal is recorded in the ordinary way and does not by itself impugn either account* — careful, correct, and drawing no line at all between which account is the account.
23. **Ch 23 — two rooms, one set of words.** Wednesday 3 October, **Alderney Row** at ten to nine, then a second-floor room above a tax office in **Fallow Street** at eleven. **Niko Senn** says the name: **Neil Hardaker**. **The session is not tomorrow**: Elias opens by telling him the office has taken his Thursday morning appointment off because of the board, that the counterwoman said so on Tuesday when he had not asked, and that he is booked back in for **the morning of Thursday 18 October at half past eleven** — and then gives him the standing advice, undated. He will not give the card and gives the reason — *if I give you the card then it wasn't worth anything, it was evidence, and then I'll be the one who made it* — and the third reason, that it was forty credits and it worked, and he would like one thing in his life to have been worth forty credits. The room has a price list on the wall, a client book with the spine gone white, a consent form whose four clauses are the leaflet's four lines and Module 4's box, and **no tools in it at all**, which Elias notices and does not ask about. Hardaker, 46, bought the Compendium in 2021 for three credits, was shown the sentences by a course tutor, teaches exactly what he was shown, keeps names in a book because he is honest, has turned down two referrals, and sent two of his own clients to the free service because he could not pay them. He did **four** sessions with Niko Senn in the last week of August. He has done **two sessions on clients' own premises** — the fourth of August and **the sixteenth of September** — and gives the dates and the reason without being pushed, because the module requires it. Elias pays forty credits, takes a **receipt** (number, no address, no Concord registration) and keeps it in his wallet, and writes the second date on a page with nothing beside it.
24. **Ch 24 — the board.** Thursday 4 October, a ward authority building, sitting at ten, eight chairs, and eight in the gallery including **Wen Senn**, **Jules Ardent** and **Nadia Kest**, and one man in a grey suit at the chair's table, all of them only members of the public. **Ada Lund** chairs; **Martin Crowther** appears for rehabilitation account 44-9 and says plainly that the account wants the hold lifted and that the honest reason is a file that sits is a file a continuity office may price off. Item 3, in one line: the hold is **upheld**; the board **records the discharge of 24 September made while the hold stood**; the account is not to receive the person before a second clinician has examined him; and a copy of the minute goes **to the account on Verge Row**, to the clinical respondent, and to the person. **Niko Senn gives his account in his own words — mug, click, room — and the minute records it in those words**, and Elias sits in the gallery with his best question and does not ask it, because he has no standing in a ward authority building and never had. Afterwards he tells Niko Senn exactly why he said nothing, and Niko Senn says he is glad: *a board that you can be corrected is not a board.* On the way out Niko Senn picks a **Witness Services leaflet** off a rack by the door, four feet from a man who knows exactly what it is, and Elias says nothing at all.
25. **Ch 25 — the order he was taught.** Friday 5 October, interview room at the Kettle Rows station, from ten to nine. **PROTECTED: the volume's midpoint.** **Niko Senn** hands over **Form W-1 (rev. 3)**, Withdrawal of Account, one side, and fills **field six** himself in his own hand: he went to the free service on 24 and 29 September, he has been given an order he did not have before he went, he cannot say which part he saw, and he would rather have no account than one he cannot stand behind — *the leaflet says an uncertain account recorded as uncertain is worth more*. **The truth underneath it:** four sessions in an invented room with an invented tap and an invented name; the same method, the same words; and then two sessions in which **Miss Kaye walked him up the actual stair of 14 Kettle Rows to the actual landing and stood where he was standing on the sixteenth.** The order is **room, click, name** — three true things in a trained order, and the trained order is **word for word** the order Elias read off his own jamb at ten to two on the morning of the seventeenth, and gave to the Registry by telephone eight days later. **Three orders are now in play: the one he withdrew and the board minuted (mug, click, room), the trained one (room, click, name), and Elias's own perception, which is the same as the trained one. Schedule 1 records three reading occasions and four limitations and records no order.** *I put a real room and a real click and a real mug in an order I had had since August, and it went in, and it is mine now.* And the thing that was actually happening to him: whatever it was on the sixteenth is not finished, and it has his name on it as well as hers, and she used it — *she did not put a room in my head, I put it in my own head.* **The liability:** the bundle produced in answer to the withdrawal carries at tab 3 a **Witness, Operations intake sheet of 11 September** and a ruled box headed *description of order as perceived, by the assigned detective, to be used in witness support*, containing **his own words** — *in the room before the door made its noise, then her speaking* — entered at **14:22 on 21 September** from a telephone call he made in answer to a polite question, in the office that issues C-441-KR-04, accepted by **J. KAYE**, Witness Services (Sequence Support). He remembers the call. He read *to be used in witness support* as *supporting the witness-support file*. And Niko Senn says she read him **item 3 of his own Schedule 1** off a sheet on her desk on the Tuesday — *he has been honest in writing that he cannot rule out the ordinary explanation, and that is exactly why it needs holding* — and that she did it twice and that he has said it nine hundred times since. Elias does not read him. He wants to, and the wanting is on the page, and he puts his hands flat the way a man in a smock taught a boy to and takes them off again. He asks **Nadia Kest** to write a custody line with the time on it and her signature under it — 11:26 — because in eleven days it will be a question. The bundle is stamped **received 08:52**; Niko Senn walked in at 09:05.
26. **Ch 26 — off file.** Saturday 6 October, the Sequence Desk from eight, the car park at twenty to one. **DS Paul Rennick** has read the whole file on a corner of a desk with his coat on and tells Elias the three things he is entitled to say and no more. The **retention review** has one box ticked — *other: connected certified record, application pending* — and the word misconduct is not on the page; **Nadia Kest signed it at five to four on Friday and it took effect at forty to twelve**, and she says the ground out loud so he has heard it from her. The application was **served by hand at 11:40 on Friday**, signed for at the second floor of 14 Kettle Rows. Orr's office declines in one paragraph — the archive reports sequence positions, a form category is a publishing convention, and you may not reapply for twelve months without the Deputy Chief's written authority. **The Tribunal archive reported on Friday to Deputy Chief Orr's office**, a clerk opened the 2017 file on Thursday alone in a room, and **Nadia Kest is the reason it is not Elias** and says so. He does the arithmetic on 29 September standing up: at eleven he was at a wire rack buying a concordance; at half past one a woman read a man a line off a sheet; at about a quarter to two he filed the note whose item 8 says he had never read the office's name. The out-of-hours magistrate's **refusal** has sat unopened since Friday afternoon: the application does not name the premises, the material does not connect to anything provable, it is refused as presented, and *a refusal in the same terms is barred* — and Nadia will not name the address now either. In the car park she says **yes**, the department is better off without him on the file, and then says the rest of it in four minutes, and warns him that Rennick will get an account out of his own office inside nine days. He takes his notebook off the desk and Priya Sandoval says *all right* and asks nothing. **The board is washed. Three columns in marker, a case number, and a street he has never heard of.**
27. **Ch 27 — the back room.** Sunday 7 October, 14 Kettle Rows, from nine into the night. **Otto Vahl** takes his radio bench out of the back room to the front window, and the reason he gives is the cat, and the tea urn changes ownership. **Halvard Reisz** on the stairs asks whether the file touches the shop and is told no and then told the rest. Elias reads **Part Three end to end** and finds **Module 7, Accounts of Exposure**: *Where the account given is an account of a working the person has been exposed to, the exposure is the anchor, and the order the person is asked to hold is the order the person gives. The office does not supply the order*; *the manual does not describe a method of distinguishing the order perceived from the order expected, because no such method is established*; and on page five, *there is no evidence on the file that there was a previous account.* **Mara Quill** comes to the shop for the first time in six years, because the laboratory and room two are both closed to one of them, and makes him say the names out loud: **Niko Senn, Sana Okafor, Jules Ardent, Marguerite Osei** — and then says he has missed one, and that the harm to her needs no name. Both of them are right, and neither can move, and she will not write it for him. He writes **four sheets by hand** — *A NOTE BY A MEMBER OF THE PUBLIC. On the origin of a description of order* — naming documents and no person. **Nadia Kest** cannot file it, and then **receives** it: a miscellaneous receipt, no case number, *received as a document, not evidence, not a note on any matter, no file*, plus a second line in her own hand that has never been written in this department: *to be produced on the request of the addressee only; not to be indexed; not to be circulated.* She gives him the stub and says it is the last thing she will do for him as an officer.
28. **Ch 28 — what Schedule 1 is worth.** Monday 8 October, Room Two at Morrow Hill at nine, and a tea room off Fallow Street from ten past ten until half past four. **Bettina Haile** produces **406 pages** because a Registry cannot withhold from a party, gives nothing else, will not say who is behind the solicitor, and warns him that the appeal panel is a different room with different names and is not the twenty minutes he wants. Tab 3 is Schedule 1 in his own hand with a printed cover sheet, and at the foot of it, **a line saying that no word of a produced document is to be removed at the request of the officer who wrote it.** **Tab 61: CARRIER SPECIFICATION CS-3, issued 30 September** — a channel that agrees with the stockroom carrier in width, wall and lip, a seat with **no dimension on it**, the note *per description relied on*, *held against the assigned account; not to be widened without reference to that account*, and a box reading **DEPTH: Fig. 3, Q.** **Tab 88: PRACTICE NOTE W-11, issued 2 October**, listing order-perception methods with **Schedule 1 as a worked example** and the guidance that *where an officer states that he is unable to exclude an ordinary explanation for his own account, the account requires support rather than clarification.* **Tab 140** is **Lindqvist & Roake** for account 44-9 asking to strike Schedule 1, and **tab 141** is the account's own schedule of every four-act box on its paperwork, each line reading *account position: no knowledge and no objection; bookkeeping only; not an admission.* **Tab 356** is a procurement line: a class, a code and a premises, and nothing else. He telephones **Jules Ardent** once from a call box and gets the one sentence he needed: *when a drawing says per something, that is not a dimension, it is a referral to another document — it does not say how deep the seat is, it says go and find out.* He does not telephone Mara Quill, and writes down why. 29. **Ch 29 — Fig. 3.** Tuesday 9 October, the residue laboratory at Saint Orra Reach, 13:20 to ten to ten the next morning. **Four conditions on a piece of A4 with a torn top edge**: a new sleeve and log number; her name on the consent and his on the breach on the same sheet, in front of a witness who is neither of them; **her figure first, in her log, under her number**; neither of them stops the other. The fifth thing, which is not on the sheet, is that a woman who has known him for six years looked at him and could not say the word. **Nadia Kest** comes at one, off the case, because the condition that he does not work alone is still in 441-KR and does not carry her name. The plate comes out of its case and goes back into it; the padlock goes through; log **1147**. The channel section agrees with the drawing; the seat's depth is **not on the drawing**, only referred to — *per description relied on* — so she measures the metal: **2.604 mm**, and the number on the drawing is **2.6**, and it is hers, certified in Concord Hall on 27 September. And the seat in the metal has **no tool in it**: the radius narrows a third of the way along and changes, and where it changes there is a thickening a fortieth of a millimetre wide, on the inside of the curve, **exactly the width of a pen**. A man changes pens once and the second pass is the thickening, and the place where he changed pens is in the brass. Mara's instruction, given first because her figure is hers: *your four sheets are a list of documents and a list of documents gets answered with a document, and you have not got the one thing in it that cannot be answered with paper.* He puts a hand on the plate, having said out loud in front of a witness that she can refuse and that he will do it anyway. **The one panel:** *the order was the order of a sentence; everything in that channel was made after the sentence; and the second thing was that there was no man in it at the end — a sentence, a number, a pen that changed once, and a bag.* He comes off it at **13:20** and **cannot place the rest of the afternoon in order**. He walks, he does not know which bus, Otto Vahl finds him at six and at nine, he tells the same lie twice, and the order does not come back until **09:50 on Wednesday morning**, in a corridor, in the middle of a sentence to a doctor about a wall, with nobody announcing it. **Mara Quill's breach note is in the post and she says it is worse than what he would have written.**
30. **Ch 30 — on the board.** Wednesday 10 October, the Kettle Rows Community Clinic, from ten past five in the morning. The telephone rings at ten past five and it takes him forty seconds to know it is morning, and **Dr Okafor** has telephoned a shop because she cannot work out how to put a sentence in front of four sheets without a police officer in it. She makes him say who it costs, and he gives **Niko Senn, Sana Okafor, Jules Ardent, Marguerite Osei**, and when he gives Mara Quill she says *no*, and makes him find the one he has not thought about, and he cannot. **The four sheets go up flat at 08:14**, four drawing pins, one in each corner, under three lines in her ink with her name under his. **Nine people in the waiting room** at half past eight, one of them **Jules Ardent**, fourth chair, who says *that's right* to nobody and does not get up. He reads them in the order they are written on the paper and says out loud, to the room, that he has not got the order of the last day and a half and is not going to pretend otherwise, and somebody asks him if he is all right and he says **no**. He then says the one thing nobody can answer with a document: a seat in brass with a pen line in it, a drawing made from a description, and a depth written as **Fig. 3, Q** — *my own account of the order of that working is unverified, I live at the address, and this is about where a written description of an order came from and not about how anybody died.* At about ten o'clock **Marguerite Osei's note** is put on the dispensary counter: three numbered items, a municipal form, and *I am not refusing a formal request. I am refusing a person.* **He does not reply**, because an answer is correspondence and correspondence has a file on it, and writes four lines on the back of his left hand instead. **Nadia Kest** adds a second entry to the back of the receipt stub recording where the single copy of the paper is. **DS Rennick** has a **photograph** of the four sheets, taken at the clinic counter that morning by a woman who asked him for no form, and says on the page that it is a photograph off a wall and not a copy of the paper. He then says the only three things he is entitled to say in a car park: anybody may publish; it changes nothing about who holds the file; and he is putting it in the file because it is a document, which is the whole of his respect. At five o'clock he stands in the yard of 14 Kettle Rows with a set of keys and **does not go up to the fourth floor**, and spends ninety seconds deciding not to, and knows that the ninety seconds were not about courage. At six the shop telephone rings and it is **Niko Senn**, from a call box, to say that his aunt has been crying since half nine and could not get to the end of the second sheet, and that he has gone and bought the little cakes, and that he is ringing to say that he did it.
**Batch close:** a true trained order and a man who is honest about it; a police officer's own description of an order typed into an office by telephone on 21 September and read back to a frightened man on 29 September; a four-sheet note on a public wall with no person's name in it; a municipal clerk's three-line refusal; a seat in brass with a pen line in it and a depth that is a woman's threshold measurement; and a man with no file, no board, no standing, and a receipt stub in a pocket.
