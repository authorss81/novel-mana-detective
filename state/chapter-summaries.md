## Volume 01, batch 0002 — Arc B, "The Ward Under the Clinic" (Chapters 11–20)

Cards: `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0002.md`. Arc B owns escalation beats 5, 6 and 7. It contains **no reading, no margin panel and no nosebleed**; what it takes is Elias's access.

11. **Ch 11 — until Tuesday.** Saturday 22 September, from twenty to seven. He cannot account for Friday except through other people's records of it. At 07:16 a number he has never had sends him a photograph of himself standing on paint bins with blood on his face, and a screenshot of a 400-member residents' group that got it at 04:52 and had written forty-one replies under it by seven, among them *he let her in* and, at 06:58, a stranger's observation that the ward light over the dispensary counter came back at four and the light on Kettle Rows street did not. He writes what Friday night actually cost the building into the Sequence Desk day book in four timed lines, including a fifth about the second-landing stair light whose ballast he replaced himself in the spring and which nobody has ever signed off. Ahmed walks him down the corridor so that he is not the first one through with a camera, tells him he has decided the seventy-one-year-old night clerk is not a villain and wants that written down, and tells him the fire officer's honest answer is Tuesday ten o'clock. Jules has used the two brass saddle clips on a new length of conduit and has written down every tick in the building with three copies in the city, one of which is not his. Mara puts eggs in front of him at ten without discussing it. **Niko Senn** has been marked *discharge* by account 44-9 at six that morning, which fixes him and loses him, and a lad in the corridor has told him there is another one, in Halloway, the woman who had the flicker, and Niko did not ask a single question about her. He hands the photograph to Nadia as a recorded item at twenty to one and says he has not traced the sender and is not asking anybody to.
12. **Ch 12 — the slip in the tray.** Nadia's Saturday. In a room above a licensing office on Vine Street at eight, **Magistrate Hadwin Slade** grants her a *conditional* entry: the endorsement at 23:41, the entry at 23:54, thirteen minutes, no application filed, no finding either way, and his own name on the note. He says he will hear her Carrow Lane application on Monday and will object if she brings it at nine. She then discovers the Deputy Chief's routing slip has acquired a typed annex asking three things — the form category, the box number, and the office that completed the box — and writes four hundred words: items one and two refusable because a form category is a printer's convention, item four the true excuse, item five the true sentence, which refers the file to the Tribunal archive for a full sequence position at the Deputy Chief's authority, due **Friday the fifth of October**. Orr's office accepts and the archive will be opened by a clerk, **without him and without her**, on paper. She initialled the annex *received and referred* and did not send it, and she admits to herself in a car that item four is not an excuse. She discloses the countersigned slip, annex and reply to Elias at four o'clock **in front of a witness**, because a man told on Saturday and read on Sunday has had a whole weekend to be angry about it.
13. **Ch 13 — the whole book.** Sunday 23 September. **Halvard Reisz** comes up his own stairs with a district re-assessment: the block's score has gone from 61 in June to 49, the band from *elevated* to *standard*, and the ward maintenance contract for the block was re-tendered on the June score in August and has been running at forty per cent under since midnight — while the one thing he complained about, a landing light late by a second and a half, has got worse. Elias cannot tell him, because his answer would rest on a faculty that is not evidence, and Reisz goes back down his stairs. At the clinic the consultation form demands a number in public; Sana Okafor writes **a hundred**, and Elias does not correct her, because a hundred is the number that stops a van turning into that yard and he has no standing whatever in the number of people in her waiting room. She refuses him one page of the maintenance book and gives him all forty-one months of it, copied and pinned to her own waiting-room board, so that no page can be lifted out later; the price is the clinic's own failures, published in its own ink, in front of people who can read them for two days. The Wednesday page is photographed again from the stockroom doorway with Ahmed as a second witness, and on it are a name and a vest number: *D. MEARS. W-4471. Check the line.* Both check out — a live vest, a real man, sixty-one, twenty-two years in the trade, resigned in June. A Registry hearing pack arrives by hand for the clinic to answer, and item four of its production schedule asks for the maintenance book complete, **including all pages bearing entries by persons not employed by the clinic** — so somebody already knows the book is there and knows which pages they want.
14. **Ch 14 — Monday the twenty-fourth.** Morrow Hill, Room Two, a Registry *preliminary attestation* and not a hearing of a dangerous working. Bettina Haile reads two and a half well-made pages in which every sentence is true and every sentence is a reason not to look at anything else. Elias gets the Registry's own systems clerk to confirm, on the record, from the screen, that certificate C-441-KR-04 was issued at 16:10 on Wednesday 19 September, that the transfer order came out of the outbox at 16:51, and that the file was opened at 09:20 on Tuesday the 18th. **Nobody disputes the times.** Wren Abasi explains that certificates are generated against the request and not against the print, and that there is a note about it in the manual, and that the note in the manual is a stronger document than a witness in a room. Elias asks for two things. Haile grants the first — the four-item Disagreement attached to the attestation in full as **Schedule 1** — and denies the second, on the correct ground that recording a named man in a document he is not a party to and cannot contest would convert a witness into a subject, and tells him the young man is recorded better in a clinical hold a physician filed that this Registry cannot overwrite. The attestation issues on the Registry's own authority at 10:20, unsigned by him, closing nothing. Nadia affirms on the record, with her name, that she retained an assigned detective she knew lived at the scene. In the lobby Haile warns him that a schedule is appealable, and that the forty-one minutes are in the file, and that she signed the certificate with her eyes open.
15. **Ch 15 — Halloway Street.** Tuesday 25 September. A new consequence of the reissue: account 44-9 marked **Niko Senn** *discharge* at 06:00 on Monday for non-attendance, the file closed, and he is now nobody's patient and unfindable. In a cold room four streets from the market, **Mavis Brannigan**, 57, fish-market stallholder, is on the floor with a chest freezer open and the roof unit running and four degrees of cold. Her daughter **Kirsten Brannigan**, 29, tells them unasked that she bought a *ward continuity unit* in August for 340 credits on the account, found through a card in the letting file, and that the man came at night and gave her a sheet with a diagram and a tick box and told her the tick box was for the warranty survey. A hardback appointment diary holds fifty-three entries since the second week of July, nine of them ending *resolved*, eleven reports in six weeks. On the office desk, ordinary lease paper nobody reads: a **schedule of ward fittings with a four-act sequence box** where the printed instruction describes three. Elias counts four from arm's length with his hands visible and says out loud that all he has brought to this desk is the ability to count four. Mara Quill tells Kirsten Brannigan, before anyone else can, that **no instrument in this city has found anything unusual in the room over the deceased** — that it is true, that it will be true in a year, and that the only thing standing between that sentence and this case is a piece of paper with her figures on it. She takes the lift at 11:35 with the daughter present, in the daughter's name, describing every step, and the daughter signs the sleeve. The Registry's information office circulates a draft line at 14:00, ten hours after the body was found: *a refrigeration fault at a commercial premises in Halloway Street.*
16. **Ch 16 — five documents.** Wednesday 26 September, and no crime anywhere in it. **Desmond Bramwell**, 71, of Bramwell & Sons, will not produce the die ledger without a case number, and volunteers two things nobody asked for: the acceptance-pair profile was cut twice in August, once to a licensed maker on a Kettle Rows address and once to a left-hand post box under a trading name, and the second die carried no maker's initials. A Cutter Street merchant produces three cash invoices to a sole trader trading as **Halloran Supply** — forty sheets of four-tenth foil, sixty lengths of galvanised conduit, one gross of channel section — collected by a courier. At the **Tanager Street works depot** the municipal ward fault log is a plastic box: 106 slips in the quarter, 81 resolved, 77 same-day, 68 with an attendance code, four codes, no names, the approved list in an office, and a woman from the municipal contracts office telephoned on Wednesday asking for the distribution of codes by post code and whether the mapping is unchanged since the April revision. Nadia gets the ward continuity schedules out in public: **44-9 is in the book of premises that are not covered**, and the district block's premium has fallen eleven points while its reported faults have fallen sixty per cent, which is a district made cheap on purpose. At 17:52 on Halloway Street the door schedule is read standing back, photographed in place, original in the client's drawer — and its five printed lines end *Confirm the patient is in the room.*
17. **Ch 17 — what she will not certify.** Mara Quill's Thursday. The Concord letter has already told her that no instrument in the city has found anything unusual in the room over the deceased, and the assessor **Priyanka Lyle** is not cruel: she asks Mara to see where the office is about to stand. Mara certifies the **measurements** — four figures off the threshold plate, the phase signature, the absence of a settling curve, the two break measurements, every log number — and refuses to certify that the act was copied, that anybody had a motive, or to put anything on a Concord register. **Roy Prentice** then asks for the foil stencil, reasonably, on the ground that it is the only physical object linking the first two frames to one production method; the only registered facility in reach is the third floor of the building they are sitting in. She refuses, gives her reason — the moment the object is in a register every figure she has produced becomes a figure taken out of a registered object — and asks for the refusal to be minuted word for word, *because a refusal that nobody wrote down did not happen.* She also hands over her own note of Wednesday the 19th, **headed 20:50 and written at 21:03, thirteen minutes later, from memory**, which is the most damaging document in the file and is in her handwriting, and says she was not going to soften it and is not. On a bench in a corridor she tells Elias that she would put the plates on a register this afternoon if he were not the assigned detective; that he is angrier about the note than she is; and then she says her brother's name — **Iven Quill**, six years and seventeen days — and attaches no lead to it. She refuses him the laboratory and room two, and ends on the thing that matters: a refusal and a request are the same piece of paper with two names on it, so her refusal is now a reason to want the stencil.
18. **Ch 18 — the supplier.** Friday 28 September, Concord Hall, ground floor, the public register of licensed ward practitioners, a queue and a woman at the counter. **Orin Sable**, registered 4 March 2019, class **supply**, premises 2nd floor Carrow Lane above a printer's, renewed this March, with one endorsement for supply of ward components within a licensed premise and one **restriction** from carrying out any dangerous working, any working affecting a person, and any attendance at a scene where a working is in progress — the restriction being in the original registration and therefore not an investigation. A man of about forty in a very good coat stands behind him in the queue and then goes out at a speed that is not fast. Elias does not follow him, and understands why, and writes four words and nothing else: **SUPPLIER OF FRAMES**. He cannot put the nine inches on an invoice for conduit; what he has is a profile, a trading name, a class of licence and a fortnight. He tells Nadia to write the limitation in his own hand, and that a man in a good coat now knows his name, and that the man in the good coat walking out unhurried is the *only* reason he did not follow. Then the cost of Wednesday: Bess Yonkin telephoned the desk, which she has never done in nineteen years, because **Jules Ardent walked in at 09:45 with his die block in a bag and asked her to seize it**, standing at her own counter and reading the seizure form aloud twice. A maker's enquiry opened. A standard clinical practice notice went on the door of 9 Pell Street at about two o'clock. The uncollected second set is now inside that enquiry and cannot be moved by anybody, including him. In a solicitor's rooms above a tobacconist's the solicitor establishes that a seized die and an unregistered plate can be compared in about twenty minutes, in the one room in the city that can hold both — the room Mara refused on Thursday. Jules offers to open his bench room. **Elias says no**, and records that both refusals are in the chain and that they are now both worse off. At 16:40 a Concord assessor in a Ward Works coat stands four feet from the clinic's board with a finger on a page of the maintenance book, reading, alone, with no questions, because everything she wants is on a wall in the clinic's own ink.
19. **Ch 19 — the unknotted gap.** Saturday 29 September. He reads a footer: the registrar's transfer order on the clinic's board is **Form R-2 (rev. 4), sequence box SEQ 4(c)**, and he did not read the footer on Friday because he was counting. The concordance of sequence-box codes costs four credits at the Morrow Hill forms counter, and the preface says the categories have not been renumbered since 2006, so a 4(c) in 2017 and a 4(c) this morning are the same category in the same words from the same office. **4 (c):** *an act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own* — and then the part nobody reads: entry does not assert that a release was performed, it asserts the record is to be treated as though one was, **for the purposes of this record only**. And **4 (d)** — *a sequence with no recorded release, closed at the discretion of the certifying office* — withdrawn from standard forms in 2011 and retained for indexing pre-2011 records only, which is his sentence: *no release was recorded at the threshold.* Twelve years of carrying a sentence that was an indexing instruction. But the only document that says what is in the box on the Morrow Street report is the report, in an archive, behind a form whose field eleven cannot be left blank. He writes a nine-item note — item 6 the prohibition, item 9 that he is not requesting the certificate either — signs it, and puts *this is a note, it is not a request, do not process it as one* along the bottom. Nadia files it as a note in front of him, and then tells him, in a car, that she wrote the four hundred words on Saturday morning and **deliberately left out the line**, and that the correct response to that is to be extremely angry with her, because she would rather be argued with than admired.
20. **Ch 20 — a line she would not write.** Sunday 30 September. In the car she gives him the real reason: the moment that phrase is in a departmental position the old matter is live, and a live matter makes 441-KR a matter with a connected record, and a detective with a connected record is a man whose *refusal of a countersignature* is now a fact to be attached to a young man's address in every report anybody writes. Then the form. An application for access to a certified record is one side and costs nothing and about four hundred people fill one in a year, and field eleven is headed *other matters to which the applicant is or has been connected, in which the record is or may be relevant*, and it must be completed, and a request without it comes back to whoever returned it. So he fills it in, in his own hand, in the order in which it will be used against him, and gives the full disposition of 441-KR and states that the answer to his own question may be no. It is lodged by hand at about five, logged, with the time on it, and it is not yet served. **The review board is on the page as its minute** — four lines, a stamp, a signature and one sentence in brackets, *the Board notes that the production was received after the sitting had been convened* — and it was **adjourned on Thursday the 27th to Thursday the 4th of October** because the clinic's ninety-odd pages were served at four o'clock in the afternoon for a board that sat at ten that morning. Nadia says the correct answer is not the same as the right one and is not going to say it out loud to anybody. He asks for the record to be served after the board. Then, in the clinic corridor, **Niko Senn** — out of room two, waiting on a taxi at 17:10 because the 18:30 bus is too late, and who worked the discharge out himself on Tuesday and is not told it — puts a card in his hand: three weeks in a wallet, the smock man's name written on the back in the room. **Elias refuses it**, because in four months it would have his prints and a faint and would make Niko Senn a man who gave a card to the police; and when the boy starts to say the name anyway, he refuses that too, and says why, and it is not noble: the day he has a name he has an expectation, and he has done that to a mechanic and to a woman with a plate in a hard case and he is not doing it to a twenty-four-year-old in a corridor. The card stays in the pocket. At the gate the boy shouts that if he ever sees the man in the smock he will tell Elias first, and goes up Marshall Street to a bus.
**Batch close:** a supplier's name off a public register and a limitation in the assigned detective's own hand; a preliminary attestation that exists with his four-item disagreement bound to the back of it; a second death carrying the same fourth act in a different medium; a district made cheap on purpose and a ward contract cut forty per cent; a stencil refused onto a register on the page; a mechanic's die block surrendered without a word to anybody; a shelf number that has been a man's name for twelve years; and an application lodged in a car park on a Sunday that takes the case away from him, and will be served after Thursday the fourth of October.

## Volume 01, batch 0003 — Arc C, "The Witness Who Was Rehearsed" (Chapters 21–30)

Cards: `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0003.md`. Arc C owns escalation beat 8, the volume's **midpoint reversal**, in full. It contains **one reading, one panel and no nosebleed**; what it takes is the people who were willing to argue with a record.

21. **Ch 21 — the flag on the board.** Monday 1 October, 07:45, the Sequence Desk. The case board is wiped for a **Holloway Street burglary** and re-used, and the eraser has been the good kind. **Nadia Kest** says the flag on his application went up at **eleven on Sunday night**, earlier than she said, and that the Registry's index office rang at half past twelve. He is the assigned detective until somebody in writing says otherwise: **four days and four hours.** She is the one who made the deal that his application is served after the board. At **Alderney Row** in the outer wards, **Wen Senn**, 66, nineteen years on a launderette counter, no telephone, tells him about the man who came on Sunday night at about nine, was very polite, and gave her nephew a card: a **Witness Services leaflet**. **Niko Senn**, 24, one night at his aunt's, has been twice to the free service at the back of Concord Hall — **Monday 24 September and Saturday 29 September** — and has been told by a woman called **Kaye** that nothing she did could put anything in his head, and believes her. Elias asks for nothing; he tells him the board is Thursday at ten, that he may bring his aunt, that he is not to be helped into an order he did not see, and he does not ask the other question. **Niko Senn says the name and then stops**, and says: *you don't get to do that.* He leaves **the leaflet and nothing else**, and in the corridor at twenty to twelve on the same street he finally reads item 8 of his own note of 29 September and stands in front of a clear sleeve on a public board and reads the issuing-office line he wrote that he had never read: **Witness Services (Sequence Support).** He writes the date, the time and the name on the back of his own left hand and does not scrub it off.
22. **Ch 22 — Witness Services.** Tuesday 2 October, the Morrow Hill forms counter at opening, then the back of the ground floor of Concord Hall. The **Witness Training Compendium, Part Three: Sequence Support and Recall**, third printing, **three credits**, eleven modules, **Module 4: Grounding and Anchor**, nineteen pages. The manual is careful about the word it does not use. Two boxes: *A supplied order is not an account and may not be recorded as one*, and a second box requiring the practitioner to **record the premises, the date and the reason where a session is conducted anywhere but a room the practitioner has prepared** — the commonest reason being that the person cannot travel. Page eleven, and the four lines in the box on the leaflet are on that page. The office has a free rack of leaflets, a board of thirty certified Module 4 practitioners, and a woman at a counter who tells him the file on **Niko Senn was opened on 11 September**, six days before a woman was killed two floors above his own shop, that the referral is a **card number** she will not read him, and that **a card referral is countable, and somebody is always told who sent a person**. She refuses him the address and is right to. On the wall opposite, **practice note W-7**: *A withdrawn account is superseded and remains on file. Withdrawal is recorded in the ordinary way and does not by itself impugn either account* — careful, correct, and drawing no line at all between which account is the account.
23. **Ch 23 — two rooms, one set of words.** Wednesday 3 October, **Alderney Row** at ten to nine, then a second-floor room above a tax office in **Fallow Street** at eleven. **Niko Senn** says the name: **Neil Hardaker**. **The session is not tomorrow**: Elias opens by telling him the office has taken his Thursday morning appointment off because of the board, that the counterwoman said so on Tuesday when he had not asked, and that he is booked back in for **the morning of Thursday 18 October at half past eleven** — and then gives him the standing advice, undated. He will not give the card and gives the reason — *if I give you the card then it wasn't worth anything, it was evidence, and then I'll be the one who made it* — and the third reason, that it was forty credits and it worked, and he would like one thing in his life to have been worth forty credits. The room has a price list on the wall, a client book with the spine gone white, a consent form whose four clauses are the leaflet's four lines and Module 4's box, and **no tools in it at all**, which Elias notices and does not ask about. Hardaker, 46, bought the Compendium in 2021 for three credits, was shown the sentences by a course tutor, teaches exactly what he was shown, keeps names in a book because he is honest, has turned down two referrals, and sent two of his own clients to the free service because he could not pay them. He did **four** sessions with Niko Senn in the last week of August. He has done **two sessions on clients' own premises** — the fourth of August and **the sixteenth of September** — and gives the dates and the reason without being pushed, because the module requires it. Elias pays forty credits, takes a **receipt** (number, no address, no Concord registration) and keeps it in his wallet, and writes the second date on a page with nothing beside it.
24. **Ch 24 — the board.** Thursday 4 October, a ward authority building, sitting at ten, eight chairs, and eight in the gallery including **Wen Senn**, **Jules Ardent** and **Nadia Kest**, and one man in a grey suit at the chair's table, all of them only members of the public. **Ada Lund** chairs; **Martin Crowther** appears for rehabilitation account 44-9 and says plainly that the account wants the hold lifted and that the honest reason is a file that sits is a file a continuity office may price off. Item 3, in one line: the hold is **upheld**; the board **records the discharge of 24 September made while the hold stood**; the account is not to receive the person before a second clinician has examined him; and a copy of the minute goes **to the account on Verge Row**, to the clinical respondent, and to the person. **Niko Senn gives his account in his own words — mug, click, room — and the minute records it in those words**, and Elias sits in the gallery with his best question and does not ask it, because he has no standing in a ward authority building and never had. Afterwards he tells Niko Senn exactly why he said nothing, and Niko Senn says he is glad: *a board that you can be corrected is not a board.* On the way out Niko Senn picks a **Witness Services leaflet** off a rack by the door, four feet from a man who knows exactly what it is, and Elias says nothing at all.
25. **Ch 25 — the order he was taught.** Friday 5 October, interview room at the Kettle Rows station, from ten to nine. **PROTECTED: the volume's midpoint.** **Niko Senn** hands over **Form W-1 (rev. 3)**, Withdrawal of Account, one side, and fills **field six** himself in his own hand: he went to the free service on 24 and 29 September, he has been given an order he did not have before he went, he cannot say which part he saw, and he would rather have no account than one he cannot stand behind — *the leaflet says an uncertain account recorded as uncertain is worth more*. **The truth underneath it:** four sessions in an invented room with an invented tap and an invented name; the same method, the same words; and then two sessions in which **Miss Kaye walked him up the actual stair of 14 Kettle Rows to the actual landing and stood where he was standing on the sixteenth.** The order is **room, click, name** — three true things in a trained order, and the trained order is **word for word** the order Elias read off his own jamb at ten to two on the morning of the seventeenth, and gave to the Registry by telephone eight days later. **Three orders are now in play: the one he withdrew and the board minuted (mug, click, room), the trained one (room, click, name), and Elias's own perception, which is the same as the trained one. Schedule 1 records three reading occasions and four limitations and records no order.** *I put a real room and a real click and a real mug in an order I had had since August, and it went in, and it is mine now.* And the thing that was actually happening to him: whatever it was on the sixteenth is not finished, and it has his name on it as well as hers, and she used it — *she did not put a room in my head, I put it in my own head.* **The liability:** the bundle produced in answer to the withdrawal carries at tab 3 a **Witness, Operations intake sheet of 11 September** and a ruled box headed *description of order as perceived, by the assigned detective, to be used in witness support*, containing **his own words** — *in the room before the door made its noise, then her speaking* — entered at **14:22 on 21 September** from a telephone call he made in answer to a polite question, in the office that issues C-441-KR-04, accepted by **J. KAYE**, Witness Services (Sequence Support). He remembers the call. He read *to be used in witness support* as *supporting the witness-support file*. And Niko Senn says she read him **item 3 of his own Schedule 1** off a sheet on her desk on the Tuesday — *he has been honest in writing that he cannot rule out the ordinary explanation, and that is exactly why it needs holding* — and that she did it twice and that he has said it nine hundred times since. Elias does not read him. He wants to, and the wanting is on the page, and he puts his hands flat the way a man in a smock taught a boy to and takes them off again. He asks **Nadia Kest** to write a custody line with the time on it and her signature under it — 11:26 — because in eleven days it will be a question. The bundle is stamped **received 08:52**; Niko Senn walked in at 09:05.
26. **Ch 26 — off file.** Saturday 6 October, the Sequence Desk from eight, the car park at twenty to one. **DS Paul Rennick** has read the whole file on a corner of a desk with his coat on and tells Elias the three things he is entitled to say and no more. The **retention review** has one box ticked — *other: connected certified record, application pending* — and the word misconduct is not on the page; **Nadia Kest signed it at five to four on Friday and it took effect at forty to twelve**, and she says the ground out loud so he has heard it from her. The application was **served by hand at 11:40 on Friday**, signed for at the second floor of 14 Kettle Rows. Orr's office declines in one paragraph — the archive reports sequence positions, a form category is a publishing convention, and you may not reapply for twelve months without the Deputy Chief's written authority. **The Tribunal archive reported on Friday to Deputy Chief Orr's office**, a clerk opened the 2017 file on Thursday alone in a room, and **Nadia Kest is the reason it is not Elias** and says so. He does the arithmetic on 29 September standing up: at eleven he was at a wire rack buying a concordance; at half past one a woman read a man a line off a sheet; at about a quarter to two he filed the note whose item 8 says he had never read the office's name. The out-of-hours magistrate's **refusal** has sat unopened since Friday afternoon: the application does not name the premises, the material does not connect to anything provable, it is refused as presented, and *a refusal in the same terms is barred* — and Nadia will not name the address now either. In the car park she says **yes**, the department is better off without him on the file, and then says the rest of it in four minutes, and warns him that Rennick will get an account out of his own office inside nine days. He takes his notebook off the desk and Priya Sandoval says *all right* and asks nothing. **The board is washed. Three columns in marker, a case number, and a street he has never heard of.**
27. **Ch 27 — the back room.** Sunday 7 October, 14 Kettle Rows, from nine into the night. **Otto Vahl** takes his radio bench out of the back room to the front window, and the reason he gives is the cat, and the tea urn changes ownership. **Halvard Reisz** on the stairs asks whether the file touches the shop and is told no and then told the rest. Elias reads **Part Three end to end** and finds **Module 7, Accounts of Exposure**: *Where the account given is an account of a working the person has been exposed to, the exposure is the anchor, and the order the person is asked to hold is the order the person gives. The office does not supply the order*; *the manual does not describe a method of distinguishing the order perceived from the order expected, because no such method is established*; and on page five, *there is no evidence on the file that there was a previous account.* **Mara Quill** comes to the shop for the first time in six years, because the laboratory and room two are both closed to one of them, and makes him say the names out loud: **Niko Senn, Sana Okafor, Jules Ardent, Marguerite Osei** — and then says he has missed one, and that the harm to her needs no name.
