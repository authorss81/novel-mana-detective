# Volume 02 Review Notes — the honest defects

**Written at the volume close, after Chapter 100 shipped. Nothing here defends anything.** These are the things a reviewer should be told plainly, in the order a reviewer will hit them. **No chapter was reopened for this file and no prose was changed by it; the manuscript is the manuscript, and where a state file and the prose disagreed, the state file is what this pass corrected.**

**One thing to know before reading: the last batch of this volume went through three review-and-repair passes — and the second pass found real defects in the first, including a missing next-phase prompt and four state-file claims that contradicted the prose they were describing. Volume 01's last batch went through two. That history is the single most useful thing in this file, and section 2 is why.**

**Where the batch sat against its card band, honestly: over in four batches out of five, and over in a way that tracks what the arc is doing.** The two arcs that carry documents are the two longest, the arc that carries the midpoint reversal is the one that ran over without saying so, and the one batch that fits entirely is the one that is eleven people in a canteen and a drawer in a clinic. That is a pattern, not a coincidence, and it is the most useful single fact about the shape of this volume: **a chapter in which a woman reads a line out of a form runs long, and a chapter in which two people talk in a room does not.**

**Which expansion worked, every time: a scene at a real seam.** A canvas holdall on a bench; a buff store request and a storekeeper of thirty-one years; a linen door at a handover; a coin laundry on a Saturday morning; a darkroom and a box camera; a barrow and a blanket in a municipal yard; a front room, a landing and nine hours on a bench; a canteen at nine at night with nine people in it; a green book, a shop counter, a stair and a first-floor room; a district nurse's back room and a Sunday step. **The expansion that did not work, in this volume as in Volume 01, was restyling a paragraph.** Nothing here is padded and nothing here needs cutting for its own sake.

**Chapter 99 at 6,487 words is the volume's real length problem and it is disclosed rather than solved.** It carries the whole climax: two hours and a half of a raid in an empty room, the eleven going up a stair past a police officer, the declaration, the false start, rule (c), the ward four miles away, the counterturn, the Return, the destruction of the frame, the chain, the counterfoils, the cost and the closing image. **It grew because a review found the first draft had the raid entering an empty room at half past five when the room is not opened until eight** — which is the correct reason and also the inconvenient one. It will not be cut without removing one of those. **A reader who wants the volume trimmed will want to trim here, and this file says the chapter will survive being long but not being cut.**

**Complete as they stand, and not to be reopened:** Chapters 51, 52, 56, 57, 58, 59, 60, 61, 62, 63, 65, 66, 67, 69, 71, 72, 73, 74, 76, 77, 78, 79, 80, 81, 82, 83, 85, 86, 91, 93, 94, 95, 96, 97, 98, 100. **Chapters 53, 59, 64, 70, 87, 88, 89, 90, 92 and 99 are the ten a cold reader should be asked about. Section 4 covers the five in the last ten chapters; the five in Arcs A, B and D are the four over-long ones plus Chapter 70, and each of them is over because a document is being read aloud in a room, which is the volume's one reliable way of making itself long.**

## 2. The three rules this volume had to learn the hard way, and where they nearly recurred

**(a) A state file that asserts a fact the prose does not support is worse than no state file.** This volume produced the worst instance of the failure in the whole manuscript, and it was in a state file, not in a chapter. **`state/batch-0010-summary.md` carries, in its SECOND REPAIR PASS section, the claim that the doctor wrote her eleven names on Monday evening off a drawing of eleven chairs that a man read out of a coat pocket in a shop. The prose says the opposite and the third pass fixed it: the eleven names come from eleven she has carried in her own head since about 2021, she has never seen the drawing, and there is no telephone call between the member of the public and the doctor anywhere in Arc E.** Two further state files carried the wrong version, and `state/character-state.md` had already grown a second wrong claim on top of it — that her eleven names cover nine of the thirteen because the other four are the two patients, the man with the keys and the nurse. The true reason is different and less tidy: the two who could not say their own name are on nobody's list, and two of her eleven names are for people who have been in her building in nineteen years and have not been in that room. **All three are corrected in this pass, and the batch summary keeps the wrong version because it is the record of what was wrong. A writer who reads that file must read to the end of it.**

**Where it nearly recurred, four more times, all of them found:** the arrest times, which were three different sets across three files; the interval between the parade building and the licensed ward, which was twenty minutes in one file and forty in the chapter; the number of people who had been in that room, which was eleven, then twelve, then thirteen, and the file that had corrected it to twelve had its own justification that added up to eleven; and the four people who held the room, which one file listed as including Mara Quill, who says the word and holds nothing. **Every one of the four was a state-file error and not a prose error, and three of the four were found by a reviewer who was checking arithmetic rather than reading fiction.**

**(b) A count introduced in one place must be enumerated on the page and not asserted.** The eleven chairs were a drawing. The people who come to the room are eleven. **The number of people who have been in that room is thirteen, and Chapter 99 now says so in a sentence that names the two a drawing cannot hold and says why neither of them came to be worked on.** This rule is not bookkeeping. It is the difference between a number a reader can check and a number they have to trust, and the volume is built on numbers a reader can check.

**Where it nearly recurred: three times, and the third was the worst.** The first was the split of the eleven, which for a while was recorded as three named, four named, five named across three files. The second was the count itself. The third was a count of hostel beds — **four of them, stated as full, in a pay-point column of a thread file — and no chapter anywhere counts how many of that hostel's beds were full.** A state file may not supply a figure the prose has not put on the page. That one is now removed.

**(c) A repair pass that changes prose must sweep the state files in the same pass, because the state files are what the next writer believes.** The second pass of batch 0010 exists because the first pass fixed the prose and left four state files describing the prose that had been, plus **no next-phase prompt at all, in a batch that finished a volume.** A repair pass that touches Chapters 91–100 and does not touch `state/current.md`, `state/open-threads.md`, `state/character-state.md`, `state/chapter-summaries.md`, `bible/terminology.md`, `bible/characters.md`, `bible/power-system.md` and the five batch summaries has not finished.

**Where it nearly recurred, inside the repair passes themselves:** the first pass moved the number in Chapter 99 and left twelve standing in the bible and the thread file. The second pass found it and corrected it. The third pass found four state-file claims that contradicted the prose and corrected them in the same edit. **In other words: the first pass created the error the second pass had to clean up, and the second pass created one the third had to clean up. Two of the three passes in this batch's history made the state files worse before they made them better, and the reason is that the prose is read by everybody and the state files are read by whoever is unlucky.**

## 3. Known soft spots

- **The thirteen is a small number doing a very large job, and it is load-bearing on one doctor's line.** The volume's whole moral close is a woman saying *I counted eleven chairs, and I have eleven names, and I did not ask one of them.* For that to be the ending, the reader has to believe that the drawing and the room and the people in it are three different things, and that the count of people is a different number from the count of chairs, and that a doctor who has carried eleven names in her head for nine years has a list that is not a record. **That is a two-paragraph argument and it is carried by two characters, one of whom is on the page for a total of about a page and a half.** If a reader does not feel it, the volume's ending is a woman being efficient. This is the most fragile thing in the last ten chapters and it cannot be repaired by adding material; it can only be repaired by a Volume 03 that keeps paying it.

- **The sergeant of twenty-nine years is the volume's largest new emotional beat and it rests on an officer with no name.** She is the best thing in Chapters 99 and 100: she does not tell him to stop, she puts herself between his hand and a door, she opens it eleven times, and on the twelfth time he gets his own hand open and stops, and neither of them says a word. **A reader who wants to know her name will want it badly, and she has not got one, on purpose, because naming her would turn the volume's most restrained gesture into a character.** The cost is real and this file states it: **a Volume 03 writer who needs a policewoman on her side will have to invent her, and everything she does in this volume is a gift to whoever gives her a name later. That is a debt with interest.** The rule stands: no name without a reason on the page, and the reason has to be better than a name.

- **The Chapter 100 question is now spent, and it cannot be asked again in the same words.** It was asked once, in an office, in its institutional form, and it was not answered, and the non-answer is the finding. **That is a good ending and it is also a trap.** The question has four offices, a claimant, an issuing office, a schedule number and a form of words in print behind it, and a Volume 03 writer who restates it as a question has spent the volume's best sentence for nothing. **Volume 03 must convert it into a different question rather than repeat it** — that is, the question has to change shape, not be re-asked, and the change has to cost something or it is the same question in a new coat.

- **The eleven objects from Volume 01 are still unidentified.** One of them has eleven letters scratched in its lid and they are not transcribed. **They are a promise of about forty chapters and this volume did not touch them beyond one competent civilian examiner refusing to transcribe them for the correct professional reason.** That refusal is the best thing Volume 01 did with them and Volume 02 correctly did not undo it. A writer opening Volume 03 will be tempted to identify the tin in chapter three. **Do not.**

- **The ward on the Wexford Row premises is a live wrong that nobody has been charged for.** A police inspector entered a licensed building at half past ten at night with no instrument of any kind and stood in a room with three people in it for an hour and ten minutes, and she is right, and her name is at the top of the report, and she asked a magistrate to minute that she had not applied for a warrant. **Three people were worked on in that room on the night and nobody has been told anything and nobody has been charged.** That is the most uncomfortable thing the volume leaves on the table and it is deliberate. It is also a thing a reader may reasonably feel the book has simply not dealt with, and a Volume 03 that touches the ward owes the reader an accounting of the three in it, beginning with **Doris Sowerby, 81, who was asleep and awake at the same time and sat up at ten to ten when a sheet of paper came apart four miles away and does not know that yet.**

- **Two of the volume's three biggest set pieces are about documents and one is about a room of frightened people, and only one of the three could have been cut without loss.** That is a fair balance and it should not be corrected. It is in this file because a reader who wants the volume to be about people has to notice that the volume is about a product.

## 4. What a reviewer should look at first in a cold read of Chapters 91–100

In this order, with no knowledge of the state files:

1. **Chapter 99, the two hours and a half of waiting, from the sentence *They waited in a first-floor room above a shop on a parade in Kettle Rows for two hours and ten minutes* to the bell.** This is the passage a review created and it is the most likely thing in the volume to read as padding. **It is not padding and it is the chapter's best writing**, because it is the only place in the book where five people who are about to do something terrible are shown waiting. Check that the last hour of it earns the last hour of Chapter 88.
2. **Chapter 99, the counterturn, from *He put his hand out in front of the fourth chair* to *and the eleven numbers and the eleven figures went with it*.** He puts his body between a frightened woman and a room "because he did not think about it." Check that the act is not a victory speech: rule (c) has already been said, in his own words, eleven minutes earlier, and the counterturn itself is four hundred words of a man failing to describe something.
3. **Chapter 99, the paragraph that enumerates the thirteen, and the doctor's answer to it.** This is the load-bearing paragraph of the whole volume. **Check it against the stair, where ten come up and one is already in the room: a reader who counts the people on the stair and the people in the chairs will get eleven and will be told thirteen, and the thirteen has to be obviously right.**
4. **Chapter 99, the sergeant of twenty-nine years, and the four minutes after it.** The beat is about four hundred words and there is no name in it and no thanks. **Check that the prose earns the refusal to say anything.** If it reads as a withhold rather than a gift, the choice was wrong and the chapter needs a different kind of sentence.
5. **Chapters 99 and 100 together, the last page of the volume.** Chapter 99 ends on a woman of thirty-eight saying her own name twice into the rain. Chapter 100 ends on a woman of thirty-eight getting up out of a chair by herself. **These are the same gesture in two directions and they are nine pages apart; that is the design, and it is also the most likely thing in the volume to read as a repeated ending.**
6. **Chapter 100, the settlement sheet and the printed line, and the question after it.** The line is the best sentence in the volume and it is a printed line on a form, and the risk is that the chapter stops being a scene and becomes an argument. Check that Imelda Szabo is a person: she picks up her tea, finds it cold, and puts it down again.
7. **Chapter 100, the waiting room, last four hundred words.** The board, the four kinds of paper, the nine chairs, the book of her own, the third line about the third box, and the woman getting up. **Check the count: nine chairs, and the man counts them on the step and again from the inside and it is nine both times, and he is aware of the number the whole time.** If the nine reads as a stray detail it has failed, because it is the cost of the climax stated once and never explained.
8. **Chapter 97, the shop counter, ten minutes, two customers.** The second panel of the arc is on a sheet of A4 lying flat in a shop and it is the only place in the volume where a panel is read in daylight by strangers. **Check that nobody in that shop touches the paper and that nobody photographs it, because the moment either happens the frame stops being a man's and starts being evidence.**
9. **Chapter 92, the stair, seven people on eleven steps at four in the afternoon.** The woman who gives her name at ten past four is the first time a person in this book says their own name to a stranger on purpose, and it happens in one line. **Check that nothing before it has prepared it.**
10. **Any chapter in which a document does the work of a character.** There are five: the lapsed form in Chapter 91, the refusal in Chapter 93, the schedule in Chapter 98, the settlement sheet in Chapter 100, and the charge sheet's negative. **The negative is the one to check, because a charge sheet that says *not charged with any other offence* is a document nobody has ever written before in this city, and it is announced in a corridor at ten past seven on a Wednesday morning by a woman who has three names to read out.**

It is a bootstrap spec file and it was not in the scope of this close, so the close left it alone — **but it is a factual claim that was wrong, and this phase exists because wrong factual claims in files people believe are the failure mode it was written to stop.** It now says that all five arcs are written and Volume 02 is closed at Chapter 100, names the Arc D and Arc E handoffs alongside the other three, and states that no batch is queued and that **Volume 03 may not begin until a Volume 03 outline exists, which is its own phase.**
- **The first and second repair passes of batch 0010 each corrected prose and each left state files behind them.** That is section 2(c) and it is the reason this volume shipped through three passes on its last batch alone. **It is not a reason to distrust the chapters. It is a reason to distrust the notes, and the notes have now been swept.**
