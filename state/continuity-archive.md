## World facts established in the manuscript during batch 0001

- **Rehabilitation account 44-9.** An account that is a legal person, with an address on Verge Row, a card, and a return clause on any tool issued against it. It cannot book a patient into the Kettle Rows Community Clinic address, so it reaches people by **merging files**: the patient's record is joined to the account's record and the account's own address becomes the patient's address of record. This is how a transfer order became legal on its face without anybody at the clinic having agreed to anything.
- **A salt-glass plate is not admissible unless it is on a Concord register**, and Mara Quill holds no Concord licence and has decided not to. Her material is the testimony of an uncertified forensic mage. This is her standing limitation and it must not be quietly solved.

What it arrived on — tool, card, coin, token, bead — is not knowable at First Margin. **Seam rule:** the same joint failing the same way in two different objects is a specification, not an accident.
- **A transfer of place of residence can carry a sequence box** with four acts in it, and a certified sequence can be attached to a document that is supposed to be housekeeping. A certificate is a document with chosen words; the Registry's public line and its draft certificate say different things, and both are already written. **A Registry certificate is stamped before its subject document exists.** That is the batch's cleanest piece of institutional evidence and it rests on nothing but two system timestamps.

- **Where Niko Senn learned the outbox number.** He said *nineteen sixty-five* to a registrar at ten past eleven on Friday morning, before the order was in front of either of them. **The source is the Registry's own telephone call to him on Sunday night** — the one in which a woman offered to stabilise his account.
