# Character State

**Updated after Volume 01, batch 0002 (Chapters 11–20).** For the writer's handoff only. The authoritative descriptions live in `bible/characters.md`; this file records where each person physically and emotionally is at the end of Chapter 20, and what they now know that they did not know at the start of the batch.

## Elias Venn

- **Where:** Kettle Rows, and — from about Monday — **not on the Kettle Rows case.** He has lodged, in his own hand, an application for access to the certified Morrow Street record, and field eleven of that form says the Kettle Rows matter, so the Kettle Rows matter is about to go to somebody else. He is, on paper, a man with ten days to prepare a review board he intends to attend as a member of the public.
- **Body:** unchanged and unrepaired. Left-ear loss permanent, the whole left side gone rather than muffled; he still sits on people's right-hand side and still cannot locate a sound without watching a mouth. **No tinnitus and none in this volume.** **No reading in the batch and no panel, and no nosebleed** — the four Arc A readings remain spent. He has slept about four hours a night, he has eaten at the two times somebody made him, and he has a day book with five timed lines in it that is now the only honest record of what Friday night cost his own building. He has started washing his hands and logging his own name before touching anything, which started on Monday and is now permanent, and he has started writing things on the back of his own hand and not scrubbing them off, which is new and is the volume's quietest gain.
- **Knows:** that a district can be made cheaper to insure on purpose and then left unprotected because of it; that the network **answers** ward failures instead of hiding them, and that this is how it knows where the grid is already blinking; that a template travels as a die, a sheet of foil, a length of channel and a printed instruction that ends *confirm the patient is in the room*; that a supplier can hold a licence whose only content is that he may sell and may not use; and that **the fourth act is a shelf number** — SEQ 4(c) — with a printed definition that says entry does not assert a release happened, only that the record is to be treated as though it had, for the purposes of that record only.
- **Wrong about, still:** the thing he read standing back from a counter on Friday the 21st was a **printed box**, and he spent a week reading it as a working. Mara Quill corrected him on the ninth and Chapter 15 has him say the correction out loud in front of a grieving woman, which is the first time he has told anybody else.
- **Gave up, this batch:** the photograph (he does not trace it and does not ask anyone to); the countersignature, permanently; the comparison of the seized die against the stamp on Mara's plate, twice, at real cost; and the Kettle Rows case itself, in a field, in his own handwriting.
- **Wants:** Thursday the fourth of October. A plate that is not on a register and a man who will not put his name on her figures. Niko Senn to keep the card in his pocket.
- **Cannot do:** interrupt a working, read a living trace, touch a sample, offer a reading to a court, or open a red box on a shelf without ending the case he is holding.
- **The lie, updated:** he no longer believes one private certainty will save anybody, and he has stopped believing that his own refusal is one. He has written a prohibition down as a document, learned that a labelled gap is worth more than a reconstruction, and twice refused a measurement that would have won him a name.

## Mara Quill

- **Where:** Saint Orra Reach residue laboratory, two rooms behind the linen office. Still holds both plates, both in her name.
- **Body:** no injury. She has not slept properly since the Wednesday and has stopped pretending she is going to.
- **Holds:** two salt-glass plates; the foil stencil, which did not move; the note of Wednesday the nineteenth about herself, which is now in a Concord file; and **a certified copy of four of her figures in a drawer in Concord Hall she cannot open**, which is the first time in six years that her material exists outside her own building.
- **Did, in public, on Thursday the 27th:** certified the **measurements** and refused, in writing and on the minute, to certify that the act was copied, that anybody had a motive, or that anything of hers should go on a register. Refused to hand the stencil to a registered facility, and asked for the refusal to be minuted word for word, *because a refusal that nobody wrote down did not happen.* She understands immediately what that costs: her refusal is now, in somebody else's hands, a reason to want the stencil.
- **Said out loud, once:** her brother's name. **Iven Quill.** Six years and seventeen days (he went in on 14 October; she says it on Thursday 27 September). No lead attached, no fact asserted, and she told him why she was telling him and then told him not to ask.
- **Will not:** let him touch a living trace, the plate, room two, or the laboratory this week. Will not soften the record. Will not take a Concord licence and has now said so in a minute.
- **Pressure:** her standing is the ceiling and the ceiling is now being used on her. A plate that is not on a register is the testimony of an uncertified mage, and the Concord's own office has put that sentence in a letter.
- **Wants:** a record that nobody can own. She is further along than she was and less protected.

## Nadia Kest

- **Where:** the Sequence Desk, and a car.
- **Has done:** got a conditional entry with a magistrate's note recording the thirteen minutes between her telephone warrant and her entry, with her name under it; written four hundred words for a Deputy Chief and sent them herself; referred the Morrow Street file to the Tribunal archive at the Deputy Chief's authority, which is the correct act and will open the old case without Elias and without her; written a note in 441-KR so that Niko Senn's discharge has a shape; affirmed the retention of a resident detective on the record, with her name, in front of a panel; disclosed the countersigned slip, its annex and the reply to Elias on Saturday, in front of a witness, three days before the deadline she set for herself; and received his application for access to the Morrow Street record by hand, logged, at about seventeen hundred on Sunday the 30th.
- **Holding:** a four-hundred-word summary she sent and a line she deliberately did not write, which she has been carrying since Saturday morning and which she told him in a car on Sunday afternoon and told him she wanted to be extremely angry with her about.
- **Believes:** institutions can be saved by the right chain of command, and this batch taught her what that costs when the chain of command is a Deputy Chief who wanted a date on a form. She also learned that protecting somebody by withholding from them is not a kindness, and she did it anyway, and she has asked to be argued with rather than admired.
- **Wants:** Elias kept and usable until Thursday the fourth of October, and the disclosure routed to a named person rather than a circulation.

## Dr Sana Okafor

- **Where:** the Kettle Rows Community Clinic, a former paint works on Marshall Street, a corridor with a waiting-room board on it that is now mostly her own maintenance book.
- **Age:** 47, and twenty-two of those years a doctor. She will be forty-eight next year. Use one number for both.
- **Has done:** filed the clinical hold that stopped a transfer; answered a Council consultation with **a hundred** people at risk in a building that holds twenty-two chairs, because a hundred is the number that stops a van turning into that yard; refused to give the police one page of her maintenance book and **published all forty-one months of it instead**, in her own ink, on her own wall, so that no single page could ever be lifted out of it later; and been given, in return for the correct act, a production that adjourned her own review board.
- **Cost, and it was not money:** the clinic's own untorqued bolt, slow trap, non-compliant landing light and eleven-month-old water heater are now public. The stockroom is still unusable, the door is still on one hinge, and the fire officer's first slot is Tuesday ten o'clock. A Concord assessor read her board at four feet on Friday afternoon without asking a single question.
- **Believes:** the only thing that stops an institution taking a person is other people knowing what it wrote down. She has now paid for that belief in her own reputation as a competent doctor, and she has said she would do it again.
- **Not yet, and will not be:** a witness, a source, or a document.

## Jules Ardent

- **Where:** nine Pell Street, above a dry cleaner. **There is a standard clinical practice notice on the door and a maker's enquiry open against his die block.** He is at Saint Orra or at his solicitor's rooms; he goes nowhere alone and he is not available from nine at night.
- **Age:** 29.
- **Did on Wednesday 26 September, at 09:45, and told nobody:** carried his own die block to the Tool Registry in a bag and asked Bess Yonkin to seize it, standing at her counter and reading the seizure form aloud twice. He did it because he worked out that the uncollected second set in his bench room is a thing people would come for, and in a bench room he would lose. **It worked, and it cost him the set as well:** a seized maker's block freezes everything stamped with it, and the second set is now inside an enquiry and cannot be moved by anybody, including him. He is the only physical link between himself and the supplier and he locked it himself.
- **Says, and Elias records it:** he did not tell Elias because Elias would have come to the counter and put a hand on the block, and Elias has not put a hand on anything since the nineteenth, and Jules was not going to be the reason he stopped.
- **Has not been charged.** He has two statements now, and Nadia has to explain the second to the Crown, and he is not cleared of anything.
- **Want:** to keep repairing the grid without giving anybody a private hold on it. **Fear:** that every practical compromise makes ordinary people more disposable. He has now made one himself.

## Niko Senn

- **Where:** the Kettle Rows Community Clinic, out of room two, and on Sunday 30 September waiting on a taxi at 17:10 for his aunt's address, having said the 18:30 bus is too late. **Room two is needed; he is not ill.** A review board sits **Thursday 4 October at 10:00** and he is going to miss it, because his aunt has no telephone and nobody has told him anything about it.
- **Status in every system that matters:** **discharged.** Account 44-9 marked non-attendance as discharge at 06:00 on Monday 24 September and closed the file. He is nobody's patient, he is unfindable in a continuity office's records, and there is a note in 441-KR saying so.
- **Knows:** that his account of the room is in the wrong order and that it is not because he is lying; that a man in a smock taught him a door for forty credits with fingers on the back of his neck; that there is a name for a treatment he never had and eleven days he never spent; and that a man of about forty in a good coat was at a register counter in the city this week and that a police sergeant did not follow him.
- **Holds, and kept:** the card. Three weeks in a wallet, a bent corner, a thumbprint, no Concord registration mark, and the smock man's own name written on the back of it in the room on his way out. **Elias refused to take it and refused the name**, and Niko Senn said the only thing anybody has said to him in ten days that was true: *you're the one who made them write it down.*
- **Do not:** turn him into a witness to the room, or into a file, or into a man who gave a card to the police.
