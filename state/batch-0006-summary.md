## What the arc did

- **Promise → escalation → turn → payoff.** 51: a seventy-eight-year-old man who lost his walking stick on 30 October has found something else to give away, and the answer to the question Otto Vahl has been carrying for three weeks is no. 52: **the most frightening document of the month is a price list** — a public annual schedule, three credits, fourteen memory procedures, and the volume's legal spine. 53: a woman of fifty-eight, unregistered six years, whose sheet cites a page of the manual that does not contain what is on it. 54: two hours and forty minutes in a client chair, eleven lines in a book, and a company on the fifteenth of October. 55: **three counterfoils, a claim number, a Schedule D service code, and a premises that is not hers.** 56: a doctor and a member of the public disagree correctly and she wins in a morning, and a protection order comes back with an end date on it. 57: four people in a queue at a claims counter, a car park, and an inspector who refuses to say a name in a car park for the best reason in the batch. 58: forty credits on a correct form and one condition accepted. 59: a working, a false start, a name for it given by somebody else, and a promise he cannot find. 60: the third box on a public wall, and a padlocked stair.
- **The volume's engine, on the page, in one sentence in a public book.** *A release in a working is not a fourth act. A fourth act is a release that is not the working's own.* Spoken aloud by Rhoda Nesbit to a sheet of polythene in a waiting room on 27 November, to a room of nineteen people, after four years of having a release in a sideboard and not saying the word.
- **The question half one, answered in the only form available, and not further.** The technique is bought, not taught: a printed sheet, a four-line card with a word in capitals at the foot, a counterfoil, a price list, and an envelope of cash. **Nobody in this batch is a teacher, because there is no teacher.** Desmond Coyle is still one man, one visit, one stick, charged 2 November. Orin Sable is not named, caught, faced, or joined to any document. The word in capitals at the foot of a card is `SUPPLY` and **it is a licence class, not a person**, and Bess Yonkin says the sentence the whole volume is built on: *a number is not a person, and that is the only sentence I have said in nineteen years that everybody in this building has needed and none of them have ever written down.*
- **Why care is the black market, stated in law and not in motive.** **Schedule D — memory and identity work, fourteen procedures, three credits, on the same wire rack by the same radiator as the Witness Training Compendium, three inches apart.** Procedure 10, *recovery of a name or date*, is twenty-six credits and unrestricted. **Tariff note 4: no claim may be made under Schedule D for a procedure at 2 to 14; a charge for such a procedure is not a claim and may not be settled by a third party.** So the practice is an offence in two particulars and no others — it is unregistered and it is undercharged — and that is why nobody prosecutes it and why nobody is frightened by it. **And tariff note 4 is two years old.** The 1927 printing has three notes, Schedule D stops at procedure 9, procedure 12 is not in the book at all, and the date on the 1929 revision is in ink and not a printer's mark, because *a printer marks a revision before he breaks the page.* The prohibition was added after the practice started and the practice got older and then got tighter, and the woman at the bottom of it was never told.
- **Question half two, converted from a suspicion into three documents, and not answered.** **Three counterfoils**, written by Rhoda Nesbit because she was a claims clerk for twelve years and it is what she was trained to do, with a claim number written across the top in a hand that leans right and presses hard: `RC-4471902/61`, `RC-5102884/61`, `RC-6180337/61`, dates **4 May 1927, 19 January 1928 and 11 September 1929**, service codes inside Schedule D, and a margin that says **22 Cutlass Row, 3rd fl** and nothing else. The only copy in the world is a negative in a member of the public's notebook, because a shop on Cutter Street will make one frame and wipes it at four in the morning, and there is no cloud and no index and no third print. **Aegis Mutual is not named in this batch. The batch's whole counter-argument is that the certificate, the number and the service code are genuine and there is no claim in existence for any of them, so the whole arrangement is a shape and not a fraud, and a shape is harder to argue with than a fraud.**
- **The three documents, and what each of them cannot reach.** The counterfoils (his). The three lines on the clinic board in a doctor's ink with her registration number under them (Sana Okafor's, and she gives him no say). The protection order, eleven unnamed premises, granted on her request, **14 November 1929 to 14 January 1930** (the end date is the point, and he does the arithmetic on the back of a receipt: fifty-five days, nine and a half weeks, and *on the fourteenth of January somebody in a municipal building is going to write a word on a form*).

## Power: one reading, one panel, no nosebleed, and a new cost

Chapter 59, first floor above a supermarket on Sallow Lane, **16:04 to 16:19**, contact on a sheet of paper on a table in a room with six people and a child in it. **He reads a letter and does not touch the child and says so out loud in front of a witness.** The order: **anchor** a letter in a child's hand about eleven years old, creased through the signature; **address** the nine-year-old on the floor with a book about a pony; **turn** done, properly, by somebody who knows the method; **release** seated, waiting for a signature on a line on a sheet on a table. **And the new thing: the order starts, stops about a fifth of the way in, and starts again from a different place, with the mark of a *begin* in the gap and not the mark of a middle.** The one panel:

> **MARGIN — The order starts, stops, and starts again from a different place. The gap is not a pause. Nothing in this room has a pause in it.**

**He cannot interrupt it and says so in front of a police officer for the second time in ninety days and the first time with a child in the room.** He does not attempt a counterturn — that is Arc D and it fails. What he does is name the order aloud in order so that somebody else can do the arithmetic, and **that is what stopped the working at 16:31**, along with two officers and a warrant signed at Vine Street at 14:00 on the strength of the protection order, which is the document Sana Okafor put her registration number to. **A release that is not committed is not a release.** The daughter keeps her name and loses her own recollection of the last four months of Sundays. **Denny Roche's own name does not come back**, and the chapter does not explain why and does not pretend to.

**Cost, new, and not spent again in this batch: a false memory of a conversation that has not happened.** He comes off the letter with a memory of a car park outside a building on Morrow Hill on a **Sunday**, in which Nadia Kest told him he was going to be taken off everything and he agreed never to read a living person again, and remembered her not arguing, and remembered putting his hand on the roof of a car and saying that if he ever did it he would want her to stop him. **The date is Sunday 16 December 1929. He writes it down on the twenty-sixth of November because a man who will not write the date down is a man who will decide later that it did not happen.** On 27 November he goes through four hundred and thirty pages twice and there is no car park on any Sunday and no such conversation, and **Nadia has no memory of it either and this batch does not resolve it.** It is the volume's standing cost, and it is a cost about the future rather than the past, which is the one thing this faculty has never taken from him.

## Fracture: a name, and no use for it

**Fracture is not delivered in this batch and the card says so.** Arc A gives him a false start and cannot use it.
