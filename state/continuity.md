## Canon established

- **Series:** *The Mana Detective*, 650 chapters, thirteen volumes of about fifty. Urban fantasy mystery / procedural. One slow-burn romance only: Elias Venn and Mara Quill. No harem.
- **Protagonist:** Detective Sergeant Elias Venn, 36 at the opening, Sequence Desk, Veyra Civic Police. A narrow apartment above his repair shop in Kettle Rows. Hearing loss in his left ear. The faculty is **Sequence Sight**, which he experiences as the **Margin**.
- **First Margin (Volume 1 only):** perceives the causal order of a fresh working after contact or entry. Never motive, identity, intent, or the future. Cannot safely read a living trace. Cannot interrupt a working. Cannot tell copywork from a genuine sequence on his own — he can see a release standing where nobody performed one and can notice the same act recurring, but the diagnosis of *copy* and the seam belong to Mara's instruments. Costs dizziness, nausea, lost minutes, sometimes a nosebleed; vary what each reading takes from him. **In Volume 01 the nosebleed is Chapter 10's cost and no other chapter's, and no two readings may take the same thing.**
- **Sequence order:** anchor → address → turn → release. The historical **fourth act** is a fabricated release *inserted* after those three by the first post-Blackline correction. The victim's original release is the act that goes missing. Do not call a normal malfunction a fourth act.
- **The unfinished act:** the first correction was a real emergency act, and its **central release was left incomplete**. The final antagonist is that established pattern — the **First Correction**, the citywide working assembled from the proof weave, Quiet Grid, living anchors, and old records, with Iona Saye as its human steward. No new final enemy may be introduced.
- **Ages that must reconcile:** Hollis Ward is late fifties at the opening, twenty at the Blackline Event 38 years ago. Teodor Rusk is dead before the opening; the Morrow Street collapse killed twenty-three. Iven Quill has been missing six years.
- **Institutions:** Veyra Civic Police / Sequence Desk; the Civic Concord (Licensing, Ward Works, Clinical Practice, Proof Registry); Proof Tribunal; Veyra Civic Council; the Quiet Grid; the Veyran Vault; Lantern Compact; Aegis Mutual; the Ninefold Assembly; the Quiet Hands. The Civic slogan is "No consequence without a witness."

- **Niko Senn** is the intended target, the witness, and the patient at risk. Name him; never revert to an unnamed patient.

- **Tamsin Ro** (night-bus dispatcher) is the first victim; Jules Ardent is the mechanical suspect who becomes a reluctant ally.
- Batch ends with: a broken second frame, a refused certificate signature, a patient who is safe for now, and an open file. No certificate issued, no witness omitted, no reprimand, no arrest, no volume-climax working.
- **The four-reading contract is fixed.** Elias reads in Chapters 1, 3, 6, and 10 only, once each, with a different cost each time: four minutes and his balance; his hearing and a block of the afternoon; food and his right to handle the clamp; his legs, forty minutes, and the volume's only nosebleed. No chapter may add a fifth reading by letting him recognise a working through contact, and no two readings may cost the same thing.
- **The Chapter 9 recognition is not a reading.** It runs on Mara's instrument numbers, her comparison across the three samples, and the sequence printed on Niko's transfer order. He touches nothing, including the Chapter 2 plate, whose sleeve he signs; the only cost is standing.
- **Sample custody is fixed and must not drift.** There are two salt-glass plates: the Chapter 2 threshold plate Mara lifts in front of Elias and the inspector, carrying the coarse seam that proved the release was assembled, and a second plate lifted in Chapter 10 at Saint Orra Reach holding the stockroom carrier's two broken halves, the foil template bonded inside its tooth channel, the graded stamp marks, and a **second, finer seam** — the same defect at a smaller scale, which is the evidence that the frames are graded. Nadia signs the objects over to Mara as a transfer, not a seizure. Neither plate is a certificate; neither enters the proof weave in this volume.
- **The foil template** the Chapter 10 crew came to remove is a stencil of the stolen release, not a working, and holds no charge of its own. It survives the break — the crew leaves with a measurement of it rather than the template — and it lives on the second plate at Saint Orra Reach. One location only. It is the only object in the batch linking the first two frames to one workshop.
- **The Jules Ardent clamp** is documented rather than seized and lives in Mara's laboratory, which is the only reason Elias may handle it. His unlogged touch in Chapter 6 contaminates it permanently; he does not touch it again in Volume 01. **The loan also falls due at midnight, so the clamp leaves Mara's laboratory during Chapter 6** and is back with the account it was lent to for the rest of the batch. That clause is what makes the Chapter 6 comparison a deadline. Only Mara's written numbers survive it, which is why Chapter 9 runs on paper and Chapter 10 has nothing to check against.
