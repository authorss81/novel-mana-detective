# The Mana Detective — Terminology Bible

**Status:** Bootstrap canon. This glossary fixes names and ordinary usage so later chapters do not drift into generic game language or unexplained fantasy jargon.

## Core magical terms

### Mana

The public word for ambient magical capacity. It gathers in water, metal, living tissue, and old ward-stone. It is not visible as a color, not stored in a body as a numeric total, and not the same thing as a spell. Characters use “mana” casually; formal documents usually say “working charge” or “current.”

### Current

A local concentration of mana through which a working draws and settles. Currents are uneven and recover slowly. A current is not a character, and it does not speak or want anything.

### Drift

The temporary or lasting instability caused by overworking a current or a proof weave. Symptoms include ward flicker, unstable records, sensory disagreement, and emotional flattening of recent memories. Drift is a social and material condition, not just a magical status effect.

### Working

A deliberate sequence of actions that changes reality. “Spell” is common in conversation. “Working” is the formal term used by practitioners, courts, and the Civic Concord.

### Sequence

The causal order in which a working's changes became possible and were committed. A sequence is not a video, a set of gestures, or a complete account of motive. It can contain false starts, folds, omissions, and a release that occurs in the wrong order.

### Anchor

The object, place, person, record, or social obligation to which a working is bound. The anchor determines what a working can touch and how a consequence returns. A living target can remember contact, which makes it a strong and dangerous anchor.

### Address

The part of a working that chooses the target and route of the effect. Addressing a lock, a threshold, and a person are different choices even when they seem to describe the same door.

### Turn

The act that changes the permitted state of the target. A turn may stop a fall, open a bounded passage, alter sensation, or commit a false memory. The word is technical; characters rarely use it in ordinary speech.

### Release

The act that commits and closes a working. A missing, premature, or duplicated release can leave a live or backtracking sequence; those failures are not the historical fourth act unless a correction inserts a release after an anchor, address, and turn.

### Fold

An optional act that hides, redirects, or compresses part of a working. A fold can make an attack appear to happen in the wrong place.

### Witness

A person or a carefully prepared record used to stabilize and certify a working. A witness is not automatically a source of truth. The Civic Concord's official witness training is the target of much of the central mystery.

A **witness-binding** is the Concord's technique for stabilizing a witness's account: it aligns the person's inner trace to the residue so the account can be certified. It is genuine, widely used, and does not lie about what happened. What it does is **take the account out of the witness's own hands** — the witness can no longer revise it, and cannot testify to having revised it either. Whether that is permanent is not established in the text, and practitioners will not say, because asking is how you find out that they already have. It is offered as a kindness and accepted as a service, and it is dangerous to use on any witness whose order may have been given to them rather than lived by them. The formal proof-weave witness act is defined in `bible/power-system.md`; the batch 0001 note on duration and reversibility is a drafting addition and is recorded here so later chapters do not contradict it.

### Return

An optional consequence that travels back along a working's connection. A returned consequence travels to the nearest connected anchor that can contain it; if no anchor can contain it, the nearest living body—or the practitioner—absorbs the consequence. A counterturn can force this consequence into an uncommitted state.

### Residue

The physical or energetic trace a working leaves behind. Residue can preserve order and contact, but it rarely preserves motive. Its duration depends on the material and the strength of the working.

### Inner trace

A living person's causal record after magical exposure. It can be affected by injury, stress, and false rehearsal. It is not a full memory and cannot be treated as a transcript.

### Sequence Sight

The formal name for Elias's rare faculty: perceiving the order of a working after a magical change. The Sequence Sight is not a camera, a lie detector, or a source of motive. Elias experiences it as the **Margin**, a short sensory pressure in which order and gaps become perceptible.

### Margin

The private sensory form of Elias's Sequence Sight. It gives causal order and sometimes a gap, never identity or motive. It is not a floating game interface.

### Counterturn

A bounded interruption that inserts an uncommitted act into another working. It is a baseline technique for trained practitioners, but it requires an understood sequence, a physical anchor, and someone ready to carry the Return. Elias first attempts it at his second power stage and can use it reliably with a team only at his sixth. It stops or redirects; it does not automatically undo a crime or command a practitioner.

### Copywork

An illegal replay of a prior working using a source anchor and a working frame. It is expensive and usually leaves a seam where the copied order failed to create a live change of its own.

### Seam

A physical or causal discontinuity in copywork or a correction. An early Elias may not recognize it; later, the seam is a warning that the sequence has been assembled rather than performed as a single original working.

### False rehearsal

A conditioning method that teaches a living target to carry a different causal order in an inner trace. It can fool an untrained Margin reader for a time, especially when paired with a real source anchor. It cannot create a physical event by itself.

### Proof weave

The Civic Concord's citywide system for aligning residue, witness memory, paper and digital records, and the Quiet Grid. It produces certificates used by police, courts, insurers, and infrastructure offices.

### Certificate

A formal record stating that a working has been checked against a standard anchor and witness. A certificate is powerful evidence, not reality itself. Its maker and exclusions matter.

### Correction

A working that inserts a missing act into a record so incompatible sequences can be reconciled. In the central historical case, the inserted act is a fabricated **release**: the fourth act after an anchor, address, and turn. The victim's original release is the act that is missing; the correction supplies a substitute so the record appears complete. The first correction was a post-Blackline emergency measure whose central release was deliberately left incomplete. Quiet Hands later use smaller corrections to redirect blame, memory, and consequences, and their final plan is to complete that unfinished release citywide.

### Working frame

A physical device that carries a copied sequence or stabilizes a live working. Frames are made from mundane and magical materials; they are not portals and do not create effects without an anchor.

## Institutions and places

### Veyra

The modern delta city-state where the series takes place. It is a functioning city with ordinary public services, not a magical kingdom.

### Civic Concord

The public authority responsible for ward maintenance, practitioner licensing, clinical standards, and the Proof Registry. Its official work protects Veyra; its centralized certification also creates the central danger.

### Proof Tribunal

The specialist court for dangerous workings, magical injury, unlicensed practice, and sequence disputes. A Tribunal ruling can be binding even when the underlying record is incomplete.

### Veyra Civic Council

The twelve-member elected municipal government. It sets the city budget, ratifies ward projects and licensing budgets, and votes on a Safety Charter. It does not certify magical evidence directly. After the final reform, its emergency orders and contracts are public and subject to appeal.

### Quiet Grid

The citywide infrastructure that stabilizes Veyra's ambient currents. It is not sentient. Its failures are physical, administrative, and political.

### Veyran Vault

The sealed convergence chamber beneath Morrow Hill where the Blackline Event began and where the First Correction's central anchor is held. It is an established location, not a late secret fortress.

### Civic Police

Veyra's ordinary police force. The Sequence Desk is a small specialized unit within the Serious Crimes Unit.

### Ward Reauthorization Review

A scheduled public review of the Quiet Grid's decade-long operating authority. This is the first review after two consecutive seasons of ward failures; earlier decade certifications were extended administratively while the Quiet Hands controlled the records. It can approve continued central certification or require a new oversight structure. The Quiet Hands plan to use it to complete the First Correction's unfinished central release.

### Morrow Hill

The civic and financial center of Veyra. It contains Concord Hall, the Proof Tribunal, police headquarters, and older ward structures.

### Kettle Rows

Dense working-class neighborhoods, repair shops, markets, clinics, and mutual-aid networks. Elias grew up there.

### Saint Orra Reach

The hospital and medical campus where Mara's independent residue laboratory is based.

### Saltmarket

The port, wholesale, and cold-storage district where magical components and illegal frames move through ordinary commerce.

### The Cut

A network of old tunnels, drainage channels, service vaults, and abandoned rail works beneath the city. It is a lived-in legal and physical space, not a monster dungeon.

### Aegis Mutual

The largest insurer in the opening volumes. It uses certified magical sequences to deny or delay claims, and its risk assessments can make a district too expensive to protect. It is a Lantern Compact member, not an independent magical authority.

### Ninefold Assembly

The regional body of nine river-corridor government seats. It is older than the Quiet Hands and distinct from the nine institutional anchors that support the central network.

### Lantern Compact

A public coalition of insurers, private clinics, guilds, contractors, developers, and councilors who depend on predictable magical risk.

### Quiet Hands

A clandestine network that uses copywork, false rehearsal, and corrections to protect a curated public reality. Its members have different motives, but they share the belief that controlled forgetting prevents worse harm.

### The First Correction

The post-Blackline working pattern that supplied the first fabricated release and left a central completion unfinished. Its later citywide completion is the planned structural final adversary: not a new villain, but the established pattern returning as a last attempt to control the city.

## Terms and places added while drafting (Volume 01, batch 0001)

Canon recorded here as it was established in prose. No power rule changes.

- **14 Kettle Rows** — the four-storey Kettle Rows building whose ground floor is Elias's repair shop. Elias's apartment is the second floor; Tamsin Ro rented the top room, and Otto Vahl has the ground floor. Owner: Halvard Reisz. Ward certificate on the shared circuits expires in March. Use *top floor* for the scene room and *second floor* for Elias.
- **Rehabilitation account 44-9** — an account on Verge Row that is a legal person, with a card, a premises address, and a clause returning any tool issued against it at the close of the working day of issue. It books patients only at its own address. When it needs to reach somebody else it **merges records** rather than moving through a door, and the merge makes the document legal on its face.
- **The delivered act** — a residue with the timing and load profile of a release and **no settling curve**, meaning it was made somewhere else, carried on a solid object, and seated into a slot already the right shape. It is the physical signature of copywork. What it arrived on — tool, card, coin, token, bead — is not determinable at First Margin.
- **The maker's pair** — a maker's convention for grading a production run: an open square over a filled square, cut as a pair so a maker can number his own sets, first cut through fourth, and the cut is the grade. Volume 01 has exactly three pairs: the **first** cut on clamp JA-4417, the **second** on an uncollected set in Jules Ardent's bench room, the **third** and cleanest on the stockroom carrier.
- **A seated slot** — a drilled, filled and re-drilled frame or channel that is already the right shape to receive a release. Assembled once by a licensed working and thereafter capable of being filled again.
- **Kettle Rows Community Clinic** — Sana Okafor's clinic, a former paint works on Marshall Street with a yard and a dispensary stockroom. It is a stakeholder with a public board in its waiting room, not a location to be searched.
- **Bramwell & Sons**, Ash Street — the stamping firm that put maker's marks on licensed ward tools.
- **Saint Orra Reach residue laboratory** — Mara Quill's two rooms behind the hospital's linen office. Four instruments, two older than the department beside them. A salt-glass plate is not admissible unless it is on a Concord register, and hers is not.

## Terms and places added while drafting (Volume 01, batch 0002)

Canon recorded here as it was established in prose. No power rule changes.

- **SEQ 4(c)** — the **public shelf number** for the sequence-box category *an act of release entered after a turn where the working contains none of its own*. Found in the footer of **Form R-2 (rev. 4)**. The printed concordance adds: *field is completed on the standard form in every case in which a working has a turn and no release of its own; entry does not assert that a release was performed; entry asserts that the record is to be treated as though a release was performed, for the purposes of this record only.* **Nobody in the manuscript may know who fills a 4(c) box, why the box exists, or that a machine once filled one in on its own.** Volume 01 ends with that question open.
- **SEQ 4(d)** — *a sequence with no recorded release, closed at the discretion of the certifying office.* Withdrawn from standard forms in 2011, retained for indexing of pre-2011 records only. This is the sentence in the Morrow Street report.
- **The concordance of sequence-box codes** — a bound book, four credits, sold at the Morrow Hill forms counter because a form's code has to be in a printer's catalogue and is therefore not privileged. Its preface fixes that categories have not been renumbered since the 2006 consolidation, so a category number in an old form and the same number in a new one are the same category in the same words.
- **Form R-2 (rev. 4)** — a transfer-of-residence form whose printed instruction above the sequence box describes a **three**-act standard transfer and whose box has been filled in with **four**. A **certifying office** completes a 4(c) entry, and every issued certificate carries the name of the office that issued it.
- **The ward fault log** — a plastic box on a counter at the **Tanager Street works depot**, municipal, holding a slip for every fault reported in a ward-protected block. Slips are closed as *resolved* by an **attendance code**, and there are four codes and no names; the approved-contractor list is in an office. **A failure marked resolved on the day it was reported, by a contractor nobody can name, is the arc's cover mechanism.**
- **Ward continuity office** — a risk office, not an insurer in law, that prices off **reported faults** and publishes schedules of premises. There are eleven in Veyra. Schedules have a *covered* book and a **premises that are not covered** book, and rehabilitation account 44-9 is in the second. **The firms are named on the schedules and are not used in prose. Aegis Mutual is not named in Volume 01.**
- **Halloway Street** — four streets back from the Kettle Rows market. **Carrow Lane** — a print's, over which Orin Sable's registered premises sit. **Vine Street** — the out-of-hours magistrate's rooms. **Halloran Supply** — the trading name on the August invoices; a legal person, and the register of trading names is behind a counter.
- **A maker's acceptance pair** — the open square over a filled square, cut as a pair. A **die** cut from that profile is a stamping that can be sold to any maker, which is why a die shop's ledger is not public and why a seized die and an unregistered plate can only be compared in one room in the city.
- **A preliminary sequence attestation** — a Registry instrument that records a sequence against a standard and goes into the world whether or not the assigned detective signs it. **It is not a dangerous-working finding and it closes nothing.** Schedules attached to it are appealable.
- **A labelled disagreement** — the volume's real instrument: a document in which an officer records what he perceives, what he cannot say, and what he cannot rule out. Stronger evidence than an unlogged certainty, and the only thing Elias has that survives a hearing.
- **A note is not a request** — a distinction with teeth. A note has no form, no case number and no route; a request has all three, and a request for a connected record carries a disclosure field that cannot be left blank. Field eleven of the standard application for access to a certified record is headed *other matters to which the applicant is or has been connected, in which the record is or may be relevant*.

## Capitalization and style

- Capitalize **Veyra**, **Mara Quill**, **Civic Concord**, **Quiet Hands**, **Proof Tribunal**, **Quiet Grid**, **Veyran Vault**, **Proof Registry**, **Lantern Compact**, **Ninefold Assembly**, **Aegis Mutual**, **Sequence Sight**, **Witness Service**, **Witness Rooms**, and **Witness Compact**.
- Use lowercase for **working**, **sequence**, **anchor**, **address**, **turn**, **release**, **residue**, **inner trace**, **copywork**, **false rehearsal**, **discharge**, and **resolved** unless beginning a sentence or naming a formal certificate.
- Use “spell” in ordinary dialogue and “working” in professional or legal contexts. Do not make the distinction a class joke.
- Use “sequence reading” for Elias's skill and “certified sequence” for an official record. Never call a raw reading a certificate.
- Do not use “mana points,” “mana bars,” “skill trees,” “XP,” “cooldowns,” “level up,” “critical hit,” “quest,” or “NPC.” The world may use those as foreign modern gaming words only if a character has a specific reason to use them.
- Do not call the Margin a “system screen.” If a panel appears, it is a rare perception shaped by the faculty and the working.
- Do not invent a new formal act without defining its anchor, route, and consequence in the power bible or continuity state.
- Do not use invented Latin or pseudo-scientific notation. Technical terms should be plain enough for a witness, nurse, mechanic, or defense lawyer to understand.

## A note on the word “mana”

Characters may call the phenomenon “mana” because it is familiar, even when a specialist would use “current.” The word is never a universal language or a symbol for good and evil. It is the public name for something that can heal, harm, preserve, lie, and require repair.
