## ⚠️ THIS BATCH NAMES AEGIS MUTUAL

**This is the batch in which Aegis Mutual may be named, and it must be named here and not before.** Two ward continuity firms have been printed on public schedules since Chapter 16 and neither name has been used in prose, and **Aegis Mutual is the largest insurer in Veyra, a risk office whose published premiums price off reported faults, and a Lantern Compact member.** It is named now because **the workshop's first ledger names an insurance account, and that is the first documented link between a frame and an account** — which is exactly what escalation beat 9 requires.

Three rules for how it is named, and all three are load-bearing:

1. **It is a document, not a scene.** The name arrives in a ledger, an order book, a schedule or a purchase record, in a hand, on a date, in front of somebody who has to decide what to do about it. Nobody in a room says the name and a door opens.
2. **Naming an account is not naming a conspiracy.** Aegis Mutual is an institution that insures, prices, re-tenders and audits, and a great deal of what it does is defensible and documented. What is new is a **document in which its name sits next to a frame.** The batch must let the reader do the rest and must not let a character do it aloud.
3. **It does not make Aegis the network's author.** Aegis Mutual is a customer, a payer or a book-holder in this batch, not the hand that made anything, and not the reason a ward went dark. **Aegis's question — why it holds a certificate for a treatment no patient remembers receiving — is the volume close question in Chapter 50 and must not be answered here.** It may be *sharpened* here and not answered.

## The hardest open thread in the batch, and how to use it

**The drawing-office reference on CS-3, and the register behind a counter that needs a case number.**

That is the door into the whole arc, it is cheap, it is human, and it is the same institutional shape the volume has used twice — the die ledger behind Bramwell's counter in Chapter 16, and the register of trading names behind a counter in Chapter 18. **Do not let it become a wall.** The register exists, somebody keeps it, somebody will look at it for a man with a stamped copy of a drawing and a receipt stub, and the reason it takes four chapters is that a case number is required and he does not have one and cannot get one, because **he is not an officer any more.**

**Which means the batch's real engine is a man with no standing trying to do an officer's work, and the institutional question is: on what document does an officer open a register for a man who is not an officer?** Do not answer that abstractly. Answer it by having a competent person refuse once, correctly, and then a second, differently competent person find a lawful way round that costs somebody something.

**Two disciplines govern this thread and they are the same two that have governed every register in the volume:**

- **Nobody moves a third party's record, however small the gesture. Photograph in place. If two objects must appear in one frame, move the camera, not the objects.** A drawing office's register is a third party's record.
- **A drawing that cannot be found is not the same as a drawing that has been destroyed**, and a batch that blurs them will not be able to write Chapter 47.
