## What this arc is

Arc B — **"The Ward Under the Clinic," Chapters 11–20, batch 0002.**

- **Arc promise:** find who supplies the frames and how the templates travel.
- **Arc-level beat to land:** **Orin Sable is named, as a supplier of frames** — not as the man who built the stockroom carrier, not as arrested, and not as the final anything. And the network is shown using **real ward failures as cover**: the Quiet Grid is failing in small, uneven ways, and the crimes are being placed where the grid is already blinking, so nobody looks twice.
- **Escalation beats this batch owns**, with these chapter windows:
  - **Beat 5, the certification order (Chapters 11–14).** Arc A opened it. The Registry's own attestation goes out over Elias's refusal, the hearing on Monday 24 September arrives, and the certificate's text was written before the evidence was finished. This beat is a pressure, not a climax: it must be survived, not won.
  - **Beat 6, the copied pattern (Chapters 15–18).** Separate victims begin carrying the same fabricated release, and the network is shown supplying frames to operators who do not share its motives. The inference to draw is a trained network rather than one frightened criminal.
  - **Beat 7, the old case echo (Chapters 19–20).** The fourth act resembles the unknotted gap in the Morrow Street record. Elias sees it and **cannot join the two records**: any comparison requires copying or opening the old file, which he is not permitted to do, and which would finish him. The batch ends on the resemblance and the prohibition, not on a connection.

## Power rules for this arc

**First Margin, unchanged. No counterturn. No new stage.** Volume 01 ends where it starts.

- **Arc B contains no new reading.** The four readings of Arc A are spent, and **no two of them may take the same thing again**: four minutes and his balance; his hearing and three hours of the afternoon; food and the right to handle the clamp; and his legs, forty minutes, and the volume's only nosebleed. **A fifth contact is a new and separately paid cost, and only if a chapter earns it.** The volume outline is explicit that **what the later arcs take is not his body**: Arc A has already paid expensively, and **Arc B takes his access** — an insurance finding and a ward budget cut a district's protection on a conclusion he can see coming and cannot yet prove, and he watches it happen to people he has no standing to protect. Run Arc B on documents, witnesses, physical route, custody law, and Mara's instruments. That is the constraint; it will make the arc better, not worse.
- **Reading is contact or entry. It is not counting, and it is not a plate held against a door.** In Chapter 8 Elias read four printed acts on a transfer order standing back from a counter with his hands visible, and had to argue with himself that this was document reading. Mara may hold a plate against a surface and take a **measurement** from it — she did exactly that in Chapter 10, through a closed steel door, and logged it on the instrument. That is her facility, not his, and it is the difference the whole case turns on. Keep the two visibly separate.
- **The nosebleed is spent for Volume 01.** Do not use it again in this volume.
- **He still cannot tell a fold from a genuine turn, cannot separate a delivered act from a performed one, and cannot interrupt a working.** Chapter 9 established in front of the reader that he read the delivered release as a performed one and was corrected. Keep him under that ceiling and keep him honest about it.
- **Never read a living person.** The margin-panels are spent as well: at most one restrained panel in the whole batch, and only if a rule changes a decision before he makes it.

## Findings from the batch 0001 review that this batch must not regress on

- **Name Niko Senn in every chapter where he is present, intended, or discussed.** He is the address of the working, not its anchor, and naming him is what makes that legible.
- **An object that leaves must be shown leaving.** In Chapter 10 the operator's hands held a caliper and a phone and nothing else, which is the only reason the carrier, both halves and the stencil survive into the plate. A template that is wanted must be shown to be physically inseparable from what it is bonded to, and a crew that comes for a template must leave with a measurement instead.
- **A document has one location.** Batch 0001's worst custody defect was one transfer order standing in three places at once. If a second copy of anything matters, make it a different document, put it somewhere specific, and say who put it there and who has read it.
- **The left ear is a permanent condition, not a reading cost.** It is older than the case, and the batch made it worse rather than creating it: at 01:52 on Monday the ear went quiet for the first time and **came back inside an hour**, and from Monday afternoon it did not come back, so for the rest of the volume the whole left side of his head is gone rather than muffled. **Do not spend it again.** He still sits on people's right-hand side, still cannot locate a sound in a room without watching the mouth, and still has no tinnitus — that is reserved for Volume 10.
- **Sana Okafor is 47 and twenty-two of those years a doctor.** Use one number for both. Jules Ardent is 29. Tamsin Ro's room and Mrs Achterberg's are the top, fourth floor of 14 Kettle Rows; Otto Vahl is on the ground floor; Elias is on the second.
- **No thesis may be spoken aloud.** Let records and records do the arguing. Arc A's last chapter was rewritten in repair to end on a man writing something on the back of his own hand and then scrubbing it off, precisely because the previous version ended on a neat summary of what the reader had already been told. Do not close a chapter by telling the reader what they just read.
- **A list is not a scene.** Arc A's weakest openings are its two inventory passages: Chapter 3's three-column case board, and Chapter 7's paragraph that summarises a day of paperwork. Open on a person, an object, or a disagreement, and put the inventory in later, in a character's hands, where it can be wrong.
- **A man cannot know what he has no way of knowing.** Chapter 1 contained one line of impossible knowledge and it was cut. Watch for it — including inside a reading, which gives order and anchor, not the furniture of the room.
- **Say less about what is coming.** Bettina Haile was cut back from announcing the issued certificate. An antagonist who tells the reader the ending spends the ending.
- **Vary the closing move.** Too many Arc A endings land on a wry self-aware line from a speaker, and a chapter where a man makes the worst mistake of his life does not get to end on procedure either. Not every chapter may close on a neat line of somebody's, and none should close on a summary of the chapter it just finished. A decision, a cost, a discovery, a reversal, or a shift will all do.
