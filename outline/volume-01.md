# Volume 01 Outline — The Missing Fourth Act

**Volume range:** Chapters 1–50.
**Batch structure:** five batches of ten chapters; see the arc and batch map below. Escalation beats 10 and 11 are reserved for the volume climax in batch 0005 and may not be delivered earlier.
**Central pressure:** linked magical homicides copy their victims' last workings while the Sequence Desk is pressured to certify a safe, simple account of each case. The first network can carry a stolen release template, but it cannot yet insert a correction independently; the missing technique is the next volume's question.

## Starting state

Elias Venn is a 36-year-old detective sergeant in the Veyra Civic Police Sequence Desk. He carries a twelve-year-old complaint about the Morrow Street ward collapse, an inactive legal file for a disaster that killed twenty-three people before the false attribution narrowed it to one maintenance worker. He still believes Teodor Rusk was made to carry responsibility for a correction he could not see, and treats a privately kept sequence reading as more reliable than an official certificate. He is competent, isolated, and physically affected by the hearing loss in his left ear.

The Quiet Grid is stable enough for ordinary city life but has begun intermittent failures. The Civic Concord wants dangerous cases closed quickly. The Sequence Desk has authority to certify, not power to compel every witness to submit to a living trace.

**The building:** Tamsin Ro's rented room is two floors above Elias's own apartment, in the same Kettle Rows building whose ground floor is his repair shop. He is not attached to the case by choice — he is the nearest Sequence Desk sergeant and he lives on the site. The conflict of interest is permanent pressure rather than a single plot problem: the Concord inspector raises it, Nadia records it, and the building's owner has a licensed ward certificate that a dangerous-working finding would put at risk. Everyone around the case has a reason to want a boring answer.

## Starting relationships

- **Elias and Nadia Kest:** long-term investigative partners; Nadia trusts his work and distrusts his habit of acting alone.
- **Elias and Mara Quill:** guarded professional friction; Mara's residue instruments and consent boundaries keep him from treating the Margin as proof by itself.
- **Elias and the Civic Concord:** adversarial dependence; the Police need certificates, and the Concord controls the standards those certificates follow.
- **Kettle Rows residents and the clinic network:** wary of official readings but not uniformly anti-magic; they help or obstruct according to immediate safety.

## Starting power level

**First Margin:** Elias can perceive the order of a fresh magical working after physical contact or entry into a residue scene. He cannot read motive, identity, intent, or the future; he cannot safely read a living person; he cannot interrupt a working. A reading causes dizziness, nausea, and lost minutes. He has no public name for the faculty and no reliable way to prove it in court.

## Power change across the volume

The volume ends at the stage it starts with. **First Margin** becomes reliable enough to follow a working that is happening now, and never becomes a weapon Elias can swing.

- The readings grow longer and more precise: from a few seconds of order in a dead threshold to holding a live order steady long enough for another person to act on it.
- What improves is duration and comparison, not reach. He learns to recognize the same act recurring in separate objects. He still cannot separate a fold from a genuine turn, cannot tell copywork from an original, and cannot interrupt anything; the diagnosis of *copy* stays with Mara's instruments and the seam.
- He never learns a counterturn. The Fracture stage, the first attempt at interruption, belongs to Volume 02 and needs a partner who can warn him plus a physical anchor.
- The cost curve rises without becoming special. Dizziness and lost minutes in Arc A become balance loss, phantom pain, and a reading that damages the sample it was meant to protect. By Arc E he cannot stand unaided after a sustained reading and loses time he can only reconstruct from transit logs and other people's accounts.
- The volume's real gain is procedural, not magical: he learns that a labelled disagreement is stronger evidence than an unlogged certainty, and that a refusal can be a documented act rather than a personal one.

## Relationship change across the volume

- **Elias and Mara:** from guarded professional friction to a working consent practice that both of them keep informally. She sets the boundary in Arc A, he breaks it once and pays for it, and by the close of Arc A he is asking her to publish a measurement under her own name. The formal joint protocol remains a Volume 03–04 goal; nothing romantic is declared, and intimacy never grants magical access.
- **Elias and Nadia:** Nadia keeps him on the case on the condition that he stops working alone. Her trust survives Arc C only because he accepts a correction in public instead of defending a private reading.
- **Elias and Jules:** suspect to accountable technical ally. Jules never clears by innocence; he is charged with carrying other people's work and cleared of building the frame, and he remains a reluctant ally the network can still reach.
- **Elias and Sana:** she treats him as a risk to be managed, then as the officer who stayed outside her treatment rooms. The clinic is a stakeholder in the case, not a location to be searched.
- **Elias and the Civic Concord:** adversarial dependence hardens. The institution he resents gives him a reprimand in the same volume that it certifies a version of the case he knows is false.

## Major locations

- **Kettle Rows:** apartment rooms, the repair shop below Elias's home, community clinic, night market, and the first witness sites. The first crime scene is inside Elias's own building, two floors above his door.
- **Saint Orra Reach:** Mara's independent residue laboratory and the hospital's emergency intake.
- **The Cut:** service tunnels, abandoned ward channels, and the physical places where copied frames are hidden.
- **Morrow Hill:** Sequence Desk offices, Concord Hall, and the public pressure of certificates and jurisdiction.
- **A private rehabilitation clinic:** a legal-looking site that begins as a lead and later proves connected to memory work.

## Major factions

- **Veyra Civic Police / Sequence Desk:** Elias's employer; capable but dependent on certified evidence.
- **Civic Concord:** Licensing, Ward Works, Clinical Practice, and Proof Registry staff who want dangerous cases closed and the grid protected.
- **Kettle Rows Community Clinic:** Sana Okafor and the mutual-aid network providing care outside the official system.
- **Orin Sable's copywork network:** unlicensed clinicians and frame-makers who steal working traces and hide a safe-looking sequence inside crimes.
- **Lantern Compact interests:** insurers and landlords who benefit from a narrow definition of magical harm.

## Chapter distribution

The volume is a fifty-chapter procedural with a five-arc spine, so the mix tilts toward investigation and aftermath rather than combat. Approximate targets across the volume:

- 30% investigation and discovery, including interviews, scene work, and record tracing
- 15% action and survival, one concentrated sequence per arc and the Arc E working
- 15% character and relationship work, carried by consent, custody, and who is treated as a person
- 10% training and controlled experimentation, always in Mara's lab and always with a cost
- 15% political and social pressure: certificates, hearings, insurance, ward budgets, clinic access
- 15% recovery, aftermath, and practical repair: funerals, forms, custody hearings, curbs, and clinic tea

Two rules for the mix. No arc may resolve itself by a fight, and no arc may end without a recovery beat that shows who pays in sleep, money, medication, or standing.

## Escalation sequence

Each beat is assigned to an arc. Beats marked **volume** may not be delivered by any earlier batch.

1. **The first copied victim (Arc A, Chs 1–2):** a Kettle Rows resident dies in a room two floors above Elias's own, whose door and personal working appear to show a self-directed attack. Elias sees an order that does not match the room.
2. **The survivor and the frame (Arc A, Chs 3–4):** a surviving witness remembers a different order. Mara identifies a physical seam, while Jules Ardent is suspected because his ward tools are missing.
3. **The carrier, not the source (Arc A, Chs 5–6):** the tool taken from Jules's workshop turns out to be a carrier that can hold a stolen release template, never the source object itself. Elias contaminates the sample with an unlogged touch and loses the first useful measurement.
4. **The clinic route (Arc A, Chs 7–10):** the carrier's paper trail leads through a community clinic and a legal rehabilitation clinic. The source object itself is still unidentified, because nobody has reached the network yet. Sana's patients need protection before the Concord can secure the site, and the frame crew returns to the dispensary stockroom at night.
5. **The certification order (Arc A opens it, Chs 9–10; Arc B carries it, Chs 11–14):** the Proof Registry orders the Sequence Desk to certify a clean account before a public hearing. Elias refuses the first draft outright; the Registry escalates to a hearing he has to attend, and the certificate's text is written before the evidence is finished.
6. **The copied pattern (Arc B, Chs 15–18):** separate victims begin carrying the same fabricated release, and the network is shown supplying frames to operators who do not share its motives. The pattern suggests a trained network rather than one frightened criminal.
7. **The old case echo (Arc B, Chs 19–20):** the fourth act resembles the unknotted gap in the Morrow Street record, but Elias cannot yet connect it to Teodor Rusk without copying evidence.
8. **The rehearsed witness (Arc C, Chs 21–30):** a witness who has been helping Elias recants, then turns out to be telling a true, trained order. The same machinery that copied a dead woman's working has been rehearsing honest people, the protocol matches wording in the Concord's own witness-training material, and someone has used Elias's reading pattern to make a frame look like his work. He loses standing in his own case by publishing the disagreement.
9. **The frame workshop (Arc D, Chs 31–40):** the graded series Elias found at the clinic leads to a workshop in The Cut that is producing them, and Jules and Mara help him read it. The person who commissioned the work is not Orin himself but a protected intermediary, and the workshop's first ledger names an insurance account, which is the first documented link to Aegis Mutual.
10. **The crowded-clinic working (volume, Arc E, Chs 41–47):** Orin attempts to complete a copied sequence in a clinic full of patients. Elias can stop it only by breaking the physical carrier with help; he cannot yet interrupt a working directly.
11. **The omitted witness (volume, Arc E, Chs 47–50):** the official certificate closes the immediate case but deletes the one witness whose order would prove the copywork. Nadia can keep the correction cell from escaping, but the city receives a reassuring version, the workshop is dismantled, and a source anchor has already left Veyra.

## Midpoint reversal

**Placement: Chapter 25, in Arc C.** The opening batch may show that a witness was rehearsed; it must not show that the rehearsal can be built on a real danger, because that is the reversal that turns Elias's certainty into a liability.

At the midpoint, the witness Elias trusted is not simply lying, and in the plan the witness is Niko Senn, whose Chapter 4 rehearsal turns out to have been built on a real danger rather than invented out of nothing. He was trained through a false rehearsal while exposed to that danger, so his inner trace carries a convincing but altered order. Elias's rare ability can be fooled when a living person is made to remember a different sequence. The case changes from “who copied the spell?” to “who gets to define the legal sequence?” and his private certainty becomes a liability.

## Volume climax

**Placement: Chapters 41–50, Arc E, batch 0005.** Earlier batches build toward this; none of them stages it.

At a crowded Kettle Rows clinic, Orin Sable activates a copywork working through a frame connected to a patient's personal object. Elias follows the causal order, identifies the fabricated release as the fourth act, and uses ordinary police procedure, Mara's warning, and Jules's physical anchor to break the carrier before the patient's living trace can be used as a permanent source. He stops the working, but the attack leaves Mara shaken, destroys part of the workshop, and allows Orin to escape with a source anchor.

Nadia secures the clinic and arrests two local operators. The official certificate records a complete self-directed working and omits the witness whose order would expose the copy; the certificate's public wording is milder again, calling the event a contained accident involving an unregistered ward tool. The public line and the certificate say different things, and neither mentions the release that was actually performed. Elias is reprimanded for touching an unlogged sample, and the city announces that the dangerous case is closed. The public is safer; the record is cleaner than the truth.

## Concrete resolution

- Orin Sable's frame workshop in The Cut is dismantled and its stolen source objects are catalogued.
- The Kettle Rows clinic receives a temporary protection order, and the immediate victims receive care and a way to contest their official accounts.
- Two local copywork operators are charged; Orin is not caught, so the network remains active.
- Elias is reprimanded for the unlogged touch and loses unrestricted access to the samples from the first scene, but he keeps a private, documented copy of the physical discrepancy.
- Mara's consent and contamination draft becomes the informal protocol both of them work under; the formal joint protocol remains a Volume 03–04 goal.
- Jules is charged with carrying other people's work and cleared of building the frame, because the workshop's measurements are finer than the clamp he made; the network still knows where he works, so he remains a reluctant technical ally rather than a rescued man.
- The Sequence Desk publicly treats the case as solved while the original release concealed by the fabricated fourth act, and the witness who would contradict it, remain outside the certified record.

## New question for Volume 02

**Asked at the volume close (Chapter 50), not earlier.**

Who is teaching Orin Sable to move a working through a living patient, and why does Aegis Mutual possess a certificate for a treatment no patient remembers receiving?

## Final chapter image

**Placement: Chapter 50, the volume close.** No batch before batch 0005 may reproduce this image or promise it as a near outcome.

Elias stands outside the closed clinic with a salt-glass plate that contains two overlapping residue orders. The city has issued a single clean certificate. He wraps the plate in his red thread, but does not tie the knot; in the glass, the missing release continues to wait between the two accounts.
