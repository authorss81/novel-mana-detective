Arc A spent six of its ten chapters on investigation. Arc B therefore leans on **political, institutional, relationship and recovery pressure**, and runs on **documents, witnesses, physical route, custody law and Mara's instruments.**

- **No new reading in this batch.** The four readings of Arc A are spent and no two took the same thing. There is no fifth contact-based recognition anywhere in Chapters 11–20.

## Arc B's specific cost

**Arc B takes his access.** An unnamed insurer's district risk model re-rates the Kettle Rows ward block as *improving* — because faults are being answered and closed instead of fixed — the municipal levy follows, the ward maintenance budget is cut by forty per cent from Monday, and a building owner and a landlord's tenants in his own street pay for it. He can see it coming and cannot prove it, and he has no standing to protect any of them. **The ward fault log at the Tanager Street works depot is the mechanism and it is the arc's best idea:** the network does not suppress ward failures, it *answers* them. A fault is reported, a helpful contractor with a card comes to the door, and the fault is marked resolved without anybody ever looking at the wall. That is how the network knows where the grid is already blinking, and that is why nobody looks twice.
