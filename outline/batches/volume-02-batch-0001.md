### Chapter 0052 — Three Credits

**POV:** Elias Venn.
**Location/time:** Sunday 11 November 2029, the reference library on Corrie Street from 10:00 to 13:00, then the ground floor of Concord Hall at 14:20.
**Immediate goal:** Establish whether eleven credits is an offence, and if it is, whose offence it is.
**Resistance:** It is public, and it is three credits, and it is the most frightening document he has read since the certificate. **The Schedule of Registered Procedures and Fees** is a reference book that may not leave the room, and a woman at the desk says so kindly and once, to a man who has not asked to take it. The device of the chapter is the rack: **the Witness Training Compendium and the Schedule are sold three inches apart on the same wire rack by the same radiator**, and the man behind the table remembers selling him the first one in October and does not remember him and is not asked to.
**Information revealed:** **Schedule D — memory and identity work, fourteen procedures.** Number 2, *grounding and anchor*: two hours, **sixty credits**, and the form of words for it is the method — a published, safe, Module 4 procedure taught to thirty certified practitioners and printed for three credits. Number 9, *identity recollection support*. Number 12, *removal of a persistent association*, restricted, countersignature required, and the tariff note against it says the commonest ground of loss is **a person who cannot say afterwards what was asked for**. **Tariff note 4: no claim may be made under this schedule in respect of a procedure at number 2 to 14. A charge for such a procedure is not a claim and may not be settled by a third party.** So: the procedure is legal, the method is published, the price is published, and **the practice is an offence because it is undercharged and unregistered**, and neither half of that is anybody's villainy.
**Action or decision:** He buys the Schedule for three credits, does not buy the Compendium because he owns it, copies Schedule D's procedure 2 out of a public document in a public room, and cannot take the book off the shelf. He writes the arithmetic in his own notebook: **the practice is not a crime against the city, it is a crime against sixty credits an hour, and that is why nobody prosecutes it and why nobody can be frightened by it.**
**Emotional change:** He discovers that the most dangerous thing he has read this month is a price list, and that the reason nobody in a building full of lawyers is alarmed is that there is nothing in it that is against the law.
**Power or resource change:** No reading, no panel, no object examined. Gains: **the tariff, the prohibition on claims, and a method he could do himself, legally, for sixty credits** — which he cannot afford and will not say so in the shop.
**Continuity fact to preserve:** the wire rack by a radiator on the ground floor of Concord Hall is established and is not moved. **Module 7 and the office's refusal to supply the order are not read in this chapter.** Nobody learns who completes a 4(c) box. The book's presence on a public shelf is public in the ordinary sense and is not privileged.
**Ending type:** discovery through a public document, in a room with other people in it.
**Next-chapter pull:** The woman comes on Tuesdays at four, and he has to go to a first-floor room in Corrie Street as a person who has been told what she is, and to do it on a Monday, which means knocking on a working afternoon.
