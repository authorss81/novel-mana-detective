# Volume 03 Outline — The Witness Who Recanted

**Volume range:** Chapters 101–150.
**Volume window as executed:** **Wednesday 20 February 2030 to Friday 17 May 2030.** Volume 01 ran 17 September to 3 November 2029; Volume 02 ran 10 November 2029 to 23 January 2030. Weekdays are checked against the real Gregorian calendar in the frame the whole manuscript runs in: **17 September 2029 = Monday, 3 November 2029 = Saturday, 23 January 2030 = Wednesday, 20 February 2030 = Wednesday.** Arc windows: **A 20 Feb – 6 Mar 2030 · B 11–22 Mar · C 25 Mar – 11 Apr · D 15–26 Apr · E 29 Apr – 17 May.**
**Batch structure:** five batches of ten chapters, one arc each. Escalation beats 18–25 are reserved for the arcs named against them and may not be delivered earlier.
**Central pressure:** a dangerous-working case is closed with a perfectly certified account, and the only person in the city who can be shown to have got that account wrong is a night porter who signed a statement in December 2029 and withdrew it in writing on 11 February 2030. **The volume's engine is not a conspiracy and not a villain. It is a course.** A witness-training course, three credits at a counter, taught in a room with other people in it, and a sentence printed in it about what a registered witness undertakes to do with an account they have already given.

**Reconciliation with the Chapter 100 close, and the conversion, recorded here and nowhere else.** Volume 02 closed on a question asked once in an office and answered by nobody: *who is allowed to sit in a room where a claim is turned into a certificate,* and *the claim was signed by somebody and the certificate was issued by an office and the treatment was received by nobody who remembers it — so which of those three people is the one who is wrong.* The non-answer was the finding: **the department is not a person, and the only person who could ever be shown to have got it wrong is a person, and there is not one on the sheet.**

**Volume 03 spends fifty chapters finding that person, and the person is Ruben Sallis, 54, a night porter, and the city already knows what to do with him. It gives him a form.** The withdrawal form is in a drawer in a station front office and it is free to anybody and it takes four minutes to fill in and the box on it that says what happens to your account next is **pre-ticked with the word SUPERSEDED**, and the pre-tick is not a trick, it is a form design, and nobody has ever seen it fail to save anybody. So the Chapter 100 question is **converted, not repeated**: the person exists, and the answer to *which of them is wrong* is that one of them can withdraw, and the withdrawal does nothing to the certificate, and the volume's question is about what that does to a man.

**The question for Volume 04, asked once, at Chapter 150, and nowhere earlier:**

> *The Chapter 100 close asked which of three people was wrong and was told that none of them could be, because a department is not a person. **This volume found the person. What is a witness's account worth on the morning after the day he has been asked to sign it twice — and who in this city is authorised to say so?***

**It cannot be asked again in the same words anywhere in this volume, and no chapter may restate the Chapter 100 form of it.**

## Starting power level

**Fracture, and the volume ends with the first working of Threaded Evidence and no stage name on the page.** He can read a fresh completed sequence after contact, hold a working that is happening now, name a false start out loud and be checked, and attempt a counterturn with an understood sequence, an anchor he made himself, and other people holding the room. He cannot read a living person and never will, and this volume puts the central case **inside that inability on purpose**: the one act he needs is the one act that happened in a man.

**Volume 03's power change, as recorded in `bible/power-system.md` by this phase:** the first use of **connected secondary traces** — reading a residue out of a record that has been in contact with a working, and comparing it against another reading of the same working. **The stage name is not used in the prose in this volume.** He gains it the way every gain in this manuscript has to happen: at a bench, with a second object, with a disagreement, and at a cost that is somebody else's feeling.

**THE DISCOVERY, and it is the volume's technical heart, and it is a different mechanism from Volume 01's:** a dangerous-working certificate can be manufactured from a **genuine** sequence. Not by fabricating an act. By **selection and reordering**: take a real four-act working, certify three of the four as somebody's own self-directed work, and **omit the fourth.** The omitted act is the one that would have shown a second person in the room. **The Volume 01 fourth act is a fabricated release inserted into a record. This volume's is a real act left out of one. Both are the same city and they are not the same crime, and no chapter may conflate them, and no chapter may call the omitted act a fourth act.**

**The cost of the stage, paid in Arc A:** contamination and emotional bleed. A secondary trace carries what was in contact with it, including a person's feeling, and the feeling does not belong to him. It is not a memory of his own and it is not a vision of somebody else's life. It is somebody's, and it is in him, and it goes off, and he is left hollow and cannot say what it was.

## Relationship change across the volume

- **Elias and Mara:** from a working practice neither of them has named to **a written instrument with a name on it.** The five informal lines become the first draft of a **consent and contamination protocol** — the formal joint document is a Volume 03–04 goal and **this volume drafts and signs it but does not finish it**; the finished version, with the tribunal's own objections answered, is Volume 04. **They become collaborators before they become lovers and they are not lovers at the end of this volume.** The volume's cost on this relationship is a breach: he conceals a witness's whereabouts from her, and she is the person who taught him that a person's consent cannot be produced by somebody who was not in the room. He offers her the evidence and lets her decide whether it is published. **The breach is repaired by a change in the plan and not by an apology.**
- **Elias and Nadia:** the dispute she lodged in her own name is still live and unexercised, and in this volume she is the officer who reads a letter out of a tray and who cannot reopen a closed case without a Deputy Chief, and the Deputy Chief is the volume's institutional face. **He lies to her once, in Chapter 110, and the lie is four words long and he does it to a woman who has twice chosen not to ask him anything.** She does not ask. **That is the whole of the cost and it is the volume's cheapest and best scene.**
- **Elias and Sana Okafor:** she put her name under a date range with an end in it. In this volume he comes back to her door three times and twice has nothing to bring her, and the second time she says the sentence that costs them something: *you have come to my door twice in three weeks with nothing, and a man who comes to a door with nothing is a man who wants something, and I would rather you wanted something out loud.*
- **Elias and Bettina Haile:** adversarial dependence, hardened into something colder. The Registry can produce a **record of issue** for a certificate whose subject is dead, a person it has never met, or a ward it has never entered, and cannot produce the certificate, and she is entirely correct about both halves. **She is not a villain. She is the fourth wall of a building.**
- **Elias and Deputy Chief Aveline Orr:** they meet once, properly, in a room, in Arc C, and both of them are doing their jobs. **She is the volume's first genuinely formidable person and she is not a liar.** Her position: a closed case, a withdrawn statement, a certificate, and an officer who kept private notes — and the department's exposure if it reopens. Her counter-argument is better than his and he cannot answer it in the room.

## Escalation sequence

Beats marked **volume** may not be delivered by any earlier batch.

18. **The order ends and a statement is withdrawn (Arc A, Ch 101–102).** The first dated event of the volume is a document that stops being true by lapse. Then a three-sentence letter arrives at a police station nine days late, in the wrong tray, from a night porter who says he did not do what his statement says he did. **A witness can withdraw and a certificate cannot be withdrawn, and they are two documents in two buildings and the law has never once had to say which of them is more important.**
19. **The consequence box is pre-ticked (Arc A, Ch 103–104).** A withdrawal form in a drawer in a station front office, free to anybody, four minutes, and the box that says what happens to your account next is **pre-ticked SUPERSEDED**, and the woman behind the counter does not tick it. **And in Concord Hall, a public register with a course reference in it says the man is ACTIVE, and the register was last updated after the letter arrived.**
20. **A certificate made out of a true thing (Arc A, Ch 105–106 and 109; volume).** A bed schedule that pays for a bed and not for a person, and a review date on a wall. **A night book in a refuge, read in place: a genuine four-act working, and a second reading off a door, and a comparison, and the finding that the certified sequence is three of the four in a different order and the fourth is simply not in it.** Cost: emotional bleed, somebody else's grief, about four minutes, and a man in a chip shop the next day who is not sympathetic.
21. **The four offices and one course (Arc B, Ch 111–120; volume).** The claims floor, the Proof Registry, a ward, a refuge and a precinct are not five conspiracies. They are five buildings that have all sent somebody to a two-day course.
22. **The day room at 2 Wexford Row (Arc B, Ch 111–120; volume).** The operator that has a ward. **Doris Sowerby, 81, and the other two are told what was done to them on 22 January 2030**, and it is told by a doctor and not by a police officer, and it takes two hours, and one of the three asks a question nobody can answer.
23. **The safe house and the text message (Arc C, Ch 121–130; volume).** A transfer by text message on 12 December 2029, a file into a store, a sergeant's own notes in a hardback notebook in a drawer, and a woman at 18 Pellow Street with a book of first names.
24. **The continuity amendment (Arc D, Ch 131–140; volume, the midpoint).** **The coercion behind the recanted witness is ordinary procedure.** A registered witness undertakes, in printed words, in a module that costs three credits, to bring an account into continuity with the certified record. The police have been producing usable witnesses by the book, because the book says the witness agrees to do it himself.
25. **The leak and the crowd (Arc E, Ch 141–150; volume climax).** The safe-house record is ordered destroyed. The raw sequence goes to a public room. A crowd forms and a man of about seventy-one who came to ask about his own night is hurt. The precinct-level cell is exposed in a document and not a fight. **Elias is suspended, and the thing he has built survives him.**

## Midpoint reversal

**Placement: Chapters 131–140, Arc D.** The opening arcs may show that a witness was trained, that a course exists, and that a certificate rests on his signature. They must not show what the course says he undertakes to do, because the reversal is that **nobody hid anything from anybody.**

Volume 01's reversal was one honest man rehearsed on a real danger. **This one is an institution following its own form.** The same sentence that trains a witness to hold a sequence is the sentence used to correct one afterwards; the withdrawal is requested in the room by a cooperative man who is shown an extract of the record and asked whether he agrees with it; he says no once and then agrees; the agreement is minuted as a **continuity amendment** and it is not a coercion on the page. **Mara Quill has to hear it as a practitioner rather than as a sister, and the sentence she has to accept is that a document can be true and still be the last thing that happens to a person.**

## Volume climax

**Placement: Chapters 141–150, Arc E, batch 0015.**

A Deputy Chief orders the destruction of a private record held in a house in a terrace, on the grounds that it is a record of people whose cases are closed. The team has about three days. Elias has a raw sequence — uncertified, four acts, in the right order, with a second person in the room — and a lawful reason to hold it and no lawful way to publish it. **He puts it where a person can read it, and the person he puts it in front of is a woman who has been waiting four months for a hearing.** The crowd forms outside the Corrie Street rooms at two o'clock on an afternoon in May, and a man of about seventy-one who came to find out whether the working that put him in a hostel was his own is hurt, and he is hurt for real, and he is in a bed for eleven weeks. **The precinct-level cell is exposed by a document and a rota and not by a fight.** Nadia Kest opens a complaint and evidence unit inside the police on the day she is told she cannot. And Elias is suspended, and the protocol is signed, and it is lodged with the clinic and not with the police.

## Concrete resolution

- **The precinct-level cell is dismantled** — a supervisor, two officers, and a records clerk who moved a file by text message — and the cell is not a conspiracy about magic. It is three people who did not want a reopened case.
- **Elias Venn is suspended** and loses the only thing he had left: the right to ask a general question at a counter, and with it the only way this volume's work can go on. He does not lose his hearing, which is already gone in one ear, and does not gain tinnitus, which is Volume 10.
- **A real injury is caused by the leak**, and it is not repaired in this volume, and the person it happens to is not a plot device and is not thanked.
- **The continuity amendment is not abolished**; it is **published with its own form of words printed beside it**, and a doctor signs the first witness who has asked to read it. That is a smaller thing than a law and it is the thing that survives.
- **The Anselm Street Refuge closes on 31 March 2030** and its forty-one beds are emptied over nine days, and the volume counts what can be counted and says plainly that it does not know how many of the beds were full, **because no chapter in this volume counts them and a state file may not supply the figure.**
- **Nadia Kest keeps a reform investigation alive inside the police**, in her own name, with no claim in it, as she did in November, and this time it has a witness who is alive to read it.
- **Iven Quill is not found and is not declared dead**, and the revelation lock holds.

## New question for Volume 04

**Asked at the volume close (Chapter 150), not earlier.**

The series outline gives Volume 04's question as *who profits when a city can make a dangerous working disappear from a compensation claim,* and Volume 03's own close has to hand the tribunal the thing it needs: **a certificate that is not a lie, a sequence that is not fabricated, a witness who is alive, an account that was superseded, and a form of words with three credits on the cover.** The question the volume asks is about a person's account. **The question Volume 04 asks is about money, and it may only be asked once, at Chapter 150, and it is a different question from the Chapter 100 one because the answer to it can be money.**

## Final chapter image

**Placement: Chapter 150, the volume close.** No earlier batch may reproduce it.
