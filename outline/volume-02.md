# Volume 02 Outline — The Clinic of Small Repairs

**Volume range:** Chapters 51–100.
**Batch structure:** five batches of ten chapters, one arc each. Escalation beats 12–15 are reserved for Arc D and Arc E and may not be delivered earlier.
**Central pressure:** unlicensed memory clinics and stolen-memory recoveries turn care into a black market. The work is cheap, effective, legal in method and illegal in price, and it is being done in front rooms and above shops in the same districts the Kettle Rows clinic serves. The volume's engine is not a monster. **It is a product: a four-line card, a printed sheet, a counterfoil, and a price list that somebody is losing money on.**

## Starting state

Inherited from the Volume 01 close without re-breaking anything. The manuscript runs **Monday 17 September to Saturday 3 November 2029**; this volume runs **Saturday 10 November 2029 onward**.

Elias Venn is a member of the public. He has a departmental censure (23 October) and a formal reprimand (3 November 09:30) with a right of appeal to a panel in about five weeks. He has lost unrestricted access to the first-scene samples, his twenty-year trade account, his case, and his standing in a room with an officer in it. He keeps a private documented copy of the only description of that working in existence, and there is nowhere to file it. He has a notebook, a repair shop, a plate that was lent to him for twenty-eight minutes on 3 November and came straight back, and two questions that were asked out loud on a pavement on 3 November and answered by nobody.

**Mara Quill** holds three plates in her name, a certified figure that is now the depth of a hole in a man's walking stick, and a laboratory that is behind a laundry and is on no register. **Log 1202 was opened and closed on 3 November: plate 1201 was signed back in at 16:38, and the due date of Thursday 14 November was never reached.** The five lines are signed, informal, lodged nowhere, and broken once already at line three.

**The case for the public is closed.** The city has a certificate that says a man of seventy-eight performed a complete self-directed working, a door notice that says a contained accident involving an unregistered ward tool, and a supplement carrying one word. Orin Sable is a trading name on two documents and a man in none of them. The walking stick is gone. The eleven objects in a crate store on Fenwick Street are nobody's yet.

## Starting power level

**First Margin**, unchanged at the volume's opening. He can read a fresh completed sequence by contact, hold a working that is happening now for as long as he can stand, and name what he cannot state. He cannot interrupt a working, cannot read a living person, cannot tell a fold from a genuine turn, and cannot separate a delivered act from a performed one.

## Power change across the volume

**Fracture.** He learns to tell a **false start** from a **turn**, and attempts a **counterturn** — an interruption inserted into a working — with a partner who can warn him and a physical anchor in his hand. It is attempted badly before it is attempted well.

- The gain is diagnostic before it is active. Arc A gives him a false start he cannot name, and a woman with four instruments who tells him that neither of them can tell a false start from a turn; Arc B makes the distinction usable on paper; Arc C makes him wrong with it in public; Arc D is the first attempt, and it fails, and it costs him a phantom pain and a body that repeats an act; Arc E is the first one performed with a team, in a room, on a person, and it works.
- The cost curve changes what it takes. **A false memory of a conversation that has not happened yet** is the price of the Arc A reading: he remembers agreeing to something in a car park with a police inspector, writes the date, and cannot find the conversation. From then on he cannot trust his own recollection of a *decision*, and the volume's theme is that nobody in it can either.
- He still cannot read a living person and never does. A wrong interruption makes his body repeat the next act, and this is written as a failure with consequences, not as a shortcut.

## Relationship change across the volume

- **Elias and Mara:** from an informal sheet about objects to an informal practice about **people**, and the practice is not written down. She says the five lines were drafted for a thing on a bench and that a person is not a bench. **One counterturn in the volume is possible only because she is in the room and says a word at the right moment**, and the whole argument of the arc in which it happens is about whether he is allowed to be the one who acts. Romantic interest is present and is not declared, in gesture, in a coat, in a plate, and in one conversation neither of them finishes. The formal joint consent and contamination protocol remains a Volume 03–04 goal and is not written here.
- **Elias and Nadia:** her condition that he does not work alone is live in a file he no longer holds, so she cannot impose it, and they find a worse arrangement in which she imposes it anyway on a document of her own. She gets him no access he has not got and costs herself something to get him the access he is entitled to.
- **Elias and Sana:** stakeholder to ally, and it costs her board space. She makes a condition of continuing help and he accepts it on her terms, having learned in Volume 01 what happens when her name is under his.
- **Elias and Niko Senn:** the man who would rather be a name in a document than a route sets a limit in Arc A that Elias obeys, and obeying it costs them a man they could have had. Niko reads an order without being read, and refuses to say what he can feel.
- **Elias and Jules Ardent:** technical ally, and Jules reads paper the way he reads metal — by hand, in the hand, with no instrument and no standing.

## Major locations

- **Kettle Rows:** Tanners Row, 14 Kettle Rows, the repair shop, the Kettle Rows Community Clinic on Marshall Street, **Corrie Street** — a short street of first-floor rooms above shops, holding a dry cleaner, a tool registry and one unregistered practice.
- **Saint Orra Reach:** Mara's two rooms behind the hospital's linen office, and the hospital's public corridors.
- **The Verge Row side:** rehabilitation accounts that are legal persons, and the offices that bill them.
- **A debt clinic:** a room above a shop where people go when the money has gone, staffed by people who are not all qualified and paid in cash.
- **A private insurer's rehabilitation ward:** licensed, clean, warm, and paid for by a company that prices off what is claimed rather than what is done.
- **The Cut and the outer wards:** where the practice goes when a client cannot pay in a room with a door.

## Major factions

- **Kettle Rows Community Clinic and the mutual-aid network:** the care network that connects the volume's cases and the reason the volume is not a police story.
- **Unregistered memory practitioners:** people doing a published method, well, for less than the published price, with no register, no supervisor, and no way to be wrong safely.
- **Rehabilitation accounts and claims offices:** legal persons that book patients at their own premises and bill somebody else for what was done.
- **Aegis Mutual:** named in Volume 01 in one burnt ledger line and a question asked on a pavement. **This volume does not answer why it holds a certificate for a treatment no patient remembers receiving. It converts the question from a suspicion into a set of documents.**
- **Civic Concord, Clinical Practice:** publishes the schedule of procedures and fees that makes the volume's central legal fact ordinary — a restricted procedure, priced, three credits, on a wire rack.
- **Lantern Compact interests:** insurers who need a claim to have a treatment behind it, and clinics who need a claim to have a patient behind it.

## Chapter distribution

The mix tilts away from Volume 01's crime-led balance. This is a volume about a market.

- 25–30% investigation and discovery — thirteen to fifteen chapters of the fifty
- 10% action and survival, in three concentrated sequences: the debt clinic, the rehabilitation ward, and the Arc E raid
- 20% character and relationship work, carried by consent, price, and who is treated as a customer rather than a patient
- 15% training and controlled experimentation, always in Mara's laboratory and always with a cost
- 15% political and social pressure: tariffs, claims, registers, protection orders, hearings
- 15% recovery, aftermath and practical repair: ledgers, waiting rooms, front rooms, and clinic tea

Two rules for the mix. **No arc may resolve itself by a fight**, and no arc may end without a recovery beat that shows who pays in fees, standing, sleep, or a person being named.

## Escalation sequence

Beats marked **volume** may not be delivered by any earlier batch.

11. **The second half of the Volume 01 question opens in an ordinary lane (Arc A, Ch 51–52).** The teaching is not a man. It is a **four-line card with a tool reference on it**, and a price list, and a sheet of paper, and the people who use them are people who cannot afford a sixty-credit hour and are being charged eleven.
12. **The published method and the cheap practice (Arc A, Ch 52–54).** The Concord's **Schedule of Registered Procedures and Fees, Schedule D (memory and identity work)** makes the whole trade legible: the procedure is published, the price is published, and the practice is illegal only because it is **undercharged and unregistered**. The first relationship of the volume is between an unregistered practitioner and a frightened client, and both of them are right.
13. **The paper, and the third box (Arc A, Ch 60; Arc B carries it).** A single side of A4, headed with a module and a page, setting out three boxes where the printed manual has two. **The third box is a release.** A release in a working is not a fourth act; a fourth act is a release that is not the working's own. Who printed the sheet is not answered in this volume.
14. **A certificate for a treatment nobody remembers (Arc B, Ch 61–70; volume).** The claim money, the service code, the registered premises and the certificate are put side by side, and the four offices that hold them cannot see each other. **A certificate exists for a treatment its subject does not remember receiving, and every document around it is genuine.**
15. **The living patient and the network (Arc C, Ch 71–80; volume).** The people who do the work are inside the mutual-aid structures, and Elias's own four sheets are still on the clinic board, and the volume asks whether the arrangements that keep people alive are the arrangements that keep them undocumented.
16. **Consent (Arc D, Ch 81–90; volume, the midpoint).** Iven Quill signed something. The reversal is not that somebody lied to him; it is that he was told the truth about a small procedure and not told it was a source, and the documents are clean.
17. **The raid and the unfinished arrest (Arc E, Ch 91–100; volume climax).** A live memory working in a room of patients, a counterturn with a team, a frame destroyed, an escape with the records, and Iven Quill not found — and the payments that follow the money out of the ward.

## Midpoint reversal

**Placement: Chapters 81–90, Arc D.** The opening arcs may show that Iven went into a clinic; they must not show what he signed, because the reversal is that a man who has been missing for six years did not have anything taken from him that he was not asked for.

Iven Quill's trail has run for four arcs as a list of absences: a booking, an account, a claim, a premises. At the midpoint it turns out that the consent is genuine, the handwriting is his, the procedure described to him was a small and named one, and the thing it was actually used for — a source anchor for a working that ran through other people's memories — was not in the document. **The reversal is not a lie. It is a true signature on an incomplete sentence, which is the same trick as the certificate and one level down, and Mara Quill has to hear it as a practitioner rather than as a sister.**

## Volume climax

**Placement: Chapters 91–100, Arc E, batch 0010.**

A debt clinic and a rehabilitation ward are worked at the same time on the same night, with a memory working running in a room of eleven patients. Elias follows the order, names the false start, and — with Mara's warning in his ear and a physical anchor in his hand — performs the **first counterturn of the series**: he inserts an uncommitted act so that a working cannot complete its release, holds the gap for as long as four people can hold a room, and takes the unresolved consequence. Several patients are saved and come out of the working with a name they can still say. **Iven Quill is not found.** The clinic's most dangerous frame is destroyed in the room, which means the record of who it served goes with it. Nadia secures the premises and makes arrests on a list that is short. And when the payments are followed back, they lead to an insurer's rehabilitation claims department, and the volume ends on a document that says a treatment was received.

## Concrete resolution

- The clinic network loses its most dangerous frame, and with it the paper that said how to make one.
- Kettle Rows clinics receive a temporary protection order, which is a real document with a real expiry date.
- Patients are not all cured. Two people in the volume are left with a memory they cannot get back and are told so.
- Rhoda Nesbit is charged with unregistered practice and is not charged with anything else; her three counterfoils are the reason the claims are opened at all.
- Iven Quill is not found and is not declared dead.
- Elias performs a counterturn with a team and can hold an uncommitted act for about as long as a room can be held.
- Selene Rook's payments identify the next layer, and the volume closes on a document rather than on a person.

## New question for Volume 03

**Asked at the volume close (Chapter 100), not earlier.**

The series outline gives Volume 03's question as *why does Aegis Mutual possess a sequence certificate for a treatment that no patient remembers receiving*, and Volume 01 already put that question on a pavement. **This is deliberate and reconciled here rather than duplicated:** the Volume 01 form is a suspicion with no document behind it; the Volume 02 close restates it as an *institutional* question with four offices, a claimant, an issuing office, a schedule number and a form of words in print. The volume does not answer the suspicion and does not answer the institutional form. It converts one into the other, which is the only honest way to carry a question across two volumes without spending it.

**The Chapter 100 form:** *who is allowed to sit in a room where a claim is turned into a certificate,* and: *the claim was signed by somebody and the certificate was issued by an office and the treatment was received by nobody who remembers it — so which of those three people is the one who is wrong.*

## Final chapter image

**Placement: Chapter 100, the volume close.** No earlier batch may reproduce it.

A waiting room in Kettle Rows with a temporary protection order on the door in a date range that has an end date on it, and a woman at the counter writing down, in a book of her own, the price she charged and the name she asked for and the fact that the third box is on a wall behind polythene where anybody may read it. Two people sit in the room who were in a working on Tuesday and who have a name each that they can still say, and one of them is going to have to be told which parts of the week are not coming back.
